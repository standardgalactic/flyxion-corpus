RSVP inverts this dynamic, treating attention not as a commodity but as a flow within an entropy-respecting semantic plenum.

\frac{\partial \mathbf{v}}{\partial t} + (\mathbf{v} \cdot \nabla) \mathbf{v} &= -\nabla \Phi, \label{eq:vector} \\
\frac{\partial S}{\partial t} + \text{div} (\mathbf{v} S) &= \lambda \Delta S. \label{eq:entropy}

\[
\delta \mathcal{S} = \int_{\Omega} \left( \nabla \Phi \cdot \nabla \delta \Phi - \mu \text{div} \mathbf{v} \delta \Phi \right) dV = 0.

Integrating by parts and applying the divergence theorem, the boundary term vanishes, yielding:
\[
\int_{\Omega} \left( -\Delta \Phi + \mu \text{div} \mathbf{v} \right) \delta \Phi \, dV = 0,

\section{Chapter 8: RSVP Futarchy --- Governance as Field Feedback}

\subsection{8.1 Colonial and Patriarchal Roots}

The household-as-enterprise can be traced at least as far back as Pliny the Elder’s \textit{De Agricultura}, which codified the idea that families should be managed like estates—self-sufficient units whose success or failure reflected the skill of a patriarch. Under colonialism, this logic was exported globally: communal lands were enclosed, local economies were coerced into cash-crop production, and survival was made contingent on the entrepreneurial competence of the household head \citep{ortega1957}. Patriarchal legal codes reinforced this: the breadwinner, usually male, was expected to act as the sole guarantor of a family’s survival. These structures set the template for today’s neoliberal world, where everyone is expected to “run their own enterprise” merely to stay afloat \citep{zuboff2019].

\subsection{8.2 The RSVP Interpretation}

In RSVP terms, this system is pathological. Each household is forced into a sharp scalar spike \(\Phi\), with steep gradients that destabilize the surrounding field. Entropy \(S\) increases globally, as the system cannot maintain coherence when every node is overloaded \citep{shannon1948}. Communities fragment into fragile units, each compelled to perform as if they were a singular genius. This is entropically unstable: gradients this steep cannot be sustained without collapse or external input \citep{jaynes2003]. The field equation:
\[
\frac{\partial \Phi}{\partial t} + \nabla \cdot (\mathbf{v} \Phi) = D \nabla^2 \Phi + f(\mathbf{x}, t),

reveals that high \(\nabla \Phi\) induces unstable flows, increasing \(S\) and undermining collective coherence \citep{gromov1999]. This aligns with critiques of neoliberalism, which fragments social cohesion \citep{zuboff2019].

\subsection{8.3 Toward a Futarchy of Fields}

Robin Hanson’s futarchy proposed separating values (what we want to maximize) from beliefs (which policies will achieve them) \citep{hanson2003]. Citizens vote on values, while prediction markets decide policies. However, wealth-dominated markets risk plutocracy. RSVP-based futarchy retools this as field feedback governance, employing distributed deliberation networks—rotating citizen assemblies, local expertise pools, and community panels—to forecast policy impacts \citep{ortega1957]. Policies are chosen by their predicted effect on the field: smoothing gradients, reducing entropy, and reinforcing coherence.

\subsection{8.4 Formal Principle}

In RSVP futarchy, a policy \(\mathcal{P}\) is evaluated by:

maximizing expected collective potential while minimizing variance to prevent singular attractors \citep{hanson2003]. The variance term \(\mathrm{Var}(\Delta \Phi_{\mathcal{P}})\) ensures equitable distribution, aligning with entropic ethics (Chapter 6) \citep{whitehead1978]. For example, a policy reducing income inequality would increase \(\langle \Delta \Phi_{\mathcal{P}} \rangle\) by diffusing potential across communities, while minimizing \(\mathrm{Var}(\Delta \Phi_{\mathcal{P}})\) to prevent elite capture \citep{lanier2018].

RSVP futarchy is not just governance; it is a physics of political life. Geozotic families are an alternative attractor state. Rather than isolated spikes, they represent distributed wells in the scalar potential.
