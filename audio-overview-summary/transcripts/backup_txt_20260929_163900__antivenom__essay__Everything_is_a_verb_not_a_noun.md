2. **Counterfactual Sovereignty:**
   - Counterfactual sovereignty refers to the power institutions wield in selecting which alternative scenarios are permissible for evaluation.
   - By systematically excluding certain alternatives (e.g., those that challenge dominant narratives), evaluative frameworks can be rigged, leading to a skewed perception of success and merit.

3. **Scarcity as an Evaluative Construction:**
   - In conventional economics, scarcity is treated as a fixed physical limitation.
   - Flyxion argues that scarcity is often relative to specific allocation rules and institutional arrangements; thus, “there isn’t enough” can be reinterpreted as “not enough if we refuse to change how resources are distributed.”

4. **Spressatura:**
   - An Italian Renaissance term meaning the art of making a difficult performance appear effortless.
   - In Flyxion’s framework, this concept highlights the intentional concealment of the labor and effort behind visible success, akin to an Olympic gymnast hiding their injuries while appearing flawless.

5. **Meritocracy as a Mathematical Error:**
   - The attribution of success solely to innate talent or hard work is critiqued by Flyxion as a noun fallacy.
   - Success is viewed not merely as a personal attribute but as the result of an entire trajectory maintained by inherited advantages (e.g., generational wealth, professional networks).
   - This leads to the concept of the “talent reserve,” representing human potential that remains unrealized due to opaque developmental infrastructures.

6. **Developmental Opacity and Noun Fallacy:**
   - Developmental opacity refers to the invisibility of historical trajectories (e.g., inherited operator ecology) in evaluating success.
   - The noun fallacy is the mistake of treating success as a static attribute rather than an ongoing, constrained trajectory shaped by external factors.
