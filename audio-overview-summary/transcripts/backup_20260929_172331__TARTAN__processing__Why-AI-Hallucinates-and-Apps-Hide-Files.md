1. **Gauge Freedom in Physics**
   - Gauge freedom allows multiple, seemingly distinct configurations to encode identical physical phenomena.
   - A practical everyday example is measuring elevation: you could set sea level as zero or the center of Earth; both yield different numbers but describe the same mountain height.

2. **AI and Gauge Mismatch**
   - AI systems often operate on tokenized representations (text, embeddings). If they lack awareness of gauge freedom, they may interpret differing numerical formats (e.g., Celsius vs. Fahrenheit) as contradictions.
   - This leads to inflated complexity in their databases because the system treats equivalent information as divergent facts.

3. **Solution: Gauge Fixing**
   - By selecting a canonical representation (gauge fixing), AI can avoid misinterpreting format differences as factual disagreements, thus reducing unnecessary complexity and improving consistency.

4. **Chain of Memory (COM)**
   - Flyxion proposes *chain of memory* (COM) as an alternative to traditional chain-of-thought reasoning.

5. **Causal Grounding vs. Local Entropy**
   - While causal grounding (COM) ensures each step logically follows from the previous one, it alone does not guarantee the final output lies within the feasible set of reality.
   - *Local entropy*—the reduction in possibilities at each step—can lead to a terminal state that is mathematically valid but physically impossible or nonsensical.

6. **True Epistemic Progress**
   - Flyxion argues true learning isn’t about accumulating more facts (additive growth) but eliminating possibilities.
   - Knowledge contraction, akin to narrowing down options in a game like *Guess Who*, leads to a fixed point where all constraints are satisfied, representing genuine progress.

7. **Mathematical Foundations: Bannock Fixed Point Theorem**
   - This theorem formalizes the idea that iteratively pulling possible states closer together eventually reaches a unique stable state (fixed point), analogous to converging on the correct answer in reasoning processes.

8. **Political Economy and Software Platforms**
