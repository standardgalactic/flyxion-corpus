**What a Granted Patent Actually Certifies**

A granted patent does not certify the functionality or feasibility of an invention. Instead, it certifies that:

1. **Novelty:** The configuration of parts in the invention is legally new and has not been previously patented.
2. **Non-Obviousness:** The combination of those parts represents a unique innovation that would not be immediately thought of by a standard engineer or expert in the field.
3. **Enablement:** The inventor has provided sufficient detail about how to construct the device, including drawings and descriptions of its physical structure, so that someone skilled in the art could theoretically build it based on the patent documentation.

In essence, a patent is a legal document evaluating these three criteria—novelty, non-obviousness, and enablement—and not a validation of whether the invention works as claimed or adheres to known scientific principles.
