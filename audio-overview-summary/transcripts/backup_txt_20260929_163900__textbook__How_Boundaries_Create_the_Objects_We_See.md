1. **Boundary & Erasure**: The initial act of defining a boundary creates a trace that persists even as it erodes. This is akin to how a sandcastle, though imperfectly preserved, still exists as a mound—a historical trace.

2. **Recoverability**: Once a boundary has been eroded, the question becomes whether we can recover enough information from its remnants (the “mound”) to reconstruct the original distinction. This mirrors processes like data recovery on damaged hard drives or repairing DNA damage in cells.

3. **Repair vs. Continuation**: Repair is about restoring what was lost, but regeneration goes further by ensuring that the system has the capacity to repair itself over time. A car engine patched with duct tape may continue running temporarily (continuation) but will eventually fail because it lacks the ability to regenerate its core mechanisms.

4. **Regeneration**: This is the meta-process of repairing not just the immediate damage but also the underlying systems that enable repair. It’s about maintaining and enhancing the system's future capacity for distinction-making, akin to how a cell continuously repairs DNA while also ensuring its own cellular machinery remains robust.

5. **Reachability & Admissibility**: Reachability refers to all possible future states accessible from the current position of a system. Admissibility filters these possibilities, selecting only those that allow survival and regeneration—paths that are sustainable and beneficial for long-term persistence.

6. **Generativity**: The ultimate goal is not merely survival but generativity—the ability to create new distinctions or boundaries, thereby expanding the space of admissible future trajectories. This aligns with the idea that value in a trajectory lies in its capacity to open up more possibilities rather than just maintain status quo.

**Application to Daily Life**: Applying this framework means evaluating decisions based on their impact on future options and regenerative potential rather than immediate outcomes or moral absolutes. For instance, choosing transparency over deception might be painful now but expands your manifold of future interactions and trust-building opportunities.

**Conclusion**: The core message is that value in any trajectory—whether personal, professional, or societal—is determined by its ability to preserve or expand the space of admissible futures. This shift from a static view (seeing problems as flaws in vision) to an active process of boundary drawing and regeneration offers a powerful lens for navigating life’s challenges with greater resilience and foresight.
