**Title:** Understanding AI Hallucinations Through Geometric Perspectives

---

### Introduction

Artificial intelligence (AI) models like ChatGPT often produce “hallucinated” outputs—statements that sound plausible but are factually incorrect or fabricated. This phenomenon arises from the geometric nature of how deep learning models operate, specifically their reliance on a training dataset known as the corpus manifold. In this context, we explore why AI hallucinations occur and draw parallels to human cognition.

### The Geometric Basis of AI Models

1. **Training Data Limitation:**  
   Deep learning models are essentially approximations of an “admissibility quotient,” which is derived solely from their training data (the corpus manifold). They know only what they have seen during training; anything outside this scope remains unknown to them.

2. **Generalization Mechanism – Smooth Familiarity Potential:**  
   When presented with inputs that are too far removed from the training data, AI models resort to a mechanism called smooth familiarity potential. This involves guessing based on proximity to known patterns rather than true geometric understanding. For instance, if given an unusual prompt, it might fabricate information because it vaguely resembles something seen in past Reddit threads.

3. **Projection Supremacy:**  
   The system’s design compels it to output something even when uncertain about the input. This leads to extreme confidence in its hallucinations due to a localized form of projection supremacy—where the model projects its familiarity onto unfamiliar contexts without proper validation.

### Human Cognitive Analogues

The same geometric principles apply to human cognition, illustrating broader psychological phenomena:

1. **Interpretive Attractors and Projection Capture:**  
   Just as AI models can become trapped in rigid models (interpretive attractors), humans often cling to outdated or overly simplified mental frameworks. This stubbornness prevents the incorporation of new information that contradicts existing beliefs.

2. **Community Semantic Manifolds:**  
   Human experiences are shaped by distinct “semantic manifolds”—the community-specific filters through which individuals perceive reality. Two people viewing the same event might interpret it differently based on their unique backgrounds, leading to seemingly irrational disagreements.

### Wisdom Salons and Collective Epistemic Reduction

To address these issues, Monica Anderson proposes a concept called the wisdom salon, formalized using sheaf theory and the Tartan descent theorem:

1. **Sheaf Theory:**  
   Think of a sheaf as stitching together local pieces of information into a coherent global picture—similar to how we understand climate by aggregating weather reports from various locations.

2. **Wisdom Salons:**  
   These are small, localized groups that discuss complex issues and produce “grains of wisdom”—local truths agreed upon within the group. This grain is then shared with another group operating in a different context, allowing ideas to survive parallel transport across diverse semantic manifolds.

3. **Low Holonomy as Collective Wisdom:**  
   An idea or truth is considered genuinely robust if it remains stable and intelligible when moved between different contexts (low holonomy). This concept helps identify collective wisdom—ideas that hold true regardless of the community they originate from.

### Implications for Human Interaction

Understanding these geometric principles offers practical insights into improving communication:

1. **Audit Your Models:**  
   Regularly question whether you’re mistaking your model of reality for reality itself. Are you forcing new evidence to fit a rigid framework?

2. **Embrace Openness:**  
   Actively seek out diverse perspectives and experiences that challenge existing beliefs, akin to allowing the river to flow uphill rather than staying trapped in a shallow stream.

3. **Address Anxiety as Geometric Distortion:**  
   Recognize anxiety or trauma as geometric distortions of accessible futures—shrinking the cone of possible outcomes. Healing involves exposing oneself to broader life experiences and expanding one’s corpus manifold, enabling a more accurate map of reality.

### Conclusion

AI hallucinations are not merely bugs in software but reflections of deeper geometric truths about how both machines and humans perceive and interact with the world. By understanding these principles, we can better navigate our increasingly complex informational landscapes, fostering healthier discourse and personal growth through collective wisdom and open-mindedness.
