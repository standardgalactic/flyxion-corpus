### Summary of Key Concepts

1. **Baseline and Counterfactual Sovereignty**
   - The baseline often used in evaluations (e.g., zero dollars) can be manipulated by excluding alternative, highly reachable futures.
   - By restricting the comparison class, powerful entities exercise *counterfactual sovereignty*, rigging the evaluation of goodness through a chosen geometric framework.

2. **Reevaluating Scarcity**
   - Traditional economics treats scarcity as an immutable physical fact. However, Flyxion argues that scarcity is largely an evaluative construction dependent on specific allocation rules and institutional arrangements.
   - Resources are deemed scarce only when we refuse to change how things are distributed; the status quo becomes a powerful operator maintaining its own existence.

3. **Meritocracy and the Talent Reserve**
   - Meritocracy’s attribution of success solely to hard work and natural talent is critiqued as a *noun fallacy*—mistaking an outcome (success) for an intrinsic essence rather than recognizing it as a verb, i.e., a trajectory maintained by external supports.
   - The *talent reserve* represents all human capacity that remains unrealized due to invisible developmental infrastructures such as generational wealth and cultural familiarity with institutional norms.

4. **Spressatura**
   - Borrowing from the Italian Renaissance term “spressatura,” which denotes making a difficult performance appear effortless, Flyxion applies this concept to hide the strenuous history of production behind visible fluency.
   - This concealing of effort prevents recognition of the underlying trajectory and historical context that produced success.

5. **Developmental Opacity**
   - The failure to acknowledge inherited operator ecology (e.g., stable housing, professional networks) leads to a *developmental opacity* where observers attribute success solely to innate talent or hard work.
   - This opacity results in missed opportunities for understanding the full range of human potential and contributes to systemic inequities.

6. **Prediction Before Interpretation**
   - Flyxion emphasizes that theories or systems must actively refuse certain outcomes before they can be considered valid science.
   - For AI, this means not relying solely on massive data volumes but instead focusing on constraint diversity—forcing the model to reconcile multiple independent constraints simultaneously (akin to solving a complex crossword puzzle).

7. **Synthetic Grounding**
   - When training AI in simulated environments intended for physical reality, the environment must provide admissibility constraints; it should be capable of refusing certain actions.
   - Without such resistance, there is no structural grounding, and the AI fails to learn robust global models of reality.
