**Understanding the Crisis Landscape**

1. **First Crisis – Care Deficit & Societal Burnout**
   - *Manifestation*: Massive deficits in care provision (for elderly, children) due to societal burnout and demographic collapse.
   - *Cause*: Surplus energy is extracted by markets rather than being reinvested into community support systems.
   - *Historical Response*: The system scales up globally, shifting domestic crises to peripheral countries through offshoring and labor arbitrage.

2. **Second Crisis – Legitimation (Political Consent)**
   - *Manifestation*: Loss of political consent as the state is perceived merely as an enforcer of inequality.
   - *Historical Response*: Core nations extract value from peripheral ones via global labor arbitrage, buying temporary relief for domestic crises.

3. **Third Crisis – Ecological Crisis**
   - *Manifestation*: Irreversibility due to Earth’s closed nature; capitalism assumes infinite free resources and waste disposal, violating the second law of thermodynamics.
   - *Irreversibility Proof*: Below a critical biological threshold, ecological collapse is mathematically irreversible on human timescales.

**Handling the First Two Crises**

- **Scaling Up**: The system expands by exporting domestic problems to peripheral economies through offshoring and labor arbitrage. This buys time but does not solve underlying systemic issues.
  
**Inability to Export Third Crisis**

- *Ecological Crisis*: Unlike financial or labor crises, ecological impacts cannot be exported; they are bound by physical realities (e.g., finite resources, waste disposal limits).

**Mathematical Transition to a New System**

1. **Austerity & Authoritarianism**: Simply changing leaders or technocrats does not alter the structural imperatives of capitalist equations.
2. **Social Movements as Bifurcation-Seeking Perturbations**
   - *Role*: They challenge hidden costs and force systemic change by pushing the system past bifurcation points, where old rules no longer apply.
   - *Examples*: Feminist movements for paid care work, environmentalists blocking pipelines, labor strikes for universal basic services.

**Four Alternative Geometries for Post-Capitalist Society**

1. **Cooperative Ownership**
   - *Effect*: Replaces the accumulation imperative with democratic allocation of surplus, optimizing social utility over profit.
   
2. **Public Provisioning & The Commons**
   - *Effect*: Guarantees basic human needs (healthcare, housing, education, UBI) outside market mechanisms, breaking survival threshold inequality.

3. **Participatory Planning**
   - *Effect*: Expands policy dimensions to allow transparent democratic institutions to plan the macroeconomy, unfolding the map back into a full 3D globe for visibility and participation.

4. **Ecological Embedding (Steady-State Constraint)**
   - *Effect*: Imposes non-negotiable constraints ensuring resource use and waste emissions never exceed natural biosphere renewal rates, closing the open boundary to respect thermodynamic laws.

**Constructive Dimension of Transformation**

- **Chaos vs. Utopia**: Transitioning past the current system without building an alternative attractor risks systemic collapse due to unforeseen chaos.
- **Necessity of Building New Valleys**: To avoid collapse, one must construct robust cooperative channels, public commons, and democratic provisioning methods before the transition occurs.

**Final Thought**

When faced with burnout or environmental degradation, question not just immediate economic logic but hidden abodes (care deficits, societal burnout) sustaining these systems. Recognizing that labor, care, community, and nature are background conditions essential for maintaining mathematical equilibrium is crucial. The path forward involves rewriting fundamental equations to ensure survival and ecological health become foreground priorities rather than background costs.
