**Entropy and Dynamic Equilibrium**

The practical implication is captured by the statement: *“The sum over all individuals or subsystems of their entropy change rate goes to zero.”* This means that while societies can exchange resources and interact without systematically increasing disorder in one part at the expense of another, they maintain a dynamic equilibrium. Stability requires mutual corrigibility—where every component can adapt and correct its actions based on feedback from the whole system.

**Mutual Corrigibility**

This concept implies:
- **Justice and Fairness:** Not merely ideals but emergent properties that minimize net entropy production.
- **Responsiveness:** Individuals, communities, and institutions must be able to adjust their behavior in response to systemic feedback without causing cascading failures.
- **Resource Distribution:** A degree of fairness in how resources are allocated, including entropic costs (e.g., waste), ensures long-term stability.

**Phase Coherence vs. Phase Locking**

Governance must achieve:
- **Phase Coherence:** Synchronization among different parts of government for effective coordination.
- **Avoid Phase Locking:** Prevent rigidity that stifles innovation and adaptability.
