**Understanding the Crisis Landscape**

1. **First Crisis – Care Deficit & Societal Burnout**
   - *Manifestation*: Massive care deficits (e.g., childcare, eldercare) lead to societal burnout and demographic collapse.
   - *Cause*: Surplus energy is extracted by the market rather than being invested in community care or public goods.
   - *Historical Response*: The system scales up globally, shifting domestic crises to peripheral countries through offshoring and labor arbitrage.

2. **Second Crisis – Legitimation (Political Consent)**
   - *Manifestation*: Loss of political consent as the state is seen merely as an enforcer of inequality.
   - *Historical Response*: Core nations extract value from peripheries by exporting exploitation, using global labor arbitrage to extend stability.

3. **Third Crisis – Ecological Crisis**
   - *Manifestation*: Irreversibility due to Earth’s closed nature and the second law of thermodynamics (entropy).
   - *Irreversibility Proof*: Unlike financial crises that can be “reset” via printing money, ecological issues cannot be quantitatively eased; they are governed by physical reality.

**Transitioning Beyond the Attractor**

- **Mathematical Transition**: Achieving a transition to a new system requires breaking through the attractor’s stability—this is not achieved merely by electing better technocrats but by fundamentally altering the underlying equations governing capitalism.
  
- **Social Movements as Bifurcation‑Seeking Perturbations**:
  - These movements aim to dissolve hidden subsidies and expose true costs, forcing a qualitative change in system behavior (a bifurcation).
  - Examples include feminist demands for paid care work, environmentalists blocking harmful projects, and labor strikes for universal basic services.

**Alternative Geometries for Post‑Capitalist Society**

1. **Cooperative Ownership**: Replaces the imperative of infinite profit growth with democratic allocation of surplus, optimizing social utility over capital accumulation.
2. **Public Provisioning & Commons**: Guarantees basic human needs (healthcare, housing, education, UBI) outside market mechanisms, breaking the survival threshold inequality.
3. **Participatory Planning**: Restores semantic entropy by allowing transparent, democratic institutions to plan macroeconomics, expanding policy dimensions back into a full 3D map for visibility and control.
4. **Ecological Embedding**: Imposes strict steady‑state constraints where resource use and waste emissions never exceed natural biosphere renewal rates, closing the open boundary and respecting thermodynamic laws.

**Constructive Dimension of Transformation**

- Transitioning is not about shattering the current system arbitrarily; it requires building an alternative attractor (new cooperative channels, robust public commons, democratic provisioning methods) before the boulder leaves its valley.
- Failure to build these alternatives risks systemic collapse due to pure chaos and unmitigated entropy.

**Final Thought**

When faced with burnout or environmental degradation, ask not just what immediate economic logic is at play but which hidden background conditions (care, community, nature) are subsidizing it. If those silent elements become foregrounded, the existing mathematical equations of capitalism will break, necessitating a new equation that respects human and ecological realities.
