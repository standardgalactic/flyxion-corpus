# admissibility-lab/processing/Why_boundaries_are_verbs_not_nouns

The passage you’ve shared delves deeply into the concept of autonomy—both biological and societal—as a dynamic process rather than a static state. It critiques traditional views that treat nations or individuals as inherently autonomous entities, arguing instead that true autonomy is an illusion created by stable environments where external dependencies are so tightly integrated that they become imperceptible.

### Key Themes:

1. **Autonomy as Illusion**: The idea that “autonomy” is merely a mathematical approximation when delta (the difference between internal and external conditions) approaches zero. This suggests that no system can be entirely independent; all have hidden dependencies that shape their existence.

2. **Dependency and Stability**: Autonomy, in practice, hinges on the stability of these dependencies—whether they’re global shipping lanes for nations or researchers maintaining a petri dish’s temperature for cells. When external conditions stabilize enough to ignore them temporarily, we perceive an illusion of autonomy.

3. **Fusion as a Conceptual Tool**: In tightly coupled systems (like neighboring city-states with interdependent resources), Flyxion proposes the concept of “fusion,” where the boundary between two entities is redrawn into a single higher-order container. This reflects how complexity scales up in nature and society, merging distinct but dependent parts.

4. **Diagnostic Framework for Failure**: The passage introduces a five-step diagnostic tool (identifiability, efficacy, sufficiency, reflexivity, relational) to diagnose failures across various domains—from tech startups to national governments—by examining whether the maintenance processes are truly effective, sufficient, and not self-destructive or reliant on external collapses.

5. **Roots in Autopoiesis**: The framework is grounded in autopoiesis, a 1970s biological theory by Maturana and Varela, which defines living systems as self-producing networks that maintain their boundaries. Flyxion expands this concept beyond biology to all non-biological domains, addressing criticisms of circular reasoning by introducing the “identifiability rule” in step one.

6. **Thermodynamic Dissipation**: The passage concludes with a profound note on thermodynamics: maintaining order (a boundary) requires exporting entropy elsewhere, implying an ecological and moral responsibility for the cost of existence—every system’s maintenance comes at a price to its environment.

### Practical Implications:

- **Self-Awareness**: Recognizing that autonomy is contingent upon external stability encourages a more nuanced view of societal or individual structures. It prompts us to question what hidden dependencies might be undermining perceived independence.
  
- **Dynamic Maintenance**: Viewing systems as actively maintaining boundaries rather than static entities fosters an appreciation for the continuous effort required to sustain order, whether in personal habits, corporate practices, or national policies.

- **Interconnectedness**: Emphasizing that every “container” (be it a company, community, or nation) has costs elsewhere underscores the importance of considering external impacts and fostering sustainable interdependencies rather than isolating systems entirely.

This exploration challenges listeners to rethink traditional notions of autonomy and independence, encouraging a more holistic understanding of how all entities exist within broader networks where dependency is both inevitable and essential.
