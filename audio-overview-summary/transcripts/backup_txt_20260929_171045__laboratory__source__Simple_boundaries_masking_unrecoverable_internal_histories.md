**Flyxion’s Folding Philosophy: A Deep Dive into Hierarchical Prioritization and Realizable Constraints**

### Core Concepts

1. **Material Continuity (Priority One)**
   - The fundamental rule is that the paper must remain intact; tearing it invalidates the topological premise of the entire folding process.
   - Any fold threatening to rip the sheet must be abandoned.

2. **Selected Face Identity (Priority Two)**
   - Ensures that designated squares stay on the exterior, preserving the communicative purpose of the cube—visibility and legibility from outside.
   - Violation results in a failure of boundary grammar, making the object unusable for its intended function.

3. **Required Incidents (Priority Three)**
   - Achieving vertex gluing is mandatory; without it, the exterior faces cannot be properly connected, rendering the cube non-functional.
   - This step demands aggressive routing to displace surplus material effectively.

4. **Edge Orientation (Priority Four)**
   - Maintains that bottoms stay as bottoms and tops remain distinct, ensuring structural integrity and correct orientation of each face.
   - Prevents misalignment that could arise from chaotic folding or insufficient tension management.

5. **Planarity (Priority Five)**
   - Aims to keep exterior faces relatively flat rather than bowed out by internal pressure, enhancing aesthetic appeal but not at the expense of functionality.
   - Recognizes that planarity is a secondary concern compared to structural integrity and connectivity.

6. **Cosmetic Regularity (Priority Six)**
   - The lowest priority; neatness or perfect smoothness of faces is sacrificed for functional success.
   - Demonstrates that aesthetic perfection can be compromised if it leads to failure in meeting higher-order constraints.

### Practical Realizability

- **Physical Laws That Stop Success:**
  1. **Contradictory Edge Rolls:** Simultaneous upward and downward facing regions are physically impossible due to the sheet’s topological nature.
  2. **Insufficient Connecting Area:** If the surplus ratio is too low, the physical gap between regions exceeds what can be folded inward by the material itself.
  3. **Excessive Material Thickness:** Folding increases thickness; when it becomes a solid block, further manipulation (folding) is impossible.
  4. **Inadequate Interior Volume:** The suitcase is full of surplus material with insufficient space for displacement, making internal volume containment unfeasible.
  5. **Demanding Geometric Exactness:** Absolute perfection in planarity and edge sharpness is impractical due to uneven tension within the crumpled interior.

### Philosophical Implications

- **From Ideal to Mechanical Realizability:**
  - The idealization assumes zero-thickness, magical paper where any configuration is possible.
  - Mechanical realizability demands consideration of real-world constraints like thickness and volume, ensuring that a human can actually execute the folding process within reasonable time and effort.

### Broader Application

- **Hidden Complexity in Systems:**
  - Complex entities (software architectures, corporate structures, legal frameworks) often present clean exteriors while concealing chaotic internal histories.
  - The principle of Flyxion underscores the importance of questioning what lies beneath a seemingly flawless exterior, revealing hidden complexities that drive functionality.

### Final Thought

- **Critical Observation and Questioning:**
  - Encourage continuous inquiry into how systems achieve their apparent simplicity—what processes have been employed to hide or manage underlying chaos?
  - This mindset fosters deeper understanding and more resilient design practices across various domains.

By adhering to Flyxion’s hierarchy of protected distinctions, one can navigate the chaotic nature of folding (and by extension, many complex systems) while ensuring that essential functional requirements are met. The supercube serves as a testament to this approach, proving that practical realizability is achievable through disciplined prioritization and acceptance of aesthetic imperfections in favor of structural integrity.
