   - AI models typically ignore these complex chemical interactions because simulating them would be computationally intensive and practically impossible.

5. **Implications for AI and Mind-Uploading**:
   - The core takeaway is that intelligence in biological systems is not stored as static data but as an ongoing, dynamic process influenced by environmental factors.
   - This challenges the notion of mind-uploading (uploading a human mind into a computer). If consciousness is distributed and continuously generated rather than being a fixed file, then simply copying or transferring it would be mathematically flawed.
   - To truly replicate human intelligence in AI, one might need to understand how biological systems self-assemble their own "fire" (intelligence), which currently remains beyond our technological grasp.

Overall, the passage emphasizes that understanding and replicating general intelligence requires acknowledging the complexity and dynamism of biological processes, rather than attempting to bypass them with static data structures. This perspective encourages a more nuanced approach to AI development, one that respects the inherent messiness and adaptability of biological systems.
