**Lev Vygotsky’s developmental account of genuine inner speech emphasizes that authentic human thought is highly compressed and predicative—essentially a “click” or intuitive grasp rather than an elaborate verbal articulation. This aligns with how we often think internally when solving complex problems: we don’t need to explain the entire solution in detail, but merely feel the concepts locking together as a structural understanding.

When applied to large language models (LLMs), this insight highlights a key difference: LLMs expand their outputs into verbose, fully articulated text. This expansion can be seen as an epistemic hazard because it may lead users to mistake length and fluency for depth of understanding, potentially eroding the compressed, intuitive grasp that genuine human cognition relies on.

**Deontological vs. Phenomenological Approaches**

- **Deontological Discipline:** Advocates a rule‑based approach—treating LLMs as calculators or text formatters only for specific tasks (e.g., formatting data). This limits reliance on evaluative judgments, aiming to protect cognitive autonomy by preventing the AI from becoming an authority figure.

- **Phenomenological Priority:** Suggests maintaining acute awareness of one’s own lived experience and phenomenology—knowing what it feels like to truly understand. It proposes using LLMs dynamically for critique or perturbation rather than strictly as calculators, allowing them to act as cognitive regulators that shake up entrenched mental models.

**Convergence on Human Cognition**

Both perspectives acknowledge the fundamental difference between human cognition (deeply grounded in embodied experience) and generative AI (operating solely within language). The debate centers not on what the machine is but how we integrate it into our thinking processes. Whether one builds rigid “sea walls” to protect cognitive autonomy or learns to navigate turbulent linguistic waters while staying anchored to pre‑linguistic judgment ultimately depends on personal epistemic values and workflow needs.

In essence, the choice between strict deontological rules and a phenomenologically aware engagement with LLMs reflects deeper philosophical commitments about the nature of understanding itself—whether we prioritize structural integrity (rigid boundaries) or dynamic interaction that leverages AI’s linguistic strengths while safeguarding our own intuitive grasp.
