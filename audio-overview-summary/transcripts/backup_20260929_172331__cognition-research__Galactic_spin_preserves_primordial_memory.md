**Mathematical Explanation – The Koopman Operator & Spectral Persistence**

1. **Problem Context:**  
   In a galaxy undergoing mergers, individual stars lose their original trajectories due to chaotic interactions (non‑invertible transformations). To prove that some relational memory survives this chaos, we need a mathematical framework.

2. **Koopman Operator Overview:**  
   - **Traditional Approach:** Tracking each star’s exact position and momentum is impossible because of entropy and the non‑linear nature of gravitational dynamics.
   - **Koopman Shift:** Instead of focusing on individual physical states (the “messy room”), we track how observable quantities—like total angular momentum or rotational patterns—evolve over time.

3. **Linearization via Koopman Operator:**  
   - The operator maps the nonlinear dynamical system into a new space where the evolution of these observables becomes linear.
   - This linearity allows us to apply powerful tools from linear algebra, such as spectral decomposition.

4. **Spectral Decomposition & Two Subspaces:**  
   - **Mixing (Decaying) Subspace:** Represents chaotic, irreversible processes where information dissipates exponentially. Any observable confined here will lose its memory of the past.
   - **Peripheral / Unimodular Subspace:** Observables in this subspace evolve quasi‑periodically and do not decay to zero; they retain a persistent “hum” or fundamental frequency.

5. **Theorem One – Spectral Persistence Criterion:**  
   - If the initial misalignment (the primordial distinction, like the proto‑halo’s orientation) projects onto the peripheral spectral subspace, then:
     \[
     \lim_{t\to\infty} \langle f(t), f(0) \rangle > 0
     \]
     where \(f(t)\) is an observable function evolving under the Koopman operator. This means that despite chaotic mixing elsewhere, a measurable correlation (e.g., rotational memory) persists indefinitely.

6. **Implications for Astrophysics:**  
   - Galaxies act as “cosmological archaeology” devices: they retain imprints of early‑universe conditions in their observable dynamics.
   - By measuring these persistent correlations (like galaxy spin), we can infer properties of the universe at its birth, such as primordial neutrino masses or other hidden fields.

7. **Broader Impact:**  
   - This framework shifts our view from isolated objects to dynamic “receivers” of cosmic history.
   - It opens avenues for detecting invisible phenomena (e.g., ancient neutrinos) through observable signatures in galaxy dynamics.

**Conclusion:** The Koopman operator, combined with spectral analysis, provides a rigorous mathematical proof that relational memory—despite the overwhelming chaos of galactic mergers—survives and can be measured. This transforms our understanding of galaxies as not just collections of stars but as carriers of deep, enduring cosmic histories.
