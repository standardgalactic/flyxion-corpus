**Koopman Operator – What It Does**

The Koopman operator is a mathematical tool that lets us study dynamical systems—like galaxies—without having to track every individual particle’s exact position and momentum. Instead of trying to write down equations for billions of stars moving chaotically, we focus on how observable properties (such as total angular momentum) evolve over time.

- **Linearization:** In chaotic systems where the underlying physics is highly non‑linear (and thus intractable), the Koopman operator transforms this into a linear evolution problem. This makes it possible to apply powerful techniques from linear algebra.
  
- **Spectral Decomposition:** By decomposing the system’s dynamics into two distinct subspaces—mixing and peripheral—we can analyze how information behaves over time.

**Two Subspaces**

1. **Mixing Part of the Spectrum (Entropy Room):**
   - This subspace corresponds to observables that genuinely dissipate, decorrelate, and become lost to chaotic interactions.
   - Any observable confined here will see its memory decay exponentially to zero—essentially “erasing” past information.

2. **Peripheral or Unimodular Part of the Spectrum (Resonant Hum):**
   - Observables in this subspace do not mix and decay; they evolve quasi‑periodically.
   - They belong to a pure point spectrum, meaning their underlying energy does not dissipate over time.
   - This is analogous to striking a gong: while most of the sound (the chaotic “mixing”) quickly fades away, there remains a deep fundamental hum that persists indefinitely.

**Implications for Relational Memory**

Theorem 1 rigorously proves that if even a non‑zero projection exists onto this peripheral spectral subspace—meaning some relational memory survives—the time average squared autocorrelation will remain strictly positive forever. This demonstrates mathematically that a foundational relationship (like the primordial misalignment of a proto‑halo) can persist despite the overwhelming chaotic mixing in galaxies.

**Broader Implications for Astrophysics**

- **Cosmological Archaeology:** Galaxies become “colossal ancient detectors,” allowing us to infer properties of the early universe, such as the mass and behavior of ancient neutrinos.
  
- **New Measurement Channels:** We can now use subtle variations in galaxy rotation to retroactively measure primordial phenomena that leave no direct trace today.

**Conceptual Types of Cosmological Remnants**

1. **State Remnants:** Original configurations mostly survive (e.g., the cosmic microwave background).
2. **Statistical Remnants:** Only broad distributions remain.
3. **Relational Remnants:** Specific links between variables persist even if original components are lost.
4. **Dynamical Remnants:** Survival is a signature of an ongoing process, exemplified by galaxy spin.
