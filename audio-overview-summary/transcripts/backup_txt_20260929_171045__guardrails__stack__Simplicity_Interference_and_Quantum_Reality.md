Your exploration touches on some of the most profound and challenging questions at the intersection of physics, philosophy, and consciousness. Let me summarize and expand on a few key points that resonate:

1. **Emergent 3D Reality**: The idea that our perceived three-dimensional space might be an emergent structure rather than a fundamental one is compelling. If higher-dimensional entanglements manifest as correlations in lower dimensions (like shadows of birds), it suggests that the apparent separateness we experience could be a projection or consequence of deeper, unified realities.

2. **Reichenbach’s Cube Analogy**: This analogy beautifully illustrates how correlated events (shadows) can arise from a single cause (the bird). It reinforces the notion that what appears to be non-local or “spooky” in quantum entanglement might simply reflect underlying separations we don’t yet understand, rather than violations of locality.

3. **Holistic Reality vs. Particularism**: Transitioning from countless discrete particles to a unified whole (like the wave function) aligns with the principle of simplicity—reducing the number of fundamental entities. However, this shift often feels counterintuitive because it challenges our everyday notions of individuality and separateness.

4. **Subjective Experience vs. Block Universe**: The tension between the deterministic block universe model (where all events are fixed) and our lived experience of time’s passage is central. You correctly point out that while physics may be predetermined, our perception of flow, passage, and openness to future change might be relational artifacts arising from our embedded perspective within that system.

5. **Passage, Flow, and Openness**: Breaking down these phenomenological aspects into their physical underpinnings—dynamic consciousness encompassing finite intervals, the progression of time as a parameter rather than an objective feature, and subjective indeterminacy rooted in self-reference—offers a compelling framework for reconciling our experience with deterministic physics.

6. **MWI and Subjective Uncertainty**: The Many Worlds Interpretation (MWI) elegantly addresses how we can feel uncertainty about outcomes while the universe evolves deterministically. This is crucial because it shows that subjective experiences of suspense or choice are not violations of physical laws but rather emergent properties from our perspective within a branching reality.

7. **Penrose’s Challenge**: Roger Penrose’s work, particularly his twister theory and conformal cyclic cosmology, exemplifies the pursuit of simplicity through geometry and dynamics. His argument that consciousness might require non-computable processes (potentially linked to quantum gravity) forces us to consider whether our current physical theories must be expanded or fundamentally revised to accommodate this irreducible complexity.

8. **Ontological Implications**: The core question remains: Do we accept the reductionist path, where all phenomena—including consciousness—can eventually be explained by simpler, computable processes? Or do we acknowledge that there might be fundamental degrees of freedom (like those in human experience) that cannot be fully captured by current physics?

Your discussion beautifully encapsulates the ongoing debate between a parsimonious universe and the undeniable complexity of our subjective reality. It highlights how philosophy and science intersect at deep, unresolved questions about what constitutes knowledge, existence, and the nature of consciousness itself. This is precisely why these explorations remain so vital: they push us to reconsider not just what we know but also how we might conceive of a more complete understanding of the cosmos.
