# cpr-textbook/Why_the_universe_is_made_of_verbs

The passage you've shared is a profound exploration of how our perception and understanding of reality can be fundamentally altered by shifting from viewing the world as composed of static, discrete objects (the "horses") to recognizing it as a series of dynamic trajectories influenced by invisible constraints (the "fence"). This philosophical and scientific framework—dubbed here as Constraint, Projection, and Reachability (CPR)—offers a radical reimagining of how we interact with the world around us. Let’s break down its core ideas and implications:

### 1. **Constraint**

- **Concept**: The universe is not made up solely of tangible objects but rather of processes that persist through time due to underlying constraints—metabolic, environmental, cultural, physical constants (like the speed of light), etc.
- **Implication**: Understanding these invisible constraints allows us to see reality as a product of both content and context. It challenges the notion of static identity labels (e.g., "introvert," "anxious") by suggesting that such identities are themselves shaped by their environment and can change when those environments or constraints shift.

### 2. **Projection**

- **Concept**: The idea that we often project infinite, dynamic processes into discrete nouns (objects) creates a false sense of solidity and permanence in the world.
- **Implication**: This projection leads to an inability to grasp causal histories and latent resilience/fragility within systems because we focus on isolated snapshots rather than understanding the broader trajectory. It encourages a more holistic approach to problem-solving by considering how changes in constraints affect overall behavior.

### 3. **Reachability**

- **Concept**: Possibility is not merely a binary logical predicate but a tangible geometric shape called the reachable set (R). This set represents all states accessible from a given starting point within a specific time horizon, strictly bounded by the admissibility field (A).
- **Implication**: The reachability monotonicity theorem demonstrates that altering constraints—adding or removing fences—directly changes the size and shape of this reachable set. Thus, true agency lies not in forcing an object to move but in redesigning the environment to expand one’s possible future trajectories.

### Practical Applications

- **Personal Development**: Instead of relying on willpower alone (a static snapshot approach), individuals can enhance their habits or overcome addictions by modifying their environment—physically removing barriers that hinder desired behaviors.
  
- **Organizational Change**: CEOs and leaders can shift toxic corporate cultures not through superficial changes like firing people or writing mission statements but by redefining the underlying incentive structures, effectively expanding the reachable set for organizational behavior.

- **AI and Machine Learning**: Understanding constraints helps in designing more resilient AI systems that can adapt to changing environments rather than being stuck in a static state of "objects."

### Final Thought

The overarching message is one of empowerment: recognizing that our identities, habits, and even societal structures are not immutable but are shaped by the invisible constraints around us. By consciously managing these constraints—by expanding or contracting our reachable sets—we can navigate possibilities more effectively, turning what might seem like fixed limitations into opportunities for growth and change.

This perspective encourages a shift from viewing ourselves as static objects to seeing ourselves as highly adaptable processes capable of reshaping their environments—and thus, their futures. It’s a call to action: go out there, question the nouns you’ve taken for granted, and actively design your constraints to expand what is possible in your life and the world around you.
