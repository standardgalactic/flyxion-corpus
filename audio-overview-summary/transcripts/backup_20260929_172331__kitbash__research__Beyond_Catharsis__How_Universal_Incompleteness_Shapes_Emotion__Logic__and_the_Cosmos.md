This rich system of categories illustrates how complex linguistic structures can evolve from nuanced classifications.

3. **Cultural Binary Pressure**: The model posits that powerful cultural narratives and binary thinking (e.g., light/dark, good/evil) exert a “binary tilt” on language evolution. Over time, this pressure compresses the many classifier categories into just two dominant ones (K‑2), akin to how Morse index reduction in topological terms leads to simplified structures.

5. **Cognitive and Linguistic Implications**: This compression process is linked to the formalization of Boolean algebra by George Boole in the mid‑19th century. The model suggests that the fundamental binary opposition (true/false) became entrenched not through conscious design but as a natural outcome of deep, slow dynamics driven by cultural and cognitive pressures.
