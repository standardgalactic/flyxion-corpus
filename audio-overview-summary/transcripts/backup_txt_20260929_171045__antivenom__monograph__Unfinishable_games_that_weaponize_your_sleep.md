**Title: The Right to Absence – Designing Digital Systems for Safe Disengagement**

---

### Introduction

The concept of "unfinishability" in digital systems—where players are compelled to remain engaged indefinitely—is explored through the lens of a modern mobile game architecture. This analysis delves into how persistent online games exploit psychological and social vulnerabilities, making it nearly impossible for users to disengage safely without facing significant consequences.

### Key Themes

1. **Persistent Engagement & Psychological Extraction**
   - Games like those in the StarCraft series allow players to quit at any time, preserving a sense of control over their engagement.
   - Modern persistent alliance games, however, do not offer this option; continuous exposure is built into the gameplay loop, making disengagement costly and often impossible.

2. **The Right to Absence**
   - Proposed as a design criterion, it mandates that any system must allow participants to invoke a bounded absence interval where their exposure drops below a tolerable threshold.
   - The critical aspect of this right is its unilateral nature—players should not need the consent or cooperation of others (e.g., alliance leaders) to disengage.

3. **Challenges in Implementation**
   - **Individual Shields:** Protect solo players but burden collective leaders, leading to inequitable distribution of responsibility.
   - **Alliance Vulnerability Windows:** Setting specific attack times can mitigate immediate threats but severely diminishes the game's dynamic nature and player engagement.
   - **Asynchronous Attack Declarations & Reversible Losses:** These solutions aim to reduce risk but also lower stakes, potentially destroying the core appeal of competitive play.

4. **Social vs. Regulatory Solutions**
   - Social fixes like mandatory role redundancy force players to share responsibilities across different time zones, addressing U variable substitutability.
   - However, these require high levels of social management and communication among players, which can be impractical in global teams with diverse schedules.

5. **Broader Implications Beyond Gaming**
   - The principles discussed extend beyond gaming into professional environments (e.g., Slack notifications for on-call staff), gig economy work, and financial markets requiring constant availability.
   - These systems exploit social guilt and professional obligations to maintain continuous engagement, mirroring the unfinishability seen in games.

6. **Regulatory Considerations**
   - Unlike loot boxes, which can be regulated like gambling due to their direct monetary extraction, temporal extraction is invisible and harder to regulate.
   - Proposed solutions would require a paradigm shift in how software design standards are viewed, potentially treating users as laborers with occupational scheduling protections akin to those for gig workers.

### Conclusion

The exploration of "unfinishability" through the lens of digital architecture reveals that many modern systems—beyond gaming—are designed to exploit psychological and social vulnerabilities. The proposed "right to absence" offers a framework for ensuring safe disengagement, but its implementation demands innovative solutions balancing persistence with user autonomy. As we navigate increasingly interconnected digital futures, safeguarding our ability to step away from these systems becomes crucial.
