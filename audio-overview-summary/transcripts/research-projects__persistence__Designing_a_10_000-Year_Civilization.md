# research-projects/persistence/Designing_a_10,000-Year_Civilization

The passage you shared articulates a profound vision of civilization that emphasizes stability, sustainability, and the long-term viability of human society. Here are some key themes and takeaways from the discussion:

1. **Sticky Identities**: The idea is that persistent identities—those that cannot be easily changed or laundered away—are crucial for building trust and reputation over time. This discourages superficial behavior changes and encourages accountability, as individuals must own up to their actions rather than simply resetting identity (e.g., becoming a new "user 1" after making mistakes).

2. **Admissible vs. Inadmissible Apps**: The text distinguishes between applications that contribute positively to society (admissible) and those designed primarily for extraction of user capacity (inadmissible). For example, gambling apps are labeled inadmissible because they drain users' money, dopamine, and attention without providing any lasting benefit or skill development.

3. **Governance as Curriculum**: Instead of viewing governance through a punitive lens—where laws are enforced to punish violations—the text proposes that laws should serve educational purposes (curriculum). This shift aims at teaching individuals the necessary skills and behaviors for long-term societal health, rather than merely deterring wrongdoing.

4. **Infrastructure as Simulation**: Cities and infrastructure are described not just as physical structures but as simulations that teach habits and prepare populations for future challenges. The critique of hidden water systems is a metaphor for how modern infrastructures can mask underlying realities (like resource scarcity), leading to panic when those truths surface.

5. **Substitution Over Prohibition**: Given the failure of prohibition in practice, the text advocates for substitution—making undesirable behaviors or products obsolete by offering better alternatives that are economically viable and socially acceptable. This approach is seen as less disruptive than outright bans, which can create black markets and undermine trust in governance.

6. **Intelligence Defined**: Intelligence here is reframed from traditional measures of IQ or computational speed to the ability to maintain a viable trajectory over time. It emphasizes adaptability and foresight—being able to navigate challenges without crashing into irreversible failure (e.g., societal collapse).

7. **Outstrapping Utopia**: The overarching goal is not utopia in its current, fleeting sense but rather outstripping it by ensuring the civilization can persist for millennia. This requires a careful balance between innovation and sustainability, respecting the physical and social substrates that support human life.

In summary, the text presents a holistic framework for building a resilient society—one where identity, governance, technology, and infrastructure are all designed with long-term viability in mind. It challenges conventional thinking about freedom, progress, and responsibility by emphasizing constraint as a means to preserve choice and stability over time.
