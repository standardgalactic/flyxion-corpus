1. **Understanding Degenerate Observables**
   - In physics, a *degenerate observable* occurs when multiple distinct quantum states yield identical measured values (e.g., degeneracy in energy levels).  
   - For human learning, this translates into standardized tests where two students—though both improve by the same score—may have vastly different underlying understandings of physics. 2. **The Reflexivity Complication**
   - Human beings are reflexive; they know they’re being measured (e.g., GPA, test scores). This awareness leads to *Goodhart’s Law*—when a metric becomes the target, it ceases to be an effective measure of what truly matters.
   - In education, this manifests as students optimizing for test scores rather than genuine learning. The result is “walking on an Escher staircase” where local correctness (the score) masks a lack of global inadmissibility (true understanding).

3. **Endpoint Metrics Mislead**
   - Whether it’s AI predicting the next word, physicists measuring charge density, or universities maximizing standardized test scores, endpoint metrics reward immediate success while ignoring broader admissibility.
   - This systemic flaw suggests that optimizing for flat, degenerate observables—no matter how flawless they appear on paper today—guarantees collapse in the future due to ignored constraint geometry.

4. **Implications Across Society**

   - To survive complexity in the coming decades, we must design systems (digital, physical, institutional) that preserve admissibility over time, not just flat, endpoint surfaces.
