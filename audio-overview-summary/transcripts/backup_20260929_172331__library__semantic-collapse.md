**Summary**

The essay by Stephen Henry McRae critiques Anthony Flew’s proposal that atheism should be understood as a “default” position—simply the lack of belief in God, rather than an active claim that God does not exist. McRae argues that adopting this view leads to what he calls a *semantic collapse*: when atheism is defined both as strong (belief God doesn’t exist) and weak (mere non‑belief), it overlaps with agnosticism so much that the terms “atheist,” “theist,” and “agnostic” lose distinct meanings. Using the semiotic square of opposition—a logical framework for mapping relationships between opposing concepts—McRae shows how this blurred definition creates contradictions where a person could be simultaneously classified as an atheist, theist, and agnostic. He contends that preserving clear distinctions among these terms is essential to maintain meaningful philosophical debate and avoid communication breakdowns.
