3. **Thermodynamic Geography** – A method for empirically measuring these fields using proxies such as GDP per capita, nighttime light intensity (VIRS), VC funding flows, flight data, and sectoral diversity indices. These metrics map the “topography” of value, revealing where capacity peaks, flow diverges, and entropy is highest.
4. **Recursive Futarchy** – A governance model that continuously recalibrates societal goals using prediction markets to forecast policy outcomes, thereby adjusting systemic parameters (like friction in trade networks) to maintain optimal curvature and entropy levels.
5. **Entropy Corridor & Stabilization** – The concept of entropic stewardship aims to keep societies within a “productive zone” where enough change occurs for innovation but not so much that chaos overwhelms stability.

The essay ties these ideas to broader philosophical questions about agency, risk homeostasis in technology (distinguishing between imperative and complementary artifacts), and the paradoxical role of institutional gravity in shaping societal discourse. It concludes by emphasizing that civilization’s survival hinges on preserving intelligibility—maintaining a symmetry difference where potential is harnessed as care.
