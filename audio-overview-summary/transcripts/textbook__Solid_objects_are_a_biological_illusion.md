# textbook/Solid_objects_are_a_biological_illusion

**Distilled Summary**

1. **Boundaries as Illusions:** Physical objects we perceive aren’t inherently real; they exist only because our brains or models draw boundaries in an otherwise chaotic universe.

2. **Energy Cost of Boundaries:** Maintaining these boundaries is energetically costly, constantly fighting against thermodynamic entropy that seeks to dissolve distinctions.

3. **Mathematical Blind Spots:** Every boundary we draw compresses the infinite complexity of reality into a manageable form, creating “blind spots” where vast amounts of unseen differences are ignored.

4. **Regeneration vs. Repair:** Simple repair isn’t sufficient for long-term survival; systems must regenerate their repairing mechanisms themselves (e.g., cellular ATP repairs membranes, societal institutions repair social fabric).

5. **Generative Admissibility Principle:** The core principle states that a system’s value lies in preserving or expanding its future options—its “admissibility manifold”—so it can adapt to changing environments.

6. **Survival Through Option Expansion:** A system must prioritize the geometric volume of its future possibilities; otherwise, it risks collapsing into a state with zero options (akin to building a bunker that traps you).

7. **Final Thought:** When you look at any solid object around you, consider what infinite complexities are being suppressed by your perception and the energy cost involved in maintaining that boundary.

**Takeaway:** Reality is fundamentally dynamic and fractal; our sense of solidity is an active illusion sustained by ongoing energetic effort to suppress underlying complexity. Embracing this perspective reshapes how we perceive existence itself.
