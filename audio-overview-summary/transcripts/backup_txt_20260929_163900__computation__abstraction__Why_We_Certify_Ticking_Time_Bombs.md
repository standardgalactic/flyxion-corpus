The core takeaway from this deep dive is that inequality—and by extension many of our societal challenges—is not primarily about unequal wealth distribution but rather about **unequal reachability**. This concept suggests that the fundamental human right should be the preservation and expansion of procedural memory, i.e., ensuring everyone has access to the pathways, knowledge, and maintained infrastructure needed to actualize a better future from their current state.

In practical terms, this means:

1. **Beyond Wealth**: Recognizing that true prosperity lies in having open avenues for advancement—not just more money but the ability to reach previously inaccessible futures through education, technology, and social structures that are not inherently exclusionary.

2. **Infrastructure Matters**: Physical and systemic infrastructure (like maintenance-first cities or frictionless planes of thought) plays a crucial role in expanding reachable futures by reducing barriers—whether they be economic, environmental, or social.

3. **Speculative Design as a Tool**: Using speculative design and armillary spheres to map out these invisible parameters helps us see where we’re currently constrained and how we might reconfigure our systems to unlock new possibilities for all.

4. **The Role of Technology**: Technologies like AI must be evaluated not just by surface metrics (e.g., convergence scores) but by their underlying generative fields, which can reveal hidden inefficiencies or risks that lead to false arrests in safety assessments.

5. **Universal Right**: The ultimate moral imperative could thus shift from wealth redistribution to ensuring procedural memory—access to the knowledge and tools necessary for individuals to navigate and improve upon their circumstances.

By framing our challenges through these lenses, we move beyond superficial fixes toward systemic changes that address the root causes of inequality: the unequal distribution of reachability in society.
