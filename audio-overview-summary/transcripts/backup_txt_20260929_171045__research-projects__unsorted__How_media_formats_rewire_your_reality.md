This passage delves deeply into the philosophical and practical implications of media formats, digital interfaces, and human cognition. Here’s a breakdown of its core themes:

### 1. **Enclosure and Medium Dependency**
- **Concept**: The idea that we can become overly reliant on specific media (like videos or AI chatbots) to the point where we forget about underlying realities or "residue."
- **Implication**: This dependency creates an enclosure, making us unable to perceive truths beyond what the medium presents. It’s akin to being trapped in a digital echo chamber.

### 2. **Exit Test**
- **Purpose**: To evaluate whether you can function without a particular medium (e.g., if your AI chatbot server goes down).
- **Significance**: Demonstrates the scaffolding role of media versus its potential as a “wheelchair” that restricts independent thinking.

### 3. **Resistance Test**
- **Focus**: How we handle ambiguity, contradiction, or unwelcome evidence.
- **Challenge**: Most people resort to quick summaries (like podcasts) to resolve discomfort, whereas true resilience involves tolerating friction and uncertainty.

### 4. **Cross-Modal Test**
- **Requirement**: Testing claims across different formats (e.g., reading persuasive prose vs. mapping it out logically).
- **Importance**: Recognizing that no single format provides the whole truth; cross-referencing multiple forms is essential for robust understanding.

### 5. **Alterity Test**
- **Challenge**: Tolerance for constraints and human independence outside our preferred framing.
- **Relevance**: Crucial in an AI-driven era where interactions often conform to predefined templates, potentially stifling genuine dialogue and problem-solving.

### 6. **Functional Concentration Test**
- **Issue**: The tendency of individuals or institutions (like corporations) to consolidate all vital functions into a single interface (e.g., smartphones).
- **Consequence**: This creates vulnerability; if the interface fails, essential services collapse, mirroring how digital company towns can lead to systemic failures.

### Cross-Modal Literacy
- **Definition**: The cultivated capacity to understand and compare different forms of representation without treating their absence as a failure.
- **Key Elements**:
  - **Declared Preservation Criteria**: Being honest about what is preserved and lost in translation.
  - **Maintaining Productive Friction**: Refusing to let technology smooth over the complexities of reality.
  - **Alternation**: Using multiple partial representations side by side rather than seeking a universal translator.

### Analogy with Binocular Vision
- The passage draws an analogy between cross-modal literacy and binocular vision, emphasizing that diversity in perception (like having two eyes) creates depth and understanding. Each "eye" (or format) provides a different perspective, and the brain’s ability to integrate these perspectives is crucial for accurate interpretation.

### Implications for Future Technologies
- **Brain-Computer Interfaces**: As neural implants become mainstream, the line between external media and internal perception will blur.
- **Risk of Total Enclosure**: If interfaces are directly overlaid onto our visual cortex, they could rewrite reality in real time, making it impossible to perceive "outside" influences or correct inaccuracies.

### Final Thought
The overarching message is a call for critical awareness about how we consume information. It urges us to be mindful of the formats we rely on and to cultivate skills that allow us to navigate multiple perspectives without becoming overly dependent on any single medium. This cross-modal literacy is seen as essential for maintaining cognitive flexibility, resilience in the face of ambiguity, and preventing total enclosure by technology or AI.
