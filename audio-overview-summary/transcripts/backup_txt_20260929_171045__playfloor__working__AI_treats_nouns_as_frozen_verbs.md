**Mental Model Explanation**

Imagine you’re navigating through an unfamiliar kitchen (the “possibility space” of ideas or actions). You don’t have a full map of the entire layout, but at each moment you stand in a specific spot. Instead of memorizing every possible path to get from one end of the kitchen to another, you simply look around and ask:

> *If I were standing exactly where I am right now, which direction would the teacher (or “expert”) move?*  

You then copy that local directional flow—matching the teacher’s immediate velocity at your current location. By doing this repeatedly across many points, even though each step is tiny, you gradually align with a larger trajectory that leads to success globally.

**Why It Works**

1. **Local Admissibility Agreement:**  
   Matching the teacher’s local velocity ensures you’re always moving in a direction that respects the immediate constraints of your environment (like staying on the right wall in a maze). This is akin to obeying differential equations: each small step follows the gradient of the constraint field.

2. **Global Reachability Guaranteed by Mathematics:**  
   The underlying theorem—grounded in differential equations and stability theory—states that if you consistently follow these local rules, the cumulative effect will lead you to a global destination (success) without ever needing to know the entire path beforehand.

3. **No Fixed Script Required:**  
   Unlike following an explicit script (a rigid plan), this method allows flexibility: you can explore nearby admissible paths and discover more efficient routes that the teacher might never have considered, much like finding shortcuts in a maze.

**Application Beyond AI**

- **Physics Analogy:** The idea mirrors how particles behave under gravitational fields—short-range forces act locally, while long-range constraints shape the overall trajectory.
  
- **AI Alignment Implications:** Instead of penalizing bad behavior (negative rewards), you reshape the geometry of admissible futures so that harmful actions are naturally avoided. This is a profound shift from “punishment” to “designing the landscape itself.”

**Key Takeaway**

The mental model illustrates that success isn’t about memorizing every detail or following a static script; it’s about aligning with local constraints and letting the underlying mathematical structure (like differential equations) guide you toward global goals. This perspective transforms how we think about learning, decision-making, and even AI alignment—by focusing on shaping the geometry of possibilities rather than enforcing rules from outside.
