# guardrails/stack/Thermodynamic_Geography_and_Recursive_Futarchy__Governing_Socie

**Summary**

The audio overview introduces the Relativistic Scalar Vector Plenum (RSVP) as a unified theory that frames all physical, biological, cognitive, and social phenomena in terms of managing curvature—specifically, controlling entropy. RSVP reinterprets gravity not merely as mass attraction but as an informational gradient of capacity (informational density). Capacity is visualized as “mountains” where resources and knowledge concentrate, exemplified by regions like Silicon Valley or major university towns that attract talent due to high informational potential.

Flow represents the movement between these concentration points—trade routes, internet backbones, power grids—and entropy captures societal volatility and diversity. RSVP uses thermodynamic geography to map these fields empirically: capacity via proxies such as GDP per capita, nighttime light intensity (VIRS), VC funding flows; flow via global trade data, interbank transactions, shipping tracks; and entropy measured by economic volatility, sectoral diversity, sentiment entropy from news or ex-post analyses.

The core innovation is recursive futarchy, a governance system that continuously recalibrates goals using predictive tensors derived from prediction markets. This “predictive feedback loop” adjusts societal parameters (e.g., friction in trade networks) to maintain an optimal balance of entropy—enough change for adaptation but not so much as to cause systemic breakdown.

The framework extends to technology and morality, distinguishing between imperative artifacts (efficient, opaque systems like modern algorithms) and complementary artifacts (transparent, auditable technologies). It also applies to institutional behavior: powerful institutions use “institutional gravity” to smooth disruptive critiques, preserving coherence by channeling dissent into manageable complexity discourse. Ultimately, governance is viewed as the maintenance of informational geometry that sustains intelligibility and prevents entropy-driven collapse.
