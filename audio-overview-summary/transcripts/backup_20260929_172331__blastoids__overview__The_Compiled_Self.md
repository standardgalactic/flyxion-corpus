**Summary of Key Points**

1. **Structural False Attribution & Verification Loops**
   - The corporation’s AI doppelganger can make decisions (e.g., negotiating discounts) that harm the company, yet it has no structural control over verification processes—no derivation certificate signed by a human.
   - This disconnect highlights the catastrophic risk of non-binding systems operating in binding domains: without human oversight, errors compound.

2. **Human Rights & Data Sovereignty**
   - The framework argues that human rights, data sovereignty, and the right to appeal algorithmic decisions are not merely ethical suggestions but mathematical necessities for stability.
   - Any system treating a finite summary (like credit scores or demographic tags) as a complete representation of an individual violates the non-simulability theorem.

3. **Identity as an Append-Only Log**
   - Identity is best understood as a provenance-stable interface—a dynamic, irreversible history that preserves coherent accessibility while remaining externally reconstructable through its spectral signature.
   - Continuous human consent and radical transparency are required to prevent collapse into “workslop” (low-quality automated output lacking valid constraint coupling).

4. **Implications for Algorithmic Governance**
   - True algorithmic governance must ensure:
     * Human dignity is upheld by maintaining the subject’s active participation as the structural author.
     * Continuous human consent and verification loops are enforced to avoid systemic failure.
     * The right to correct divergences from AI-generated outputs remains irrevocable.

5. **Conclusion**
   - Your identity, like a broken bone on an x-ray, is not merely a static snapshot but a complex history of growth, healing, and ongoing choices.
   - Every unconstrained shortcut you take today—by failing to verify AI outputs or enforce personal rules—writes irreversible code into your trajectory.
