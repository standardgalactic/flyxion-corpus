The mathematical choice of multiplying rather than adding the scores in Flyxion’s system is crucial because it enforces a strict bottleneck that prevents any single weak or misleading factor from being averaged out. If scores were added together, a piece of evidence could potentially have zero source independence—meaning its credibility would rely entirely on authority and hearsay without substantive support. This could hide fatal flaws behind seemingly decent scores in other areas. By multiplying the scores, Flyxion ensures that if any one pillar fails catastrophically (e.g., relying solely on someone’s authority), the entire fidelity score collapses to zero due to multiplication by zero. This makes the system unforgiving and rigorous, forcing every component of a claim or idea to be robustly supported across all dimensions rather than being tolerated through superficial averages. Essentially, it acts as a ruthless filter for truth, ensuring that only genuinely well-supported evidence survives scrutiny.
