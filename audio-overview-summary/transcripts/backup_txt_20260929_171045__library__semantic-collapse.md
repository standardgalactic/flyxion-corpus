**Summary**

The essay “How the Presumption of Atheism by Way of Semiotic Square of Opposition Leads to a Semantic Collapse” argues that redefining atheism as merely the absence of belief in God (rather than an active disbelief) causes a semantic collapse. Using Flew’s proposal, McRae shows that this blurred definition allows individuals to be simultaneously classified as atheists, theists, and agnostics—overlapping categories that lose distinct meanings. The semiotic square of opposition is employed to demonstrate how logical relationships between terms (e.g., atheism vs. theism) become contradictory when “weak” forms are accepted, leading to confusion in philosophical discourse. McRae contends that preserving clear distinctions among atheism, theism, and agnosticism is essential for meaningful debate; otherwise communication degenerates into a “semantic fog.” The work thus serves as a call for linguistic precision within academic dialogue.
