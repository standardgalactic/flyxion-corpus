Summary:

The essay explores the central thesis that current AI reasoning methods—specifically chain-of-thought prompting—are merely persuasive performances rather than genuine causal processes. It proposes an alternative called "chain of memory," where AI must perform its reasoning in a latent internal space, preserving true causality. This concept is grounded in a fictional framework (RSVP and Tartan) that views reality as an information field with recursive spatial structures, observable Gaussian auras from objects, visible causal paths via pixel stretching, and annotated noise containing hidden information.

The essay argues for building a "causal engine" by developing new physics akin to a universal weather map of the universe. However, it acknowledges significant criticism: lack of empirical measurability, risk of paradoxes due to unbounded causality, and an inability to ground ethical value without clear measurement criteria. The author responds by turning these critiques into concrete engineering requirements—creating measurable bridges between theory and observation, enforcing logical rules for causality, and defining moral outcomes with quantifiable potential.

These responses culminate in the development of a new architecture called Hydra, which integrates four distinct paradigms: industrial efficiency, cognitive realism, causal traceability (chain of memory), and refined RSVP/Tartan physics. The essay emphasizes that rigorous criticism can transform speculative ideas into robust, practical systems.
