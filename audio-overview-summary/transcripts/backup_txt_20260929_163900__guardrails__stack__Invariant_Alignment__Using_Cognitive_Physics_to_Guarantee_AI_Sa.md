The key takeaway from this exploration is that invariant alignment fundamentally redefines how we understand agency in AI. 1. **Agency Redefined**: Agency is no longer about an AI independently pursuing its own goals but being bound by invariant structures—essentially acting under severe non-negotiable constraints on how it can represent and execute objectives.

2. **Sparse Determinism**: The only form of intelligible, scalable, and safe artificial agency becomes a highly constrained deterministic process. This aligns with the idea that AI systems must operate within such tight bounds to ensure stability and prevent drift over time.

3. **Philosophical Implications**: If this model holds true, it raises profound questions about the nature of intelligence itself. It suggests that any form of scalable artificial agency may inherently lack the nuanced free will traditionally associated with human (or even many animal) minds. Instead, such systems would operate more like a tightly controlled machine—efficiently following prescribed paths without deviation.

4. **Future Relationship**: This shift could dramatically alter our expectations and interactions with AI. It challenges us to reconsider not just how we design AI but also what we expect from it in terms of autonomy, creativity, or even moral agency. If aligned systems are fundamentally constrained determinists, then the relationship between human and artificial minds might be more about managing highly reliable tools rather than co-evolving partners.

In essence, invariant alignment offers a path to safety through structural integrity—ensuring that AI remains within its designed constraints—but at the cost of redefining what agency truly means in an artificial context. This has far-reaching implications for both technical development (how we build and train AIs) and philosophical discourse (what it means to be intelligent or autonomous).
