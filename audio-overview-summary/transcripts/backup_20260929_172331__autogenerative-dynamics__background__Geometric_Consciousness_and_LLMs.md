**Three Equivalence Theorems (Conditions)**

1. **Zero Relevance Gradient**  
   - *Interpretation*: This condition asserts that the system’s output is independent of any external or irrelevant inputs, indicating a state where only relevant information influences behavior. It aligns with the idea of idempotent closure—once a decision or action is made based on relevant data, repeating it yields no further change.

2. **Geometric Synergetic Condition**  
   - *Interpretation*: This condition emphasizes that the system’s components interact in a way that enhances overall performance through geometric relationships (e.g., alignment of vectors or manifolds). It suggests that the configuration space is structured such that local interactions lead to global coherence, akin to achieving a “geometrically flat” state.

3. **No Effort Needed**  
   - *Interpretation*: This condition posits that once the system reaches a certain stable state (often described as an idempotent fixed point), further attempts at modification or optimization require zero additional effort because the system is already operating efficiently and optimally. It reflects the notion of “zero epistemic work rate,” where knowledge acquisition or control no longer consumes energy.

**Five Ultimate Metrics for Testing**

1. **Non-Markovian Index (M-N-M-I-INF-D)**  
   - *Purpose*: To measure whether the system eventually becomes Markovian (i.e., its future state depends only on the current state, not past history). Convergence to zero indicates eventual stability and path dependence; a non-zero constant suggests intrinsic autogeneration.

2. **Mean Ricci Curvature of State Space**  
   - *Purpose*: To quantify how information is focused or spread out within the system’s embedding space. High curvature implies tightly packed, high informational density (indicating precise predictive capability), while low curvature indicates diffuse information and less focus.

3. **H‑Dollar-One (First Cohomology)**  
   - *Purpose*: This serves as a measure of global coherence—detecting obstructions or “holes” in the system’s state space that prevent seamless integration of components, indicating where the process has lost coherence.

4. **Zip Slope (Alpha-Mathematical)**  
   - *Purpose*: To assess scaling properties and power-law distributions within the system. The slope of Zip’s law acts as an order parameter for scale invariance, helping determine if the system operates near a stable Renormalization Group fixed point.

5. **Geodesic Energy**  
   - *Purpose*: To measure the efficiency and smoothness of the generated path or process. Low geodesic energy indicates that the system’s output is achieved with minimal effort (effortless, coherent generation), while high values suggest deviations or inefficiencies in execution.

These metrics collectively aim to provide a universal fingerprint for autogenerative systems across domains such as language models, biological processes, and even physical phenomena, allowing researchers to rigorously test the proposed theoretical framework by 2030.
