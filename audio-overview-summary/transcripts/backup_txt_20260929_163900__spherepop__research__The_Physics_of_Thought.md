**Core Theoretical Shift: From Static States to Trajectory‑Based Intelligence**

The central insight of Active Geodesic Inference (AGI) is a profound shift from viewing intelligence as residing in static states, symbols, or instantaneous decisions—akin to “maximizing a static objective function”—to recognizing that **intelligence fundamentally resides in the structural integrity and coherence of trajectories** through which a system navigates its environment. This means:

1. **Intelligence ≠ Final Output**: It’s not merely about achieving an optimal score or compressing information; it’s about how the system moves, maintaining stability and low‑action (energy) across time and perturbations.

2. **Dynamic Geometry of Cognition**: AGI posits that cognition should be aligned with fundamental physical principles—persistence, structural stability, and coherence in thought geometry rather than just local maximization or compression.

**New Metrics for Measuring Intelligence**

Given this shift, traditional evaluation metrics (accuracy, instantaneous loss, single‑point reward) become insufficient. Instead, AGI proposes:

1. **Geodesic Width (Corollary G.4)**: Quantifies the volume of low‑action valleys—i.e., the breadth of possible trajectories connecting inputs to outputs while staying below a fixed action threshold. High geodesic width predicts robust generalization under perturbations.

2. **Trajectory Degeneracy**: Measures the number of distinct internally coherent histories (semantic isomers) that can produce the same external behavior. High degeneracy indicates resistance to faithful distillation, as extracting one path may fail to capture overall stability.

3. **Phase Transition Sharpness**: Assesses how abruptly performance collapses when system constraints are varied (e.g., shrinking context windows or increasing noise). AGI predicts non‑analytic behavior—sharp transitions signaling synchronization loss across cognitive dimensions of the effective Ising model.

**Empirical Evidence: The Bruhat Interval Discovery**

A concrete illustration of these principles is demonstrated by Google DeepMind’s AlphaVolv, which was optimized for maximizing a Delian variant (an energetic stabilization measure) rather than traditional performance metrics. Its discovery of a large hypercube‑isomorphic Bruhat interval within the symmetric group exemplifies:

- **Guided Along Low‑Action Geodesics**: The AI naturally found a highly coherent internal structure by optimizing for energetic stability, carving out a stable geometric solution in an otherwise chaotic space.
  
- **Non‑Linear Growth and Surprising Structure**: Unlike previous examples showing only linear growth, this discovery revealed structures that grow superlinearly, far exceeding human intuition.

This empirical success underscores the AGI framework: when systems are allowed to actively maintain low‑action geodesics within dynamically evolving configuration spaces, they inherently develop robustness and generalization—qualities not captured by static output scores. Thus, intelligence is fundamentally about **the capacity to sustain viable, low‑energy trajectories** rather than merely achieving a final destination.
