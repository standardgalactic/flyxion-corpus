# kitbash/paradigms/Reverse_Dominance_and_Federated_AI__How_Systems_Fight_Entropy_f

**Summary**

The audio overview discusses how both ancient egalitarian societies and modern federated AI systems manage disorder (entropy) by actively suppressing individual selfishness. In prehistoric hunter‑gatherer groups, “reverse dominance hierarchy” kept ambitious individuals in check through psychological pressure—mockery (“big chief”) and social isolation—ensuring liberty rather than stifling talent. When ridicule failed, execution served as a final sanction, but always within the community to prevent revenge cycles.

In contemporary AI, federated learning (federated averaging) mirrors this principle: devices train locally on their own data using stochastic gradient descent, then share only model updates with a central server that averages them without ever seeing raw data. Differential privacy further protects individual privacy by adding calibrated noise, ensuring the collective model improves while each participant’s information remains hidden.

The overview also introduces the Relativistic Staler Vector Plenum (RSVP) framework, which models order as emerging from three interacting fields—capacity (storage), vector flow (attention), and entropy (disorder)—and shows how complexity unfolds through stages: adaptive attention (pi‑2), creative bifurcation (pi‑3), cooperative synchronization (pi‑4), and reflexive metacognition (pi‑5). These concepts suggest that maintaining order is a universal, active process in both human societies and AI systems.

**KEYWORDS:** egalitarianism, reverse dominance hierarchy, ridicule, shunning, execution, federated learning, federated averaging, differential privacy, social sanctions, disorder, entropy, cooperative intelligence, RSVP framework.
