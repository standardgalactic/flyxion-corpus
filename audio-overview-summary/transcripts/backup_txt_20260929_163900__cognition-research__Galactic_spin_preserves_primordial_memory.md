**Koopman Operator – What It Does**

The Koopman operator is a mathematical tool that lets us study dynamical systems—like galaxies—without having to track every single star’s exact position and momentum. Instead of focusing on the chaotic, high‑dimensional motion of billions of individual particles (which would be impossible due to entropy), we look at how observable properties evolve over time.

1. **From Physical State to Observable Functions**  
   - In a galaxy, tracking each star’s trajectory is impractical because of its immense complexity and non‑linear dynamics.  
   - The Koopman operator shifts the focus to measurable quantities (e.g., total angular momentum) that describe how the system behaves globally.

2. **Linearizing Chaos**  
   - Although the underlying physics is chaotic and non‑invertible, the evolution of these observable functions can be expressed in a strictly linear way within this new mathematical space.  
   - This linearity opens up powerful techniques from linear algebra (e.g., spectral decomposition) that we cannot apply to the original nonlinear equations.

3. **Spectral Decomposition**  
   - The Koopman operator decomposes the evolution of the galaxy into two distinct subspaces:  
     a. **Mixing Subspace:** Where information genuinely dissipates and becomes lost to chaotic interactions (think of it as “the messy room” where memory decays exponentially).  
     b. **Peripheral/Unimodular Subspace:** Observables here do not mix or decay; they evolve quasi‑periodically, corresponding to the pure point spectrum.

**Implications for Relational Memory**

- If a galaxy’s observable (like its spin) projects onto the peripheral subspace, it means that relational information—such as the primordial misalignment of the proto‑halo—is preserved even though most matter is scrambled by chaos.

**Broader Astrophysical Impact**

This framework transforms our view of galaxies from isolated objects into “cosmological archaeology” tools. We can now:

- Detect indirect signatures of early universe phenomena (e.g., ancient neutrinos) by analyzing subtle variations in galaxy rotation patterns.
- Understand that massive, battered structures still carry measurable signals from the beginning of time, acting as lossy historical channels.

**Conceptual Types of Cosmological Remnants**

Flyxion classifies remnants into four types:

1. **State Remnants:** Original configurations survive (e.g., the cosmic microwave background).  
2. **Statistical Remnants:** Only broad distributions remain (e.g., large‑scale distribution of matter).  
3. **Relational Remnants:** Specific links between variables persist even if original components are lost (galaxy spin as a memory of primordial tidal fields).  
4. **Dynamical Remnants:** Survival is a signature of ongoing processes (e.g., galaxy rotation reflecting early gravitational interactions).
