**Calculus of Commitment Summary**

The core idea behind Flixixion’s “Calculus of Commitment” is that computation should be viewed not as a linear sequence of steps (doing step one, then step two), but rather as the disciplined reorganization and management of structural freedom—essentially, building shapes in a mathematical space. This perspective emphasizes:

1. **Pure Functional Programming:**  
   - Achieved through deterministic replay and referential transparency, where every merge operation is recorded without erasing past states.  
   - Acts like preserving total information, akin to keeping all history intact for auditability.

2. **Mutation as the Villain:**  
   - Mutation destroys historical records (the “collapse without history”), making it impossible to rewind or trace back to previous states.  
   - This loss of provenance leads to debugging challenges and system chaos due to entropy.

3. **Immutable Data Structures & Monads:**  
   - Immutable data structures maintain the integrity of state, preventing accidental overwrites.  
   - Monads are defined as “delayed collapse,” allowing side effects (like printing) to be represented as plans that execute at runtime without contaminating pure logic.

4. **Algebraic Effects & Handlers:**  
   - Decouple the core logic from its consequences by delegating effect handling to pluggable handlers, enabling flexible and testable code without altering the base structure.

5. **Continuation Passing Style (CPS):**  
   - CPS explicitly manages when collapses occur by passing continuations as function arguments, ensuring control over the timing of side effects and maintaining a clear separation between planning and execution.

**Bumper Sticker Takeaway:**  
*“Computation is geometric; think in terms of building shapes rather than linear recipes.”* This shifts focus from procedural steps to managing structural freedom, leading to more resilient and maintainable systems.
