**Template Capture**

Template capture occurs when an early dataset—often imperfect or biased—is used to create a template that becomes entrenched in the system. This flawed template works well enough for compression purposes but leads to persistent inaccuracies because it’s too deeply embedded to be easily replaced later, even if better templates are discovered.

**Hallucinated Economy**

In lossy compression regimes (like JPEGs), the archive may become overly confident in its current template and discard residual details that could help distinguish between similar objects. This “hallucinated economy” causes the system to render a highly detailed but factually incorrect image when decompressed, as it throws away information deemed unnecessary for saving space.

**Distortion Lattice**

To mitigate these issues, Flyxion proposes maintaining a distortion lattice—a mechanism that forces the archive to retain a minimum amount of raw residuals. This ensures the model remains falsifiable and prevents drift into hallucinated realities by acting as an empirical anchor against which inaccuracies can be detected and corrected.

**Scientific Resistance Analogy**

The resistance seen when new, superior data (like LIDAR scans) is introduced mirrors human sociological phenomena—scientists often resist paradigm shifts due to career investments in older models. This highlights how deeply entrenched templates can compete for computational resources and memory allocation, leading to a form of scientific resistance.

**Tartan Discipline**

A practical experimental tool called Tartan discipline visually tracks residual anomalies through heat maps, allowing engineers to monitor the clustering density of these anomalies in real time. This provides a live diagnostic view into the archive’s “curiosity” fields, helping identify and manage collapse waves that occur during theory change.

The discussion concludes with philosophical reflections on memory and identity: if human memory functions like a rematching archive, our recollections may not be fixed but continuously refined through new experiences.
