**Implications for Classical Physics**

This scenario illustrates a key limitation of classical physics’ reliance on the Markov property—assuming that the current state contains all necessary information to predict future states and that past history is irrelevant. In reality, as demonstrated by Flyxion:

- The *state space* must include **gluing data** (the compatibility structure assembled along the path), which captures how a system arrived at its present configuration.
- History matters because two systems can occupy identical instantaneous states yet have vastly different histories that affect their future evolution.

**Broader Significance**

The compensation pulse technique exemplifies how non-reversible, topological changes in state space (due to discrete events like entropy shocks) create unique paths that cannot be perfectly reconstructed or reversed. This has profound implications:

- **Irreversibility:** It underscores that irreversibility is not merely a consequence of time’s arrow but an intrinsic property arising from the geometry and topology of path-dependent observables.
