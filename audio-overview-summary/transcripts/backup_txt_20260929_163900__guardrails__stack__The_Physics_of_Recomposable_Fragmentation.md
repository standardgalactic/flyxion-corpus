In contrast, educational flashcards work because they include explicit pointers to broader structural networks (M1), allowing learners to understand where each fragment fits within a larger knowledge map.

For example, a flashcard for Arabic morphology is a tiny, iterable fragment that can be reviewed quickly but still connects back to the overarching grammar rules of the language. This provides reconstruction pressure, pushing the learner toward deeper understanding and retention. Conversely, watching a 30-second TikTok video on quantum physics offers no such context; it remains a terminal fragment with no relation to broader scientific theories.

To survive in today’s information age while preserving cognition, we must rebuild that M1 structural layer for every subject of interest. This involves demanding structured content rather than merely consuming fragmented pieces without understanding their place within the larger knowledge framework.
