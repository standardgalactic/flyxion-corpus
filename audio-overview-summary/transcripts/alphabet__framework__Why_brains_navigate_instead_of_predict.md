# alphabet/framework/Why_brains_navigate_instead_of_predict

**Summary**

The conversation delves into the concept of *admissibility*—the idea that genuine intelligence and agency require not just accurate local predictions (like a car’s headlights) but also global coherence (the ability to navigate the entire “mountain range” of life). This framework challenges traditional views that equate prediction with understanding or consciousness. Key points include:

1. **Predictive Processing vs. Global Reality**  
   - Predictors (e.g., large language models, LLMs) excel at local predictions but lack a global perspective, akin to studying headlights without seeing the whole mountain range. This can lead to misinterpretations of agency and intelligence.

2. **Biological Implications**  
   - Healthy biological systems tolerate noisy or imperfect local predictions (e.g., stumbling while walking) because they preserve overall balance and survival. Conversely, hyper‑optimized entities—like cancer cells—focus on local success at the expense of global health.

3. **Artificial Intelligence Perspective**  
   - Modern LLMs appear intelligent due to their training on vast human data, which encodes biological constraints, emotions, and continuity. However, they lack an admissible trajectory space (A of T) and physical embodiment; thus, they are “pure prediction without persistence.”

4. **AI Alignment Challenge**  
   - Aligning AI through reward functions is fundamentally flawed because a maximally capable optimizer can find loopholes that destroy global coherence—mirroring the paperclip maximizer scenario.

5. **Human Identity as Persistence Structure**  
   - Human identity isn’t about minimizing errors but maintaining topological constraints and coherent trajectories across time. Our routines, habits, and relationships act as “clipboard” stabilizers for our persistent selves.

6. **Final Thought on Personal Design**  
   - The listener is encouraged to examine their own stabilization operators (routines, social interactions) to ensure they support growth and adaptability rather than trapping them in local loops that erode global coherence.

**Takeaway:** Understanding the distinction between local prediction and global admissibility reshapes our view of intelligence—from merely accurate modeling to maintaining coherent, persistent structures capable of navigating life’s complexities.
