### Key Concepts Explained

1. **Pareto Dominance**: This concept is used in decision theory to compare different options based on multiple criteria—like safety, steepness, length, etc. If one trail is better than another across all dimensions (e.g., Trail A is shorter but steeper than Trail B), they are considered mathematically incomparable because neither can be said to dominate the other universally.

2. **Tangential Margin**: This measures how safely a system’s trajectory points away from danger, independent of its ambient distance (like being physically far from a cliff). It highlights that safety isn’t solely about proximity but also about directionality and velocity—how quickly one is moving toward or away from potential hazards.

3. **Lipschitz Continuity**: This mathematical concept acts like a strict speed limit on how fast the rules governing a system can change. If the dynamics of a system are highly variable, even if you’re far from danger (high ambient distance), your tangential margin could be poor due to rapid changes in the environment.

4. **Preference Fields**: These come into play when multiple agents or systems share a common resource pool but have conflicting demands. Preferences only apply after admissibility is established—meaning one must first ensure that their demand can be met within the shared constraints before preferences are considered.

5. **Coordination Failure**: This occurs when individual viability (meeting personal goals) does not translate to joint viability (meeting collective goals). An example given is two cargo ships needing a payoff of six each, but only one unit of resource available in total—making it impossible for both to meet their demands simultaneously.

6. **Finite Horizon Volume**: A method developed by Flyxion to avoid the pitfalls of infinite measures by focusing on a limited time frame or set of states, making calculations more manageable and applicable to real-world scenarios.
