**Summary of Key Points**

1. **First Two Crises – Extraction and Legitimation:**
   - The model identifies two primary crises in capitalism: a financial surplus extraction crisis and a legitimation (political consent) crisis where the state is seen merely as an enforcer of inequality.
   - Historically, these crises are resolved by scaling up the system—moving production to peripheral developing countries via global labor arbitrage. This allows core wealthy nations to maintain stability temporarily.

2. **Third Crisis – Ecological:**
   - The third crisis is the ecological one, governed by thermodynamics and entropy. Unlike financial or political issues that can be “reset” (e.g., printing money), ecological crises cannot be mitigated through economic policy alone.
   - If background conditions (like ozone layer depletion) fall below critical biological thresholds, the system’s attractor collapses irreversibly on human timescales.

3. **Metabolic Rift:**
   - The concept of a “metabolic rift” describes how capitalism extracts value from nature at an unsustainable rate, leading to ecological degradation that cannot be compensated by economic adjustments alone.

4. **Transitioning Out of the Attractor:**
   - Simply changing personnel in charge (e.g., better central bankers) does not alter the structural imperative of capitalist equations.
   - Social movements act as bifurcation-seeking perturbations—small changes that can trigger a qualitative shift in system behavior, forcing the transition to a new attractor.

5. **Four Alternative Geometries for Post-Capitalist Society:**
   - **Cooperative Ownership:** Replaces profit accumulation with democratic allocation of surplus, optimizing for social utility rather than endless growth.
   - **Public Provisioning and Commons:** Guarantees basic human needs (healthcare, housing, education, UBI) outside market mechanisms, breaking the survival threshold constraint.
   - **Participatory Planning:** Expands policy dimensions to allow transparent democratic institutions to plan macroeconomics, undoing semantic entropy reduction.
   - **Ecological Embedding:** Imposes a strict steady-state constraint where resource use and waste emissions never exceed natural renewal rates, closing the open boundary.

6. **Constructive Dimension of Transformation:**
   - Transitioning requires building an alternative attractor (new cooperative channels, robust public commons) before breaking the current system.
   - Without this groundwork, leaving the capitalist valley leads to systemic collapse due to pure chaos and unmitigated entropy.

**Final Thought:**
The model urges us to view economic systems not just as neutral trading floors but as complex feedback-controlled physics engines. To change them, we must reshape their fundamental equations—building new societal structures that respect both human labor and ecological limits before the thermodynamic reality forces a collapse.
