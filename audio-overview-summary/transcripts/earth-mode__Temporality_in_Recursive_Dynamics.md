# earth-mode/Temporality in Recursive Dynamics

**Summary:**

The essay explores how time may not be a fixed backdrop but an emergent property shaped by recursive processes, drawing on concepts from the Diatribe Repository such as the Relativistic Scalar Vector Plenum (RSVP) and Tartan. Time is defined as "n," emerging from entropy and causal constraints within systems, modeled using the Dirac delta function to capture localized dynamics. RSVP derives temporal flow directly from entropic gradients and vector flux, akin to a river's flow emerging from its landscape.

Tartan provides a concrete framework for embedding recursion into spatio-temporal fields by breaking down scales into localized tiles with their own entropy levels and causation speeds. Frozen tiles act as memory modules or anchors for meaning in higher-scale recursive computations, echoing Julian Barber’s notion of time capsules—snapshots holding external meaning.

Thrisps are introduced as self-propagating, autocatalytic semantic entities that replicate information across new domains (e.g., memes), illustrating how ideas can be transferred and evolve within different realms. The essay applies these concepts to cognition, where emotions are seen not as static states but as dynamic shifts in planning and control fields.

In artificial intelligence, the discussion contrasts Chain of Thought with Chain of Memory, suggesting that true AI alignment may involve targeting key nodes or modulating interactions rather than understanding every internal detail. This perspective extends to biological systems like cortical columns, which act as local geometric operators transforming neural representations dynamically.

The broader implications include viewing storytelling and even cosmic structures through a lens of emergent time and recursion, proposing that the universe’s structure could arise from reintegration of decohered information over vast timescales via field coherence and semantic resonance. This framework informs practical applications like self-paced learning, where mastery is seen as iterative refinement along paths minimizing entropy.
