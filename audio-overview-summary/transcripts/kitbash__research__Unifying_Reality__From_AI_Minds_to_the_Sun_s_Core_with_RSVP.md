### 2. **Application to the Sun**

- **Observational Degeneracy:**  
  A lattice core inside the Sun would appear similar from afar to a simple point mass or fluid core, making it challenging to distinguish via traditional observations alone.

- **Helioseismic Signatures:**  
  To detect such an elastic core, helioseismology must look for specific acoustic signatures:
  - **Sheer Acoustic Avoided Crossings:** The presence of shear (twisting) waves from the lattice would interact with normal sound modes (P-modes), creating narrow avoided crossings in the frequency spectrum.
  - **Even Order Frequency Splittings:** Residual splits in frequencies (A2, A4 terms) due to boundary anisotropy could indicate a non-perfectly spherical core.
  - **G-mode Period Variations:** Gravity modes (G-modes) probing deeper into the core might show deviations in period spacings—potentially compressing typical spacing from ~25 minutes down to ~1 minute if a lattice is present.
  - **Radial Kink in Sound Speed Profile:** A distinct kink at the core’s edge, rather than a smooth transition, could signal an elastic boundary condition.
  - **Frequency Ratio Shifts (RSVP Scalar Entropy Effects):** Altered frequency ratios due to scalar entropy shifts might provide additional diagnostic power.
