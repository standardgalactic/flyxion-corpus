# backup_20260929_172331/alphabet/The Lie of the Discipline of Symbolic Logic

Summary:

The essay critiques symbolic logic as a superficial and bureaucratic discipline rather than genuine logical reasoning. It argues that what is taught in universities—formal proofs, truth tables, and axiomatized systems—is primarily about following established procedures without uncovering true dependencies among statements. The author claims this approach stifles deeper intellectual engagement, turning students into "cipher lawyers" who excel at routine tasks but lack substantive understanding or creative problem-solving skills. The essay also denounces historical attempts to automate logic (e.g., Tarski’s World), highlighting that automating existing fragmented logics wastes resources and intellect without improving outcomes. It emphasizes that truth tables and similar tools often provide trivial, misleading results, making manual reasoning more efficient for real arguments. Ultimately, the author views symbolic logic as a collection of protocol-like practices masquerading as rigorous discipline, perpetuated by comfort-seeking academics rather than genuine intellectual merit.
