# throwback/essays/How_Systems_Survive_Through_Constant_Repair

**Title: The Universal Law of Admissibility and Repair**

---

### **Introduction**

In our exploration today, we delve into a profound concept that transcends disciplines—from geology to personal psychology—highlighting how systems manage continuity amidst disruption. This idea is encapsulated in the notion of *admissibility* and *repair*, suggesting that complexity thrives on resilience rather than static perfection.

### **Geological Perspective: Unconformities**

#### **James Hutton's Discovery at Sicker Point, Scotland (1788)**

- **Observation**: James Hutton observed an angular unconformity where near-vertical layers of older gray rock (Silurian mudstones) were overlain by younger reddish sandstone (Devonian red sandstone).
- **Implication**: This gap represented a period when violent tectonic forces uplifted, tilted, and eroded the older rocks before new sedimentation occurred. The unconformity thus encoded a significant historical rupture, not just a missing data point.

#### **The Great Unconformity in the Grand Canyon**

- **Observation**: Flat Cambrian strata sit atop tilted Proterozoic crystalline basement rock.
- **Implication**: This gap of over a billion years signifies a massive period where no sedimentary record existed. The erosion and subsequent deposition of red sandstone represent a geological repair event, reshaping the Earth's surface and potentially influencing biological evolution.

### **Philosophical Implications**

#### **Beyond Static Perfection to Dynamic Resilience**

- **Systems Thinking**: Complex systems operate under strict filters that decide what is allowed to proceed. When disruptions occur (e.g., tectonic shifts), active mechanisms repair the trajectory, ensuring continuation.
- **Resilience Over Perfection**: This shift from demanding static perfection to managing dynamic resilience challenges traditional views of stability and impermanence as failures.

### **Personal Identity: A Trajectory Rather Than a Snapshot**

#### **Psychological Perspective on Admissibility Regime**

- **Dynamic Continuity**: Viewing personal identity as an admissibility regime rather than a fixed snapshot allows us to understand life's changes—forgetting memories, repairing narratives after trauma—as integral parts of growth.
- **Repair Mechanisms**: Breaks in memory or self-image are not failures but opportunities for the mind and body to knit themselves back together, enabling forward movement.

### **Conclusion**

The exploration of unconformities in geology serves as a metaphor for understanding disruptions across all domains—be it supply chain crises, legal contracts, sleep patterns, immune responses, or personal identity. Embracing this universal law of admissibility and repair encourages us to view challenges not as failures but as essential components of our ongoing journey toward resilience and continuity.

**Call to Action**: Next time you face a breakdown in your life, consider it the raw material for future growth rather than a flaw. This mindset can transform adversity into opportunity, fostering both personal and systemic resilience.
