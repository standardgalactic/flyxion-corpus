This deep dive into neuroscience and psychoanalysis reveals how our brains operate as prediction machines designed to minimize free energy—surprise—and maintain homeostasis through unconscious strategies developed in early childhood. Here’s a summary of the key points:

1. **Prediction Error and Emotional Response**: When we encounter unexpected or threatening situations (like losing a caregiver), it triggers a massive prediction error, leading to intense emotional responses such as panic. This spike in free energy is felt as unpleasure, indicating that emotions are not just byproducts but essential for processing uncertainty.

2. **Brain’s Prioritization Function**: The midbrain acts like a decision triangle, feeling the effects of various states (hunger, tiredness, social worries), deciding which emotional system experiences the worst prediction error, and prioritizing it to focus on solving that problem. This mechanism helps us allocate attention where it matters most during uncertainty.

3. **Repression as a Survival Mechanism**: For human babies, basic animal instincts are insufficient for navigating complex social environments due to premature birth. Repression becomes necessary—a non-declarative action plan stored in the basal ganglia—allowing children to cope with overwhelming prediction errors without fully understanding them. This strategy is automized and deeply weighted, making it hard to modify later.

4. **Clapper-Ed Experiment**: Edward Clapper-Ed’s experiment with a woman suffering from severe amnesia demonstrated how subcortical memory (amygdala) can retain fear responses despite the loss of conscious declarative memories. This shows that emotional learning operates outside our conscious awareness, highlighting the power and persistence of non-declarative memory.

5. **Juisan’s IS Surplus Prediction Error**: The concept of surplus prediction error—unresolved tension in the brain—is not a flaw but a vital component of human existence. It drives creativity, desire, and growth by keeping us engaged with life rather than seeking an impossible state of perfect calmness or homeostasis.

6. **Therapeutic Implications**: Viewing mental health through this lens suggests that therapy’s goal should be to safely provoke new prediction errors, allowing the brain to rewire itself in a therapeutic environment. This approach acknowledges that our old strategies are often ineffective in adulthood and emphasizes the importance of embracing uncertainty as part of being human.

7. **Philosophical Shift**: The idea challenges traditional psychoanalytic views by proposing that consciousness resides in affective processes (brainstem) rather than solely in the neocortex, where unconscious predictions reside. It suggests a healthy mind is one capable of tolerating and creatively engaging with its contradictions, rather than striving for flawless predictability.

In essence, this exploration underscores that our struggles are not signs of weakness but manifestations of an adaptive brain trying to navigate an inherently unpredictable world using limited tools developed in early life. Embracing uncertainty might be the key to a more vibrant, neurologically alive existence.
