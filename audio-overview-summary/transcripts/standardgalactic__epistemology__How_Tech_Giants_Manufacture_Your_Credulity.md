**Goodhart’s Law & Proxy Integrity Collapse**

Goodhart’s law states that when a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure. In the context of social platforms, this manifests as algorithms optimizing for engagement metrics—watch time, shares, clicks—rather than true quality or nuance of content. Because these proxies (like “likes” or “views”) are easy to manipulate and quantify, they become targets that distort what is actually valuable. For example, a video claiming AI will save us garners massive visibility because it provokes an emotional response without needing any substantive evidence; the algorithm rewards this frictionless appeal over rigorous analysis.

**Visibility Field Theory**

Visibility field theory explains how ideas spread fastest when stripped of all “friction”—contextual information, disclosures about conflicts of interest, or critical evaluation. An idea that can be consumed quickly and emotionally (e.g., a 15-second clip promising salvation) is prioritized by algorithms over longer, more nuanced content because it demands less cognitive effort from the user. This creates an environment where sensationalism and superficial claims dominate public discourse.

**Implications for Credibility**

The combination of Goodhart’s law and visibility field theory means that in our current digital ecosystem, authenticity (being proven wrong) is penalized, while hype (being right by default) is rewarded. Consequently, we see a proliferation of “fake news” or exaggerated claims that are easy to share but lack substantive evidence. This undermines the very foundations of scientific inquiry and public discourse, as what gets amplified is often less about truth than it is about maintaining engagement.

**Defending Against Manipulation**

To counteract these effects, Flyxion proposes four non-negotiable requirements for identifying genuine epistemic authority:

1. **Domain Specificity:** Real experts clearly delineate the limits of their knowledge and avoid overgeneralization across unrelated fields.
2. **Falsifiability with Accountability:** Claims should be testable against measurable outcomes; failure to meet these predictions is acceptable, even celebrated, as it demonstrates scientific rigor.
3. **Structural Self-Disclosure:** Any financial or vested interests must be openly disclosed alongside claims, preventing hidden agendas from influencing perception.
4. **Genre Accountability:** Recognizing that various media formats (podcasts, AI summaries, biographies) serve ideological purposes and are not neutral conveyors of truth.

By applying these criteria, we can better discern which information is likely to hold up under scrutiny versus what is merely a product of the current attention economy’s incentives for sensationalism. This approach helps maintain critical thinking as a counterbalance to the pervasive influence of frictionless narratives that dominate our digital environment today.
