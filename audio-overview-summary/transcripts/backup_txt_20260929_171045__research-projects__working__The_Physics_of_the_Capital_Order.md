**Summary of Key Points**

1. **Capitalism as a Dynamical System:**  
   - The global economy is modeled as a complex dynamical system with attractors representing stable states (e.g., capitalist stability).  
   - Two primary crises—financial and legitimation—are managed by scaling up the system, exporting surplus energy through offshoring and labor arbitrage.

2. **The Third Crisis: Ecological**  
   - Unlike financial or political crises that can be “reset” via policy adjustments (e.g., printing money), ecological issues are governed by physical laws (thermodynamics).  
   - The second law of thermodynamics imposes a metabolic rift, where the Earth’s finite resources cannot sustain perpetual growth.

3. **Irreversibility of Ecological Collapse:**  
   - If background conditions (like ozone layer or pollinator populations) fall below critical thresholds, collapse is mathematically irreversible on human timescales.  
   - This makes the attractor shrink and eventually disappear, leading to systemic breakdown rather than a simple “reset.”

4. **Transitioning Out of the Attractor:**  
   - Simply changing technocratic leadership does not alter structural imperatives; movements must act as bifurcation-seeking perturbations—large enough shifts that force the system into a new regime.

5. **Four Alternative Geometries for Post‑Capitalist Society:**
   - **Cooperative Ownership:** Replaces profit accumulation with democratic allocation of surplus, optimizing social utility instead.
   - **Public Provisioning & Commons:** Guarantees basic human needs (healthcare, housing, education, UBI) outside market mechanisms, breaking the survival threshold constraint.
   - **Participatory Planning:** Expands policy dimensions to allow transparent, democratic macroeconomic planning, undoing semantic entropy reduction.
   - **Ecological Embedding:** Imposes a steady‑state constraint where resource use and waste emissions never exceed natural renewal rates, closing the open boundary.

6. **Constructive Dimension of Transformation:**  
   - Transition must be proactive; merely breaking the current system without building an alternative attractor risks systemic collapse into chaos.
   - The new “valley” (alternative economy) must be constructed before the boulder leaves the old valley to avoid total systemic failure.

**Final Thought:**  
Viewing economic systems through a lens of complex dynamical equations reveals that they are not neutral trading floors but feedback‑controlled physics engines. To alter this reality, one must rethink fundamental societal structures—cooperative ownership, public provisioning, participatory planning, and ecological embedding—to prevent the inevitable thermodynamic collapse.
