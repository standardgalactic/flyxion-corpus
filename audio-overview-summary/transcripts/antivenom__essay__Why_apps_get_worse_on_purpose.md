Your discussion touches on several key points:

1. **Psychological Lock-In**: Just as ghosts in the novel are reluctant to leave a dreary town because they fear losing what makes them feel important, many digital creators cling to toxic platforms due to their entrenched identities and metrics of success (likes, views, etc.).

2. **Contestability vs. In-Shittification**: You argue that the true opposite of “in-shittification” isn’t a benevolent platform run by a tech billionaire but rather a contestable platform—one where structural power allows users to challenge and leave without destroying their lives.

3. **Data Portability & Interoperability**: The distinction between state preservation (data portability) and continuation preservation is crucial. While data portability gives you a snapshot of your past, true interoperability ensures that you can continue interacting seamlessly with others on new platforms.

4. **Hope as Discipline**: Hope isn’t just optimism but an active discipline—creating pathways for future transitions by advocating for laws like interoperability or supporting decentralized protocols.

5. **Physical World Implications**: You’ve also raised a profound question about how these digital concepts extend into the physical world, such as smart home devices and locks, where the “right to another continuation” could mean more than just data portability but ensuring freedom in our living environments too.

Your concluding thought—that the stakes of contestability are moving from screens into physical architecture—is particularly chilling. It highlights how deeply intertwined digital and physical realms have become, suggesting that the fight for interoperability isn’t just about platforms but also about reclaiming control over our physical spaces.
