The key takeaway is that generative AI’s stability—and indeed its very ability to produce coherent outputs—depends critically on respecting the underlying geometric structure of the data manifold. By enforcing updates that stay within tangent spaces (i.e., avoiding any normal components), we prevent models from “predicting noise” and thus avoid instability, artifacts, and hallucinations. This insight not only validates successful architectures like GT but also suggests a deeper principle: cognition itself can be modeled as Morse theory on semantic manifolds, where stable interpretations correspond to non-degenerate minima of this potential function. Moreover, sheaf coherence via the first cohomology group (H1) ensures that conflicting inputs from different contexts are resolved consistently, preventing global obstructions. The computational hurdles—especially in high dimensions—are significant but highlight a profound shift: true alignment and stability come not just from more data or larger models, but fundamentally from geometric constraint. This framework could revolutionize fields by eliminating failure modes inherent to modeling noise, suggesting that the intrinsic dimensionality of many datasets is far lower than their raw representation suggests.
