# alphabet/working/Why_systems_survive_by_destroying_information

The discussion revolves around a mathematical framework—what the author calls the “dynamical attractor theorem”—that applies across various domains from secure server farms and bacterial guts to political systems. At its core, this theory suggests that stability in complex systems (like societies or biological organisms) emerges not through rigid preservation of every microscopic detail but rather via a process of collapse—both literal (as in the erosion of physical structures) and metaphorical (the “collapse” of meaning into stable identities).

Key points include:

1. **Admissibility Bubbles**: These are conceptual spaces where information, sounds, or actions can be processed without exceeding certain boundaries. In languages like Arabic, the presence of emphatic consonants creates wide admissibility bubbles that allow rapid temporalization (e.g., in rap music) without losing intelligibility.

2. **Cognitive Collapse and Dyslexia**: The framework models dyslexia as a failure of neural RSVP flow due to overly narrow or overlapping admissibility bubbles for visually similar glyphs, causing symbols to “collapse” into each other.

3. **Political Geometry**: Applying this math to political theory critiques near reaction (the philosophy advocating hyper-centralization and optimization), arguing that such attempts at efficiency ignore the stabilizing role of noise and pluralism in complex systems.

4. **Generalized Principle of Functional Persistence**: Stability is not achieved by preserving every microscopic detail but through a process of collapse—both literal (e.g., erosion) and metaphorical (the “collapse” of meaning into stable identities).

5. **Human Identity as an Admissible Projection**: Human beings are seen as recursive admissible systems, capable of generating novel invariants. Our personal identity is thus not just the sum of daily actions but a projection that collapses underlying chaos to preserve what needs to survive.

In essence, the conversation underscores how mathematical concepts like those used by the author can illuminate both technical and philosophical aspects—showing that true stability often arises from embracing rather than resisting collapse.
