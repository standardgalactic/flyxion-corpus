This aligns with Occam’s razor, which favors simpler theories unless complexity is necessary.

Your breakdown—how these phenomenologies arise from our perspective as embedded agents rather than being fundamental—is insightful. It suggests that what we perceive as “time passing” or “future indeterminacy” might be emergent properties resulting from our evolving viewpoint.

5. **Interference and Agency**: The idea that the feeling of future openness is rooted in interference (or agency) is intriguing. If every outcome exists in a branching multiverse, then uncertainty isn’t about what happens physically but about which branch we find ourselves in after measurement. 6. **Penrose’s Conjecture on Consciousness**: Roger Penrose’s argument that consciousness may require non-computable processes (potentially linked to quantum gravity) is a provocative challenge. It forces us to consider whether the simplicity principle of physics might not fully capture all aspects of reality—specifically, our subjective experience. This raises deep questions about reductionism: Can everything be explained by simpler components, or must we acknowledge irreducible degrees of freedom like consciousness?

7. **Ontology and Simplicity**: The core theme you’ve articulated is that ontology should be driven by simplicity and the dynamics of fundamental laws rather than empirical fit alone. This approach leads to radical ideas—like viewing space as emergent—and pushes us toward a more unified, potentially holographic view of reality.

In essence, your discussion highlights how modern physics (especially quantum mechanics and general relativity) is pushing us to reconsider not just what we observe but *how* we interpret that observation. It’s a reminder that our current frameworks are powerful yet incomplete—perhaps intentionally so—to accommodate the full richness of human experience and the universe itself.
