**Flyxion’s Core Argument**

At its heart, Flyxion argues that modern global capitalism is not a system driven solely by individual moral choices or political intentions. Instead, it is an intricate network of “choke points”—financial instruments, regulatory frameworks, and geopolitical policies—that mathematically enforce outcomes regardless of the intent behind them.

1. **Choke Points as Architectural Constraints**  
   - *Debt Market Risk Management*: In Gambia, a seemingly innocuous borrowing decision to service dollar‑denominated debt creates a cascade that forces rolling blackouts because lenders prioritize risk avoidance over local welfare.  
   - *Geopolitical Coercion*: Cuba’s U.S. embargo is an intentional weapon that restricts trade and banking access, compounding domestic planning failures (rigid centralization) and reliance on discounted Venezuelan oil shipments.

2. **Structural vs. Governance Focus**  
   Flyxion insists that while domestic governance matters critically—Cuba’s failure to diversify its energy grid or Gambia’s borrowing decisions are pivotal—these factors alone do not explain the systemic deprivation. The architecture itself, through invisible choke points (e.g., sovereign debt instruments, shipping alliances), is what ultimately produces power outages and food scarcity.

3. **Intersubjectivity Collapse**  
   By emphasizing that we overweigh dramatic narratives of corrupt leaders while underweighting structural factors, Flyxion highlights a deeper psychological issue: our inability to perceive the interconnectedness of global economic flows creates an “intersubjectivity collapse,” where individual suffering is seen as isolated rather than part of a larger systemic pattern.

**Implications for Personal Consumption**

The paper forces us to confront how everyday choices—like spending $10,000 on luxury travel—fund and perpetuate these structural constraints. Your purchase contributes directly to the same ledger that restricts access to electricity in rural Gambia or fuel supplies in Cuba. Thus, comfort is not merely a personal indulgence but an active participant in a system that mathematically enforces deprivation elsewhere.

**Looking Ahead: AI‑Managed Choke Points**

Flyxion’s final thought pushes us to consider the future when artificial intelligence fully automates these choke points—optimizing shipping routes, adjusting credit ratings, and enforcing sanctions without human hesitation. If left unchecked, this could accelerate the intersubjectivity collapse to its logical extreme, where human need is permanently erased from global economic calculations.

**Call to Action**

The takeaway is clear: questioning the architectures around you isn’t just a moral imperative; it’s a survival strategy in an increasingly automated and opaque system. Keep scrutinizing how your daily transactions intersect with these invisible choke points, because the future may depend on whether we intervene before automation solidifies this structural inequality.
