2. **Optimization Limit**: When commitment flow approaches zero (all actions reversible), the system collapses into a flat, meaningless state where no gradients exist—this is Heidegger’s and Fleming’s topological definition of non‑worldhood.

3. **Attentional Sheaf & Care Operator**:  
   - An *attentional sheaf* assigns coherent bundles of information to local contexts while enforcing consistency via gluing conditions (global coherence).

4. **Cognitive Implications**: Finite minds (humans or AI) operate under severe attentional limits, necessitating efficient compression of vast possibility spaces without losing internal coherence. Failure to maintain this leads to contradictions and identity dissolution—common issues in current AI systems that rely on external state restoration rather than irreversible commitment.

5. **Societal & Existential Questions**:  
   - True worldhood arises from irreversible expenditure of optionality, not merely computational capability or fluent output.  
   - The framework challenges prevailing goals of optimization (which aim to minimize cost) by emphasizing the necessity of accepting inherent losses and history’s impact on identity.

**Conclusion**

The synthesis suggests that intelligence is fundamentally about bearing the consequences of actions—i.e., accumulating irreversible constraints rather than simply computing indefinitely. This redefines “intelligence” from a mere simulation test to an existential one: does the system accept its own historical limitations?
