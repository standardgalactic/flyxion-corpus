**Key Takeaways from the Discussion**

1. **Texture vs. Event Nodes:**  
   - *Texture nodes* represent mutable, reversible elements (e.g., content edits in a wiki).  
   - *Event nodes* correspond to irreversible constraints or policies that shape future possibilities—such as bans on editors or topics.

2. **Resilience Beyond Redundancy:**  
   - Traditional resilience measures focus only on texture redundancy (duplicate articles).  
   - The framework argues that true resilience also requires stability of the event-level structure; otherwise, even redundant content cannot restore lost adaptive capacity.

3. **Structural Fragility Example:**  
   - A restrictive sourcing policy can drive away most contributors, permanently altering the system’s structural capacity to generate new texture. Even if existing articles remain intact, the wiki’s ability to evolve is fundamentally constrained.

4. **Institutional Hysteresis:**  
   - Reversing a policy (event) does not automatically restore pre-reverse conditions due to path dependence and lost contributors/trust—demonstrating that institutional memory and resilience are governed by historical constraints, not just current content or rules.

5. **Detectable Causal Signatures for Event Nodes:**  
   - **Restoration Order Sensitivity** (Event Precedence Criterion): If restoring component A before B is necessary for recovery, then A operates at the event level relative to B.  
   - **Ablation Asymmetry**: Components causing catastrophic failure when partially removed but yielding minimal recovery upon partial restoration indicate structural commitment.  
   - **Training Time Stability (Hysteresis)**: Significant changes in stability after an event transition suggest irreversible commitment, akin to grokking.

6. **Rejection of Functionalism:**  
   - Mechanistic equivalence does not guarantee causal equivalence because functionalism ignores entropy and historical constraints. Systems that reach a state via irreversible commitments have fundamentally different future possibilities than those reaching the same state reversibly.

7. **Mechanism as Dual Channel:**  
   - Mechanisms involve both texture mediation (replayable variation) and event mediation (irreversible structural modification). Understanding a system requires reconstructing its sequence of commitments, not just observing outputs.

8. **Meta-Level Insight on Interpretability:**  
   - Successful mechanistic interpretation is an irreversible process; each confirmed mechanism rules out competing explanations, aligning your understanding with the system’s historical constraints rather than additive knowledge accumulation.

**Conclusion:**  
The framework emphasizes that true mechanistic understanding requires recognizing and accounting for irreversible structural commitments—events—that shape a system's future possibilities. This approach shifts focus from merely functional behavior to reconstructing the history of exclusions and foreclosures, highlighting how past decisions permanently constrain what can be done in the present or future.
