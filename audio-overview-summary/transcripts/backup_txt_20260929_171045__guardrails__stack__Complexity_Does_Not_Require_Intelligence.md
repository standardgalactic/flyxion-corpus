**Synthesis of Key Themes**

1. **Complexity as Affordance Landscape**:  
   Complexity isn’t an internal, abstract property residing in the mind; it’s a relational structure between an agent and its environment—its affordances. This shift from “what can be done now” to “how you learn to do it” reframes how we understand agency.

2. **Role of Constraints (Power of No)**:  
   Constraints, or limitations imposed by the world (e.g., physics, materials), are not mere obstacles but essential ingredients that shape behavior and generate complexity. They force systems into adaptive solutions, making them robust and capable.

3. **Relegation & Automation**:  
   By delegating routine tasks to automation—whether mechanical parts in a car or algorithms in software—we free cognitive resources for higher-level decision-making. This scaling of habitability allows complex behaviors to emerge without overwhelming the system with detail.

4. **Indeterminacy & Plurality**:  
   The attempt to rank all forms of complexity on a single human-centric scale is mathematically meaningless due to incomparability (parallel lines). Complexity manifests differently across systems; what matters isn’t a universal “most complex” but rather the unique fit between an agent and its affordances.

5. **Democratization of Agency**:  
   Recognizing that mindless, fluent agency can be just as sophisticated—perhaps even more so than conscious planning—shifts our appreciation from abstract intelligence to embodied skillfulness. This perspective encourages us to value natural coupling over artificial cognition.

**Homework Prompt**

Reflect on your daily activities and ask yourself:

- How much of the day is spent in a state of deliberate, conscious thinking versus automatic, fluent execution?
- In which situations do you feel most complex or capable—when actively planning versus when performing routine tasks without full awareness?

This exercise challenges the notion that intelligence equates to complexity and invites an appreciation for the elegance of unconscious skill.
