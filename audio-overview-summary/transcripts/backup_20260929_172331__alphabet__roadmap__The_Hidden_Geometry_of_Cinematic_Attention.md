The discussion you've shared delves deeply into the philosophical and psychological underpinnings of human experience, particularly as it relates to media consumption—films, video games, mathematical proofs—and extends these ideas to broader questions about mammalian behavior and human motivation. Here’s a breakdown of the key themes and insights:

### 1. **Proofs and Media as Constraint Structures**

- **Designers vs. Observers**: In both mathematical proofs and films, there is an inherent separation between the designer (who sets up the rules) and the observer (who navigates those rules). The "rules" in this context are akin to axioms or narrative constraints that dictate how a story or proof can progress.
  
- **Terminal vs. Traversal Reward**: The terminal reward refers to the ultimate outcome—knowing what happens next, solving a theorem, or completing a plotline. However, the primary psychological payoff often comes from traversing the complex structure itself—the journey is as rewarding as the destination.

### 2. **The Role of Constraint and Movement**

- **Constraint (State Space)**: The foundational axioms in mathematics or narrative beats in films define what is possible within that medium. These constraints are like a state space where movement is governed by logical rules.
  
- **Movement (Rules of Inference)**: Logical inference rules, such as modus ponens and transitivity, dictate how one can move from one statement to another in proofs or plot developments. The satisfaction comes from successfully navigating these rules.

### 3. **The Paradox of Re-watching**

- **Why Re-watch?**: Professional mathematicians re-read proofs they already know are true because the process itself is rewarding—tension, relief, and aesthetic satisfaction arise from mastering complex logical steps.
  
- **Human Behavior Extension**: This concept extends to media consumption where the pleasure lies in revisiting familiar structures rather than discovering new endings. It’s about the experience of navigating constraints again.

### 4. **Perfect Days as a Case Study**

- **Minimal Narrative**: The film "Perfect Days" by Wim Wenders exemplifies how minimal narrative can still be profoundly moving if it engages with constraint and traversal reward.
  
- **Constraint as Beauty**: Public restrooms, the physical spaces Hirayama cleans, become literal constraint structures. Watching him navigate these spaces repeatedly offers a deeply satisfying experience rooted in mastery of constrained movement.

### 5. **Biological Cognition and Human Motivation**

- **Friction and Reward**: The neurological reward derived from navigating complex constraints is akin to how we seek out challenging activities like rock climbing or learning languages.
  
- **Life as a Procedural Maze**: This perspective suggests that human motivation might not be about achieving the most efficient outcomes (terminal rewards) but rather seeking the challenge itself—the intrinsic pleasure of traversing non-trivial admissibility structures.

### 6. **Implications for Human Behavior**

- **Intentional Complication**: If our primary drive is to experience the friction and reward of navigating complex constraints, then many of our life choices—career paths, hobbies, relationships—are driven by this desire rather than a pursuit of efficiency or finality.
  
- **Wandering for Satisfaction**: We might intentionally seek out difficult experiences not because they lead us to some ultimate goal but because they provide the physiological and cognitive reward derived from overcoming constraints.

### Conclusion

The overarching message is that meaning in art, mathematics, and life often emerges as a byproduct of navigating complex structures rather than achieving an endpoint. This reframes our understanding of motivation—whether it’s watching a film multiple times or choosing challenging endeavors—to be fundamentally about the experience of traversing and mastering constraints.

This perspective invites us to reconsider what we value in life: is it primarily the destination, or do we derive fulfillment from the journey itself? It challenges conventional notions of efficiency and success by emphasizing that satisfaction can come from the process of navigating complexity.
