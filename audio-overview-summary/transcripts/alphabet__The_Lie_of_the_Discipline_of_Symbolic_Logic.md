# alphabet/The Lie of the Discipline of Symbolic Logic

Summary:

The essay critiques symbolic logic as a superficial and bureaucratic discipline rather than genuine logical reasoning. It argues that what is taught in universities—formal logic—is primarily about executing pre-existing formal systems (like propositional calculus) without fostering deeper understanding of how to organize statement sets to reveal true dependencies. The author claims this approach wastes intellectual effort, turning students into “human robots” who automate others' work rather than engage with the material critically. It highlights failures like Tarski’s World—a program intended to automate logic that proved more cumbersome than manual methods—and points out that even when algorithms succeed in specific contexts, they often require greater mental resources and still fail to address real-world arguments effectively. The essay concludes that symbolic logic persists largely because it provides a safe, low-stakes environment for “pencil-pushing geeks” who view it as comfortable thinking rather than substantive reasoning.
