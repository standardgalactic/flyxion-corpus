**Summary:**

The essay explores how both ancient human societies and modern artificial intelligence systems manage disorder and maintain cooperation by actively suppressing individual selfishness. In prehistoric egalitarian groups, "reverse dominance hierarchy" ensured that ambitious individuals were kept in check through psychological pressure such as ridicule and social isolation, preventing the rise of tyrannical behavior. Similarly, federated AI algorithms like Federated Averaging (FedAvg) enforce collective intelligence by allowing devices to train locally on their own data without sharing raw information, thus preserving privacy while still enabling a more accurate global model. This mirrors the ancient practice of using ridicule and ostracism as mechanisms to curb dominant behavior.

Both systems rely on active constraints—whether through social sanctions or differential privacy techniques—to prevent any single entity from dominating the collective intelligence. The essay draws an analogy between these historical and technological methods, suggesting that the fundamental mechanism for achieving coherent cooperation in both human and machine societies is the suppression of individual selfish signals to maintain group order.
