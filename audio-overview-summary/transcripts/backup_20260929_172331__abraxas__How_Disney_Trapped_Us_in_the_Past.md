# backup_20260929_172331/abraxas/How Disney Trapped Us in the Past

**Summary:**

The essay “How Disney Trapped Us in the Past” argues that Walt Disney’s empire has not only brought joy but also entrenched harmful cultural patterns. It highlights how Disney masterfully uses consumerist tactics—through merchandise, theme parks, and character merchandising—to condition children (and adults) into viewing happiness as a product of consumption rather than an intrinsic value. This distraction‑based entertainment sidesteps deeper engagement with reality by offering simplified narratives that promise easy resolutions.

Furthermore, the essay critiques Disney’s imperialistic expansion strategy, which mirrors traditional colonialism by promoting American cultural products globally and sidelining local traditions. Walt Disney himself embodied egotistical tendencies, positioning himself as a benevolent creator who embodies American ingenuity and superiority, thereby reinforcing Western dominance over non‑Western narratives.

Disney also plays a role in propagating military patriotism, especially evident during WWII with propaganda films that glorify enlistment and war efforts. This patriotic messaging normalizes unquestioned loyalty to the nation and its armed forces, often overlooking the moral complexities of warfare.

Finally, the essay points out Disney’s persistent use of cultural stereotypes—through portrayals like “Aladdin,” “Pocahontas,” and others—that distort historical and cultural realities, perpetuating biased perceptions and limiting understanding of diverse cultures. Overall, the thesis is that Disney’s influence traps audiences in outdated narratives of consumerism, imperialism, egotism, military patriotism, and stereotypical representations.
