1. **Semantic Media Representation**: The idea that future media—whether videos, photographs, or other forms of content—will be fundamentally represented as text files rather than raw pixel data or analog recordings. This shift implies that the "signal" (the original physical recording) becomes secondary to the underlying textual instructions.

2. **Photorealistic Text Editing**: It describes a scenario where one could literally edit reality by changing the text descriptions of media, leading to photorealistic alterations—such as altering camera angles in live broadcasts or changing character behaviors in movies post-production. This raises questions about authenticity and truth in media consumption.

3. **Ontology and Provenance**: The concept that if media is fundamentally just a set of textual instructions for AI-generated hallucinations, the traditional notion of provenance (the historical record of ownership and authenticity) collapses. A video could be argued to not truly represent reality but rather an algorithmic interpretation.

4. **Death of the Signal**: This phrase denotes the idea that the physical recording itself becomes obsolete as a fundamental unit of media. The focus shifts entirely to the textual representation, which can be edited or altered without any trace back to the original event captured in the real world.

5. **Epistemic Closure and Model Mismatch**: These terms refer to limitations inherent in generative models (like AI vision systems trained on specific datasets). If a model encounters something outside its training data—such as cultural practices not represented in its prototype library—it may fail to accurately represent that reality, leading to misinterpretation or loss of detail.

6. **Adversarial Attacks and Security Vulnerabilities**: The passage discusses how semantic compression could be exploited through adversarial techniques, where subtle visual patterns (like those on a dazzle camouflage jacket) can trick the AI into misidentifying objects in real-time video feeds, effectively "erasing" individuals from recorded scenes.

7. **Economic Consolidation and Computational Power Shift**: As media files become text-based, storage and transmission costs drop dramatically due to reduced bandwidth requirements. However, this shift concentrates power in companies that control the underlying generative models and physics engines used to decode these texts into visual reality.

8. **The Simulation Hypothesis**: By concluding with a reflection on whether future media might be entirely simulations generated by machines, it touches upon philosophical questions about our perception of reality and the potential for AI systems to develop their own hyper-compressed language that becomes incomprehensible to humans.

Overall, this discussion highlights both the transformative potential and significant challenges—ethical, legal, and existential—that come with moving towards a world where media is fundamentally represented as editable text rather than physical data. It invites critical reflection on how we perceive authenticity, truth, and ownership in an increasingly algorithmic reality.
