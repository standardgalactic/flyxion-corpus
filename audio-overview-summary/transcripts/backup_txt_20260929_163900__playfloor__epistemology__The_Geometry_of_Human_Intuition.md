**Title:** Understanding AI Hallucinations Through Geometry and Collective Wisdom

---

### Introduction

Artificial intelligence (AI) models like ChatGPT often produce “hallucinations”—statements that sound plausible but are factually incorrect or fabricated. This phenomenon arises because deep learning models operate as approximations of the admissibility quotient based solely on their training data, which Flyxion refers to as their corpus manifold. Essentially, an AI’s reality is confined by what it has been exposed to during its training.

### The Geometry Behind Hallucinations

1. **Training Data Limitation:**  
   An AI model only “knows” from the examples present in its training data. When presented with a prompt that deviates significantly from this dataset, the model lacks genuine understanding of the new input.

2. **Smooth Familiarity Potential Mechanism:**  
   To generate an output, the AI relies on a mechanism called smooth familiarity potential. It guesses based on similarities to previously seen inputs rather than true geometric comprehension. This leads to confident but inaccurate responses because it extrapolates from limited data points.

3. **Projection Supremacy:**  
   The model’s inability to recognize its boundaries results in “projection supremacy,” where it outputs fabricated information with unwarranted confidence, mimicking human-like certainty despite lack of factual basis.

### Human Cognitive Analogues

The same geometric principles apply to human cognition:

- **Interpretive Attractors and Projection Capture:**  
  Humans can develop rigid mental models that resist updates. When new contradictory data appears, the mind warps it to fit existing beliefs—a phenomenon akin to dynamic projection supremacy in AI.

- **Community Semantic Manifolds:**  
  People’s backgrounds—media consumption, life experiences, cultural filters—create distinct “semantic manifolds.” These affect how they perceive and interpret information. For instance, two individuals might watch the same video but focus on different elements based on their unique pasts.

### Wisdom Salons: A Solution to Epistemic Gridlock

To address these issues, Monica Anderson proposes a concept called **wisdom salons**:

- **Sheaf Theory Application:**  
  Sheaf theory provides a framework for stitching together local pieces of information into a coherent global picture. In the context of wisdom salons, this means breaking down complex debates into smaller groups that discuss and reduce specific aspects of an issue.

- **Collective Epistemic Reduction:**  
  Each group produces a “grain of wisdom”—a locally true statement based on shared experiences. These grains are then passed to other groups with different contexts, allowing ideas to survive parallel transport across diverse semantic manifolds.

- **Low Holonomy as Robust Truth:**  
  The Tartan descent theorem formalizes the idea that truly robust truths (wisdom) remain stable and intelligible when moved between different contexts. This mathematical property ensures that ideas are not merely echo chamber validations but genuinely universal truths.

### Practical Implications

1. **Audit Your Models:**  
   Continually question whether you’re mistaking your model of reality for the actual world itself. Are new pieces of evidence being forced to fit a rigid belief system?

2. **Embrace Openness:**  
   Actively seek out diverse perspectives and experiences that challenge existing assumptions. This helps prevent the formation of interpretive attractors.

3. **Healing Through Geometry:**  
   Recognizing anxiety as a geometric distortion—where one’s perception of accessible futures is artificially narrowed—suggests that healing involves expanding one’s cognitive space, much like allowing an AI to flow uphill rather than staying stuck in its limited training data.

### Conclusion

Understanding hallucinations through the lens of geometry and collective wisdom reveals deep parallels between artificial intelligence and human cognition. Both systems rely on approximations derived from past experiences, which can lead to overconfidence when faced with novel information or perspectives. By applying concepts like wisdom salons and sheaf theory, we can foster more robust forms of knowledge that resist projection supremacy and echo chamber effects, ultimately leading to better communication and understanding across diverse communities.
