The key takeaway from our discussion today is that while technological systems—whether they be blockchain ledgers, corporate infrastructures, advertising platforms, or child-targeted video games—can appear highly functional and efficient on paper (or in their digital logs), this does not automatically equate to genuine value creation for humanity. The critical criteria we must apply are:

1. **Global Boundary Costs**: Any system’s benefits must outweigh its total environmental, energetic, and material costs imposed on external populations who do not share in the upside.

2. **Structural Stability**: Systems should naturally gravitate toward stable, low-action configurations that promote utility and reduce volatility rather than perpetuating cycles of rapid reallocation and instability.

3. **Decrease in Global Work Functional**: The ultimate measure of success is whether a system reduces the overall effort required to navigate life—whether physically (through cleaner energy use), socially (by reducing bureaucratic overhead), or psychologically (by fostering genuine learning and growth).

Examples like supply chain verification using decentralized ledgers illustrate potential pathways where these criteria can be met, provided we are willing to compromise on ideological purity for greater efficiency. Conversely, many current applications of blockchain technology still rely heavily on proof-of-work mining, which incurs significant thermodynamic costs that offset logistical benefits.

Ultimately, the lesson is clear: We must shift our evaluation from internal elegance or superficial metrics (like user engagement or quarterly growth) to a holistic assessment of total global work functional. This means questioning whether any system truly advances human progress and environmental health, rather than merely maintaining flawless records or maximizing short-term gains for early insiders. The challenge lies in recognizing that perfection on paper does not guarantee real-world value—our focus should be on systems that genuinely reduce the effort required to live sustainably and ethically.
