# paracosm/audio-overviews/Reverse_Dominance_and_Federated_AI__How_Systems_Fight_Entropy_f

**Summary:**

The audio overview discusses how both ancient human societies and modern artificial intelligence systems manage disorder by actively suppressing individual selfishness, thereby maintaining collective order. In early egalitarian groups, a "reverse dominance hierarchy" was enforced through psychological pressure such as ridicule and social isolation to prevent dominant individuals from asserting control. This mirrors the way federated AI algorithms like Federated Averaging (FedAvg) maintain privacy and prevent centralization by allowing local model updates that are averaged without transmitting raw data, thus preventing any single entity from becoming an informational despot.

The concept of differential privacy is introduced as a method to ensure individual anonymity while still enabling collective learning. The overview also touches on the Relativistic Staler Vector Plenum (RSVP) framework, which models order and complexity through three interacting fields—capacity, attention, and entropy—and suggests that high disorder can sometimes catalyze new patterns of order, akin to a phase transition.

The overarching theme is that cooperation in any system requires active suppression of individual selfish signals to maintain coherence. This principle appears not only in human social structures but also in algorithmic implementations like federated learning, suggesting a universal mechanism for collective intelligence across biological and artificial systems.
