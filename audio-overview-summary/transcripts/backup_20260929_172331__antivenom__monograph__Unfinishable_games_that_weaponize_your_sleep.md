**Title: The Right to Absence – Designing Digital Systems for Safe Disengagement**

---

### Introduction

In today’s digital landscape, many applications—especially those targeting younger audiences or designed as persistent, social experiences—embed mechanisms that make it difficult for users to disengage safely. This phenomenon is often referred to as **temporal extraction**, where the system leverages psychological and social pressures to keep players (or workers) perpetually available. The paper under discussion introduces a concept called the **Right to Absence** to address this issue, proposing design criteria that ensure users can invoke a bounded absence interval without external dependencies.

### Core Problem: Inability to Safely Disengage

The fundamental problem highlighted is that many modern digital systems—particularly games and social platforms—are structured in ways that make it impossible for participants to safely step away. This inability stems from:

1. **Persistent Engagement**: Systems are designed to keep users engaged indefinitely, often through mechanisms like loot boxes or real-time updates.
2. **Social Pressure**: The expectation of immediate response within a group (e.g., guilds, teams) creates an unspoken obligation to remain available.
3. **Lack of Control Over Disengagement**: Users cannot simply log off without facing consequences such as lost progress, missed opportunities, or social repercussions.

### Proposed Solution: Right to Absence

The paper argues that the design solution must mathematically guarantee a participant’s ability to invoke a bounded absence interval where their exposure drops below a tolerable threshold. This concept is unilaterally enforced—meaning users can disengage without relying on others’ goodwill, time zone coordination, or collective decisions.

#### Key Requirements:

- **Unilateral Disengagement**: Users must be able to press a “leave” button that stops all consequences of their absence.
- **Mathematical Guarantee**: The system’s code should ensure that once the user leaves, there are no lingering effects (e.g., progress loss, penalties) unless explicitly designed otherwise by the game or platform.

### Application Beyond Gaming

While initially framed within gaming contexts, the Right to Absence principle extends far beyond. It applies to any digital environment where persistent engagement is a core feature:

- **Professional Communication Tools**: Slack, Teams, and similar platforms can enforce temporary offline modes that prevent users from being flagged as “away” or “offline,” reducing anxiety over missed communications.
- **Gig Economy Platforms**: Apps managing on-call workers could implement scheduled downtime periods to respect personal time boundaries without penalizing availability during shifts.
- **Financial Markets**: Real-time trading platforms might allow traders to set protected intervals where they are not alerted to market changes, preventing burnout and ensuring mental health.

### Implementation Challenges

Implementing the Right to Absence is complex due to several factors:

1. **Persistence vs. Immersion**: Making disengagement possible without destroying immersion requires careful design that maintains the core experience’s integrity.
2. **Global Accessibility**: Time zone differences complicate any fixed vulnerability windows, as seen with alliance attack declarations in games.
3. **Social Dynamics**: Mandatory role redundancy (as discussed) helps but demands high levels of social coordination and trust among players.

### Regulatory Implications

Given that temporal extraction is often invisible—unlike traditional gambling mechanisms—it poses significant challenges for regulation:

- **Regulatory Frameworks**: Existing laws targeting loot boxes or casino-like mechanics may not apply, as the extraction occurs through psychological manipulation rather than explicit monetary gain.
- **Occupational Standards**: Similar to labor rights in other industries, there might be a need for new standards that protect workers from digital fatigue and ensure fair access to disengagement.

### Conclusion

The Right to Absence is an essential concept for designing more humane digital systems. By ensuring users can safely step away without external dependencies or social penalties, it addresses both user well-being and the ethical implications of persistent engagement. As we continue integrating technology into all aspects of life—from professional communication to financial markets—embracing this principle could significantly enhance user experience while promoting healthier interaction patterns.

**Next Steps**: Further research should explore how different industries can adapt these principles without compromising their core functionalities, alongside discussions on potential regulatory pathways that respect the unique challenges posed by digital persistence.
