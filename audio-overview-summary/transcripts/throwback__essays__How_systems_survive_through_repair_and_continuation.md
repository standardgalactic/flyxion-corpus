# throwback/essays/How_systems_survive_through_repair_and_continuation

**Title: Continuation Across Gaps – A Universal Principle**

---

### **1. Introduction to Continuation**

Continuation is a fundamental concept that underlies many systems across various domains—from human biology and supply chains to planetary geology. It refers to the process of persisting or evolving despite disruptions, failures, or changes in conditions. The core idea is not about returning to an exact previous state but adapting and moving forward within new constraints.

### **2. Human Systems: Sleep and Repair**

#### **a. Non-REM Sleep (Repair Phase)**
- **Function:** Acts as a filter for memories, emotions, and experiences.
- **Process:** Selects which neural pathways are essential for integration into long-term memory storage.
- **Outcome:** Cleanses the brain of irrelevant or overwhelming information, ensuring emotional stability.

#### **b. REM Sleep (Commit Phase)**
- **Function:** Deepens the commitment of selected memories by recalibrating emotional tone and integrating procedural/emotional memories.
- **Process:** Strips raw emotion from experiences to prevent waking up overwhelmed.
- **Outcome:** Ensures that only coherent, emotionally balanced narratives are committed to memory.

### **3. Geology: Unconformities as Legible Gaps**

#### **a. Concept of Unconformities**
- **Definition:** Intentional gaps in geological records where deposition was impossible due to tectonic events (e.g., mountain uplift, erosion).
- **Significance:** Represents a period when the Earth's environment could not continue its normal recording process.

#### **b. The Case of Sicker Point (Scotland)**
- **Observation:** Vertical greywax rocks were pushed upwards by continental collisions and then eroded flat.
- **Implication:** Demonstrates that massive geological events can erase entire periods, creating gaps in the record.
- **Repair Mechanism:** Horizontal red sandstone laid down later represents a new layer of geological history, symbolizing repair after catastrophic disruption.

### **4. Psychological Resilience**

#### **a. Trauma and Grief**
- **Traditional View:** Focuses on restoring the pre-trauma state as ideal.
- **Alternative Perspective:** Acceptance that the old self is inadmissible; resilience lies in adapting to new conditions rather than returning to a perfect past.

#### **b. Embracing Change**
- **Key Idea:** Instead of feeling loss, celebrate the architecture of continued existence through adaptation and innovation.
- **Practical Application:** View gaps as opportunities for growth, learning, and creating new pathways forward.

### **5. Conclusion: Living with Gaps**

The overarching theme across systems—whether biological, technological, or geological—is that continuation is achieved not by returning to a perfect state but by navigating through disruptions with resilience and adaptability. This perspective encourages embracing change as a natural part of the journey toward continued existence.

**Final Thought:** When faced with deep trauma or significant life changes, instead of yearning for restoration to an unbroken past, focus on finding new ways to continue thriving within your current circumstances. This mindset fosters psychological resilience and aligns with the universal principle of continuation across gaps.
