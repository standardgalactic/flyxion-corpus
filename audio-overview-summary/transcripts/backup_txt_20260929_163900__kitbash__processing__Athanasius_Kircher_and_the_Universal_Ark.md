### Key Themes and Implications:

1. **Empiricism vs. Holism**: 
   - Kircher's museum represents a holistic approach, where the resonance between objects—whether physical or experiential—is integral to understanding them.
   - Grimaldi’s intervention symbolizes the shift toward empiricism, where only what can be quantified and isolated is considered valid knowledge.

2. **The Loss of Meaning**:
   - By separating elements based on provenance (geographic origin) rather than their interrelated functions, Grimaldi's actions illustrate how meaning—both personal and scientific—is often lost in the pursuit of order.
   - This resonates with modern concerns about AI’s inability to grasp context or emotion, only recognizing patterns without inherent significance.

3. **Digital Reality as a Modern Ledger**:
   - The leap from 1680 to contemporary times underscores that our current digital infrastructure (servers, data flows, neural networks) is an extension of Kircher's universal machine concept.
   - These systems operate on the same mathematical principles but lack the "cosmic sympathy" or intuitive understanding that Kircher’s museum embodied.

4. **The Salamander as a Symbol**:
   - The recurring appearance of the salamander across different eras symbolizes both an element (the amphibian) and a metaphor for unexpected, emergent connections.
   - Its hallucination by AI in processing fire imagery suggests that even without explicit knowledge of what it represents, systems can generate meaningful patterns through constraint.

5. **Application to Personal Digital Life**:
   - The final call to introspection urges viewers to examine how their own digital organization (folders, tags, algorithms) might be severing the natural connections within their experiences.
   - It challenges us to consider whether our current methods of organizing information—while efficient—are also stripping away the richness and unexpected links that make life meaningful.

### Conclusion:

"Mundus Subterraneous" invites a reflective examination of how deeply ingrained practices of categorization and reductionism affect not only historical understandings but also contemporary digital experiences. It serves as both a cautionary tale about the potential loss of meaning in our pursuit of order, and an invitation to seek ways—whether through technology or personal habits—to preserve the interconnectedness that defines life itself. The film’s message is clear: while we may be building more sophisticated ledgers, we must also strive to maintain—or even rediscover—the salamander-like connections that breathe life into our systems.
