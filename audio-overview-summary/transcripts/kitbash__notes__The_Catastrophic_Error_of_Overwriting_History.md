# kitbash/notes/The_Catastrophic_Error_of_Overwriting_History

The key takeaway from these two papers is that our current systems—whether in computing, economics, or society—are fundamentally designed to optimize for speed and efficiency at the expense of preserving and protecting slow variables like memory, health, and long-term sustainability. This "erasure problem" manifests as a relentless drive to overwrite past data (in technology) and foundational aspects of human cognition and environmental systems (in society). The proposed solution is to adopt what Flyxion calls **constraint first design**—a philosophy that prioritizes the protection of these critical slow variables by establishing hard, non-negotiable constraints before optimizing for speed or efficiency. 

This approach involves:

1. **Upper Bounds on Accumulation**: Limiting how much wealth or resources any individual or entity can hoard to prevent extreme imbalances and systemic failures.
2. **Lower Bounds on Access**: Ensuring that symbolic systems (like payment networks) cannot fully block access to basic necessities, guaranteeing survival precedes accounting.
3. **Local Regeneration Loops**: Designing global supply chains and infrastructure so that production occurs close enough to consumption that the feedback loop between actions and consequences is short and unavoidable, preventing environmental depletion in one region from affecting another.
4. **Persistence Contracts**: Commitments by system builders to maintain backward compatibility and stability, allowing users to build mastery over tools without constant disruption or obsolescence.

Ultimately, the synthesis suggests a fundamental shift in how we design systems—from optimizing for speed and efficiency alone to prioritizing longevity, resilience, and the preservation of essential slow variables. This challenges us to reconsider our underlying questions: Are we building for endurance, or merely for faster performance? If not, we risk creating machines and societies designed for their own erasure.
