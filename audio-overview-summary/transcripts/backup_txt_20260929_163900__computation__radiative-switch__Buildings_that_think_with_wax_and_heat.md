2. **Terminal Computation**: Structures such as stone arches settle under initial load and remain static thereafter, preserving a final state but erasing all intermediate processes or histories.

3. **Accumulative Computation**: Analogous to biological growth, where each year adds a new ring (e.g., trees), this mode preserves history in a legible form—like how scars remember trauma without detailing the minute events of healing.

4. **Cell Membrane Analogy and Autopoiesis**: The paper draws parallels with cell membranes that exchange matter and energy while maintaining internal organization, akin to self-creation (autopoiesis) where boundaries are permeable yet functional.

5. **Containment as Computation**: Flyxion argues against the traditional view of containment as a sealed barrier; instead, it is about maintaining structural integrity despite external chaos—akin to how biological systems sustain themselves through constant exchange with their environment.

6. **Implications for Urban Design**: The radical idea that an entire city could be constructed from xylomorphic materials would transform urban spaces into one massive computational entity capable of self-optimization and adaptation, fundamentally altering our perception of cities as collections of independent structures rather than a unified system.

This exploration suggests profound implications for how we design and interact with the built environment, emphasizing the potential for passive materials to embody intelligence and adaptability in ways previously unimaginable.
