**Section 5 – Resolution & Future Directions**

The rigorous proof presented in the paper demonstrates a fundamental limitation of agents whose objective function only minimizes surprise: they inevitably collapse into passive darkroom states where action ceases and predictive complexity flattens. To achieve persistent dynamic agency, the sources propose replacing this risk‑averse energy functional with the **Expected Free Energy (EFE)** framework from active inference.

### Key Improvements via EFE

1. **Epistemic Value Term**  
   - The original objective penalized surprise alone; EFE introduces a second term that rewards knowledge acquisition and reduces long‑term uncertainty, even if it temporarily increases instantaneous surprise.
   - This creates an intrinsic curiosity mechanism: the agent is incentivized to explore high‑curvature regions of the predictive manifold.

2. **Structural Dissipation Counteraction**  
   - By explicitly rewarding information gain, EFE directly combats the tendency toward informational flatness and structural dissipation that leads to collapse.
   - Exploration becomes a stable process rather than a transient deviation.

### Critical Extensions for Future Research

1. **Complexity Density Calibration**  
   - The abstract treatment of Atal (algorithmic complexity) must be grounded in concrete measures from information theory (e.g., Solomonoff’s measure). Understanding how computational difficulty translates into geometric density will refine the model’s physical interpretation.

2. **Incorporation of Noise & Stochastic Dynamics**  
   - Transitioning from deterministic to stochastic PDEs is essential for realism, as noise can represent environmental unpredictability or internal curiosity fluctuations that may temporarily displace agents out of their minimum energy wells.

3. **Advanced Policy Modeling**  
   - The quadratic cost function used for the action field should be replaced with more sophisticated control constraints and learning algorithms that capture how actions dynamically alter complexity density, enabling true self‑modifying agency.

4. **External Forcing & Phase Transitions**  
   - Investigate whether continuous external forces or unpredictable data streams can sustain non‑zero long‑term curvature, potentially leading to a structurally stable state where the agent remains perpetually learning and exploring rather than collapsing into stasis.

### Broader Implications

- **Agency as Geometric Dissipation**: The paper underscores that agency is not merely a philosophical concept but a geometric manifestation of dissipative processes. Persistent curiosity requires mechanisms (like EFE) designed to counteract universal tendencies toward informational entropy.
  
- **Universal Principle**: If the universe’s natural tendency is indeed toward maximal predictability and minimal complexity, then any system aiming for true, non‑transient agency must embed structures that actively fight this fundamental law.

- **Philosophical & Practical Consequences**  
  - This insight challenges conventional views of intelligence as static optimization. Instead, it frames agency as a dynamic balance between exploration (curvature) and exploitation (collapse), requiring continual intervention to maintain informational richness.
  - For artificial systems, designing agents with EFE‑based objectives could lead to more robust, adaptable behaviors that resist stagnation.

In summary, the resolution calls for moving beyond simple surprise minimization toward frameworks like EFE that embed curiosity as a fundamental component. Future work will focus on operationalizing these ideas through concrete models of complexity density, stochastic dynamics, advanced policy learning, and external forcing mechanisms—each aimed at sustaining persistent agency in the face of universal informational trends.
