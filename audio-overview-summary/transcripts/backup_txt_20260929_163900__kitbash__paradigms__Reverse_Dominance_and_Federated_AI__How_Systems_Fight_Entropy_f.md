**Summary**

The audio overview discusses how complex systems—whether ancient human societies or modern artificial intelligence networks—maintain order by actively suppressing individual selfishness. In early egalitarian groups, “reverse dominance” prevented dominant individuals from asserting power; instead, collective ridicule and social isolation kept ambition in check. When these measures failed, extreme sanctions like execution were employed to prevent a tyrannical takeover.

In the digital age, federated learning (federated averaging) mirrors this principle by allowing devices to train locally on their own data without sending raw information to a central server. Model updates are averaged and then destroyed, ensuring privacy while still enabling collective intelligence improvements. Differential privacy further protects individual data by adding calibrated noise, preventing any single update from being traced back to its source.

The overview also introduces the Relativistic Staler Vector Plenum (RSVP) framework, which models order as emerging from three interacting fields: a scalar potential (capacity), a vector flow (attention), and entropy (disorder). Order is thus seen as an active process of managing or constraining disorder—whether in human social structures or AI systems. The final provocative claim is that the mechanism of suppressing individual selfish signals to foster collective coherence may be universal across all forms of cooperation, prompting listeners to examine similar constraints in their own environments.
