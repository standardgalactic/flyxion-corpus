# paracosm/Reverse_Dominance_and_Federated_AI__How_Systems_Fight_Entropy_f

**Summary:**

The audio overview discusses how both ancient human societies and modern artificial intelligence systems manage complexity by actively suppressing individual domination or selfish behavior, thereby maintaining order. In early egalitarian tribes (≈15,000 years ago), a "reverse dominance hierarchy" was enforced through psychological pressure—primarily ridicule—and social sanctions like shunning and ostracism—to prevent potential bullies from establishing dominance. This mirrors how federated AI algorithms manage data complexity: by using Federated Averaging to aggregate model updates without sharing raw data, thereby preventing any single device or central server from becoming a dominant information despot.

The concept of differential privacy (DP) is introduced as a mechanism that adds noise to the aggregated updates, ensuring individual privacy while still allowing collective learning. The overview also touches on the Relativistic Staler Vector Plenum (RSVP) framework, which models order and complexity through three interacting fields—capacity, attention, and entropy—and suggests these principles apply not only to social systems but potentially to cosmic structures as well.

The overarching theme is that cooperation in any system requires active suppression of individual selfishness or dominance; whether it’s the collective enforcement mechanisms in ancient societies or privacy-preserving algorithms in modern AI, both rely on maintaining a balance between order and disorder to achieve coherent outcomes. This idea prompts listeners to reflect on similar constraints present in their own social environments.
