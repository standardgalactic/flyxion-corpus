**Summary**

The essay by Stephen Henry McRae critiques Anthony Flew’s proposal that atheism should be understood as merely a “lack of belief” in God, rather than an affirmative claim that God does not exist. Using the semiotic square of opposition—a logical framework for defining relationships between opposing concepts—the author argues that this definition blurs the distinctions among atheism, theism, and agnosticism, leading to what McRae calls a *semantic collapse*. By allowing someone to be simultaneously an atheist (lack of belief), a theist (belief in God’s existence), and an agnostic (uncertainty), the terms lose their clear, mutually exclusive meanings. This ambiguity undermines philosophical debate by preventing precise communication about religious beliefs. McRae advocates retaining Flew’s original distinction between strong atheism (negative claim) and weak/agnostic atheism (lack of belief) to preserve semantic clarity.
