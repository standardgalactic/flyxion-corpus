The term being referenced here is "grokking." It originates from Robert A. Heinlein's 1961 science fiction novel *Stranger in a Strange Land*, where the word was coined by the character Valentine Michael Smith (Val), an alien raised on another planet, to describe a deep understanding or intuitive grasp of something after initially memorizing details superficially. In the context you provided, grokking refers to the phenomenon observed in AI training experiments—whereafter a model achieves perfect memorization of its training data (with zero error rate), it eventually begins to generalize and truly understand the underlying rules governing that data, rather than just reproducing surface-level patterns or "spikes." This concept challenges traditional views on learning by suggesting that initial brute-force memorization can precede genuine abstraction and understanding.
