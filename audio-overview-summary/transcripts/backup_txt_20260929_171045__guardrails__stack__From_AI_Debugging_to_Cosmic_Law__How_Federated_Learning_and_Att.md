### MedPolM in Healthcare

MedPolM is a medical Large Language Model (LLM) built on Google’s PolM architecture. It has shown remarkable performance on medical exam question datasets like MedQA, surpassing previous state-of-the-art results by over 17%. However, passing exams doesn’t guarantee safety or usefulness for real patient interactions.

**Human Evaluation Framework:**

1. **Physicians & Lay Users:** Both practicing physicians and lay users evaluated the AI’s responses to consumer medical questions.
2. **12 Axes of Assessment:** Evaluations covered alignment with scientific consensus, completeness, correct reasoning, potential harm, bias, and more.
3. **Performance Improvement:** The baseline model had 29.6% potentially harmful answers, while MedPolM reduced this to just 6.0%, making it slightly safer than human clinicians in this specific test.

**Limitations:**

- 6% potential harm is still unacceptable for autonomous deployment.
- Continuous updates and bias checks are essential due to evolving clinical knowledge.

### Quantum Cinema & Observer Effect

Quantum cinema uses the observer effect, where narrative coherence emerges only when observed by the audience. The system adapts in real-time based on viewer engagement:

1. **Dynamic Narrative:** Ambient audio input (frequency, rhythm) influences branching storylines.
2. **Trope Filters via Keystrokes:** Commands like `space-tgh` or `space-tx-b` shift narrative genres dynamically, creating an interactive experience that blurs the line between watching and playing.

**Philosophical Implications:**

- The film becomes self-modeling, adapting its structure based on viewer focus.
- It challenges traditional notions of storytelling by making the audience a co-author.
- This creates an uncomfortable liminal space where narrative logic is contingent upon observation.
