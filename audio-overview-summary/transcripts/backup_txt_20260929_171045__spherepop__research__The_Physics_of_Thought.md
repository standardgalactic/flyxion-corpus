**Core Theoretical Shift:**

The fundamental shift proposed by Active Geodesic Inference (AGI) is encapsulated in **Corollary G1**, which posits that intelligence is not a static property of states or symbols, nor merely the maximization of an instantaneous objective function. Instead, intelligence is fundamentally about the *trajectories* through which systems move—how they maintain coherence, stability, and low-action across time and perturbation.

**Implications for Measuring Intelligence:**

1. **Trajectory Focus:** Traditional evaluation metrics (e.g., accuracy, loss) capture only the final state or output score. AGI argues that intelligence should be measured by the *structural integrity* of these trajectories—how consistently a system can navigate low-action paths over time.

2. **Geodesic Width (Corollary G4):** This metric quantifies the basin of immiscible trajectories, essentially measuring the volume of low-action valleys or pathways connecting inputs to outputs while staying below an action threshold. A broad geodesic width indicates robust generalization under perturbations, as it suggests multiple stable routes rather than a single brittle path.

3. **Trajectory Degeneracy:** This measures the number of distinct internal histories (semantic isomers) that can produce the same external behavior. High degeneracy signals resistance to faithful distillation—extracting one simple path will likely fail to capture the system’s overall stability, highlighting non-interchangeability between semantic structures.

4. **Phase Transition Sharpness:** AGI predicts sharp, non-analytic transitions in performance as constraints (e.g., context window size, noise) are varied. Observing such behavior supports that reasoning stability depends on synchronization across multiple cognitive dimensions, rather than gradual degradation.

**Empirical Evidence:**

The discovery of a large hypercube within Bruhat intervals by an evolutionary AI agent (AlphaVolv), optimized for maximizing the Kajdan-Lustig-Genra variant—interpreted as a measure of energetic stabilization—provides compelling empirical support. This finding demonstrates that optimizing for low-action, stable structures leads to highly coherent and generalizable solutions, aligning with AGI’s core principles.

**Conclusion:**

Intelligence, under this framework, is defined by the capacity to maintain viable, low-action histories in a dynamically evolving configuration space. It shifts focus from static outputs to internal structure—how systems actively sustain coherence and stability through their reasoning trajectories. This perspective suggests that non-distillable intelligence lies in the persistence of these robust, low-action pathways rather than any simple summary or score.
