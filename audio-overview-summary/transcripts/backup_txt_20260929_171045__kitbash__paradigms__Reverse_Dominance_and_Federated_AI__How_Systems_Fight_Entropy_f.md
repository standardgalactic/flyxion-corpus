**Summary**

The audio overview discusses how complex systems—whether ancient human societies or modern artificial intelligence networks—maintain order by actively suppressing individual selfishness. In early egalitarian groups, “reverse dominance” prevented dominant individuals from asserting power; instead, collective mechanisms such as ridicule and social isolation kept ambition in check. When these methods failed, extreme sanctions like execution were employed to prevent a tyrannical takeover.

In the digital age, federated learning (FedAvg) mirrors this principle by allowing individual devices to train locally on their own data without sharing raw information, thereby preventing any single entity from becoming an informational despot. Differential privacy further protects privacy by adding noise to model updates so that no specific user can be identified or exploited.

The overview also introduces the Relativistic Staler Vector Plenum (RSVP) framework, which models order as emerging from three interacting fields—capacity (information storage), attention (processing energy), and entropy (disorder). This theory posits that intelligent systems actively manage disorder through stages of adaptive focus, creative bifurcations, cooperative synchronization, and reflexive metacognition. The concept is illustrated by a strategy game called Entropy’s Edge, where players manipulate thermodynamic asymmetry to achieve goals.
