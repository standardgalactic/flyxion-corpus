### **1. AI Safety in Healthcare: MedPolM Case Study**

- **Background**:  
  - *MedPolM* is an instruction‑prompt‑tuned version of Google’s Polam architecture (FlanPolM) adapted for medical use.
  - It demonstrated impressive performance on standard benchmarks, such as exceeding previous state‑of‑the‑art results by over 17% on MedQA, which includes USMLE‑style questions.

- **Human Evaluation Framework**:  
  - Researchers developed a thorough evaluation framework involving both practicing physicians and lay users to assess responses to consumer medical questions.
  - Responses were judged across 12 specific axes:
    - Alignment with scientific consensus
    - Completeness of information
    - Correct reasoning demonstrated
    - Potential for harm (including incorrect information or bias)

- **Results**:  
  - Before tuning, the base model produced potentially harmful answers in about 29.6% of cases.
  - After specialized tuning and safety interventions, this dropped to just 6.0%, which is slightly lower than human clinicians’ error rate (6.5%).

- **Implications**:  
  - The dramatic reduction in harmful responses highlights the importance of rigorous human evaluation for medical AI systems.
  - However, a 6% potential harm level remains high and unacceptable for many real‑world deployments.
