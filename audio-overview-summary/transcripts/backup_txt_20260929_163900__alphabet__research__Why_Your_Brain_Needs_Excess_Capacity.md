**Title:** The Power of Excess Capacity: How Our Brains (and AI) Thrive on Complexity

---

### Introduction

In our quest to understand intelligence—whether in biological systems like the human brain or artificial constructs such as large language models—we often hear a common mantra: “Simplify, simplify.” This belief suggests that true understanding comes from stripping away complexity and focusing only on the essential rules. However, recent research challenges this notion by revealing that both our brains and advanced AI operate most effectively when they can absorb—and retain—their fair share of noise.

### Biological Defense: The Cat’s Visual Cortex

To illustrate this counterintuitive idea, let's start with a biological example: the primary visual cortex (V1) in cats. When a cat looks at a mouse, visual information travels through the optic nerve and into V1 via an astonishing 25 to 1 expansion ratio of projecting axons relative to the input signals. This means for every single piece of visual data entering the system, there are up to 24 additional “redundant wires” processing that information deeper inside the brain.

**Why does this matter?**

- **Speed and Robustness:** The cat’s brain needs instantaneous recognition—whether it’s a shadow or an actual mouse—to survive. By expanding sensory input into high-dimensional representations, the visual cortex ensures rapid, reliable responses.
  
- **Energy Efficiency in Complexity:** Although processing 25 times more data per signal seems wasteful from an energy perspective, the benefits of speed and adaptability outweigh this cost. The brain’s metabolic inefficiency is a strategic choice for survival.

### Artificial Intelligence: CNNs vs. LLMs

Now let's shift our focus to artificial intelligence (AI). Two dominant architectures illustrate contrasting capacity regimes:

1. **Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs):** Used primarily in computer vision, CNNs are known for their massive overparameterization relative to the number of images they process. This excess capacity allows them to memorize specific training photos while still generalizing well—much like a human brain’s ability to recognize a stop sign even when covered in snow.

2. **Large Language Models (LLMs):** These models, such as ChatGPT and Gemini, boast billions or trillions of parameters but are operating under constrained capacity relative to their vast training data (the entire internet). Empirical evidence shows they can only perfectly memorize about 0.2%–1.5% of the information fed into them.

**Implications:**

- **Memorization vs. Generalization:** CNNs excel at memorizing specific details due to their excess capacity, leading to benign overfitting—where they learn patterns without losing generalizability. In contrast, LLMs compress vast amounts of data into generalized statistical rules, which explains why they sometimes hallucinate or produce nonsensical outputs when asked for exact facts.

### The Broader Lesson: Embracing Complexity

Both biological and artificial intelligence systems demonstrate that true brilliance often comes from embracing complexity rather than eliminating it:

- **Human Brains:** Our brains evolved to operate in an excess capacity regime, absorbing every chaotic detail of our environment. This allows us to find hidden harmonies within noise, leading to deep understanding.
  
- **AI Systems:** While LLMs are powerful generalizers due to their compression capabilities, they highlight the limits of constrained capacity—showing that simply increasing size or parameters does not guarantee better performance without addressing data efficiency.

### A Provocative Thought for Learners

In our modern world, there's a strong tendency toward curation and minimalism. We optimize environments with sleek designs, compressed news feeds, and standardized curriculums to eliminate “noise.” However, if Dubova and Sloman’s framework is correct—that our brains (and perhaps AI) thrive on excess capacity—then we might be inadvertently starving ourselves of the raw material needed for insight.

**Consider This:**

- **Is Decluttering Helpful?** Removing noise may make learning appear easier but could also deprive us of the complexity necessary to form robust generalizations.
  
- **What Can We Do?** Perhaps, instead of aggressively sanitizing our environments and educational approaches, we should allow for more mess—more complex, nuanced information—to fuel deeper understanding.

### Conclusion

The journey through biological and artificial intelligence systems reveals a powerful truth: true brilliance often emerges from embracing complexity rather than stripping away the messy details. By recognizing this principle, we can rethink how we approach learning, innovation, and even our own cognitive development in an increasingly digital world. Keep seeking out the noise—it may be where the most profound insights lie.
