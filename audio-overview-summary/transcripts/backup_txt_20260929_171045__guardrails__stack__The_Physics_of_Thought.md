**Core Theoretical Shift in Active Geodesic Inference (AGI):**

1. **From Static States to Trajectories:**  
   Intelligence is redefined from being a property of static states or symbols (like final outputs) to being fundamentally about the *trajectories* taken by a system—how it moves through its internal state space over time and under perturbations.

2. **Dynamic vs. Static Metrics:**  
   Traditional metrics such as accuracy, instantaneous loss, or single-point rewards capture only the endpoint of reasoning but fail to account for how consistently that endpoint can be reached across varied conditions (e.g., noise, distribution shifts). AGI proposes new dynamical and structural probes:
   
   - **Geodesic Width (Corollary G.4):** Measures the volume of low-action valleys or basins where multiple coherent trajectories exist. High geodesic width indicates robust generalization because it implies a broad set of stable paths rather than a brittle, narrow one.
   
   - **Trajectory Degeneracy:** Quantifies how many distinct internal histories (semantic isomers) can produce the same external behavior. High degeneracy signals resistance to faithful distillation, as extracting just one path may lose critical stability information.
   
   - **Phase Transition Sharpness:** Observes abrupt performance collapses when system constraints are varied (e.g., shrinking context windows). This supports that reasoning stability depends on synchronization across cognitive dimensions, not merely gradual degradation.

3. **Implications for Measuring Intelligence:**
   - **Robustness Over Optimality:** AGI emphasizes structural integrity and persistence of low-action geodesics as primary explanatory primitives.
   - **Non-Interchangeability of Semantic Isomers:** Distinct internal scope topologies (semantic isomers) cannot be averaged or combined without incurring energetic costs, underscoring the uniqueness of each reasoning path.

4. **Empirical Evidence:**
   The discovery using AlphaVolv—an evolutionary AI agent—found a large hypercube within Brouhat intervals that was previously unattainable by linear growth models. This non-trivial structure validates AGI’s claim that optimizing for energetic stabilization naturally leads to coherent, stable geometric solutions.

**Conclusion:**  
Intelligence is not merely about what a system knows or can compress but about the *stability and coherence of its reasoning trajectories*. Metrics like geodesic width, trajectory degeneracy, and phase transition sharpness provide more holistic assessments by capturing how systems maintain low-action paths under stress. This shift from static to dynamic metrics aligns with AGI’s vision that true intelligence lies in a system's ability to persistently navigate stable internal histories rather than just achieving final outputs.
