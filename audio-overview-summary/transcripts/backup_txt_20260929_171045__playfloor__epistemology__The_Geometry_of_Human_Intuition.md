### Introduction

In recent years, the phenomenon of AI hallucinations—where models like ChatGPT produce fabricated legal cases or confidently assert that the moon is made of cheese—has become a focal point for researchers and practitioners alike. This discussion delves into why these occurrences happen from a geometric perspective, drawing parallels between artificial intelligence (AI) systems and human cognitive processes.

### The Geometry Behind AI Hallucinations

1. **Deep Learning as an Approximation:**
   - A deep learning model functions by approximating the *admissibility quotient* based solely on its training data, which Flyxion refers to as its *corpus manifold*. This means that the AI essentially knows only what it has observed during its training phase.
   - The model’s reality is thus bounded by the data it has ingested. When presented with inputs far outside this corpus, it lacks the necessary context to generate accurate responses.

2. **Smooth Familiarity Potential:**
   - To output something in such cases, AI relies on a mechanism called *smooth familiarity potential*. This allows the model to guess based on proximity and similarity to previously seen data rather than true geometric understanding.
   - The result is that AI generalizes from its training set without genuine comprehension, leading to hallucinations with unwarranted confidence.

### From AI to Human Intuition

The same geometric principles underlying AI hallucinations apply to human cognition:

1. **Predicting Accessible Futures:**
   - Both AI and human brains operate on the premise of predicting accessible futures based on past experiences. This predictive capability is a double-edged sword, as it can lead to rigid models that resist new information.

2. **Interpretive Attractors and Projection Capture:**
   - Flyxion formalizes this psychological phenomenon as *interpretive attractors* and *projection capture*. These concepts describe how cognitive systems become entrenched in specific ways of thinking, making them resistant to updates or revision.
   - This dynamic projection supremacy manifests similarly in human behavior, where individuals cling tightly to their existing models despite contradictory evidence.

### The Wisdom Salon: A Geometric Solution

To address the epistemic gridlock caused by these rigid models, Monica Anderson proposes a concept called the *wisdom salon*. Using sheaf theory and the *Tartan descent theorem*, this approach offers a structured way to navigate differing perspectives:

1. **Sheaf Theory Explained Simply:**
   - Think of a sheaf as a mathematical method for stitching together local pieces of information into a coherent global picture. For example, understanding global climate requires integrating weather reports from various locations.

2. **Wisdom Salon Protocol:**
   - A wisdom salon is a protocol designed to facilitate collective epistemic reduction. Instead of chaotic debates across vast platforms like Twitter, complex issues are broken down into small, local groups.
   - Each group discusses the problem and performs a *local reduction*, producing what Anderson calls a *grain of wisdom*—a locally true statement based on shared experiences.

3. **Parallel Transport and Low Holonomy:**
   - The Tartan descent theorem proves mathematically that ideas (or grains of wisdom) which survive parallel transport across different contexts have low holonomy, meaning they remain stable, intelligible, and useful.
   - If an idea only works within a specific echo chamber or community manifold, it is merely a *projection capture*—a fragile model. Conversely, genuinely robust truths are those that hold true when moved into radically different contexts.

### Implications for Human Interaction

Understanding these geometric principles has profound implications for human interaction:

1. **Recognizing Geometric Distortions:**
   - Anxiety and trauma can be viewed as geometric distortions of the trajectory space—shrinkage in our understanding of accessible futures due to rigid models.
   - Healing or learning something new requires exposing oneself to a wider corpus of life, allowing the “river” (our cognitive model) to flow uphill again.

2. **Auditing Our Models:**
   - A critical step is continually auditing our mental maps and questioning whether we are mistaking our map for reality itself.
   - This involves actively seeking new evidence that challenges existing beliefs and being open to updating models based on fresh data.
