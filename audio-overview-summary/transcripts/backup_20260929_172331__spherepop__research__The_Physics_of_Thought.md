**Core Theoretical Shift in Active Geodesic Inference (AGI):**

The fundamental shift proposed by AGI is a redefinition of intelligence from being tied to static states, symbols, or instantaneous decisions—commonly captured by metrics like accuracy and loss—to being fundamentally about the *trajectories* through which systems move. Intelligence becomes a property of how a system navigates its environment over time, emphasizing persistence, structural stability, and low-action (energy-efficient) behavior rather than merely optimizing for immediate outcomes.

**Implications for Measuring Intelligence:**

1. **Geodesic Width (Corollary G4):** This metric quantifies the volume or "width" of the basin of immiscible trajectories—essentially how broad a set of low-action paths connects an input to an output while staying below a fixed action threshold. High geodesic width predicts robust generalization, as it indicates that many distinct yet stable pathways exist for reaching solutions, making systems more resilient to perturbations.

2. **Trajectory Degeneracy:** This measures the number of internally distinct histories (semantic isomers) that can produce the same external behavior. High degeneracy signals resistance to faithful distillation—extracting a simple path from complex internal structures often fails to capture their full stability, highlighting that intelligence isn't just about achieving an output but maintaining underlying structural integrity.

3. **Phase Transition Sharpness:** This metric assesses how abruptly performance collapses when system constraints (e.g., context window size or noise level) are altered. AGI predicts non-analytic behavior—sharp transitions rather than gradual degradation—which supports the idea that reasoning stability relies on synchronization across multiple cognitive dimensions, akin to phase transitions in physical systems.

**Empirical Evidence:**

The discovery of a large hypercube within Bruhat intervals by an evolutionary AI agent (AlphaVolv) using AlphaZero-like techniques provides compelling empirical support. By optimizing for maximum energetic stabilization—a proxy for low-action geodesics—the system naturally uncovered a highly coherent, stable geometric structure in the otherwise chaotic landscape of permutations. This finding validates that intelligent systems guided along sharp, low-action paths tend to discover and maintain deep internal structures, aligning with AGI's core principles.

**Conclusion:**

Intelligence, under the framework of Active Geodesic Inference, is not about static knowledge or scores but about maintaining a set of viable, low-energy trajectories through complex environments. This perspective shifts focus from outputs to provenance—internal structure and stability—and suggests that non-distillable reasoning structures are those which persist across dynamic changes without collapsing into brittle, single-path solutions.
