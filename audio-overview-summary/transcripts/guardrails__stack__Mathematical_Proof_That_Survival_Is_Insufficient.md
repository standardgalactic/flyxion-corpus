### 1. Error Correction Through Redundancy

**Digital vs. Artistic Redundancy**

- **Literal Redundancy (Digital):** In digital communication, redundancy is achieved by sending repeated bits to correct errors introduced by noise on a channel. For example, transmitting "101" as "111‑0000111" allows the receiver to infer the original message even if some bits flip due to static.

- **Structural Redundancy (Artistic):** Unlike digital systems that rely on literal repetition, art employs structural redundancy—encoding meaning across multiple dimensions simultaneously. A Shakespearean tragedy conveys its core themes through plot structure, character psychology, rhythm, and thematic exploration, ensuring the message persists even if significant portions are lost.

### 2. Scalar vs. Vector Identity

**Scalar Identity**

- Defined by current magnitude or state (e.g., job title, bank account balance). It is fragile; a collapse can render scalar identity meaningless overnight.

**Vector Identity**

- Represents trajectory and direction through constraint space rather than static state. For instance, Kirsten's unwavering commitment to performance acts as her vector identity, persisting despite physical or social upheaval.

### 3. Theoretical Implications of Collapse

**Theorem 8.6: Propagation Speed in Chaos**

- In environments of extreme preference instability (as during an apocalypse), systems that narrow the option space propagate faster because they reduce cognitive burden. Coercive regimes exploit this by offering a single, clear path forward, bypassing complex decision-making.
