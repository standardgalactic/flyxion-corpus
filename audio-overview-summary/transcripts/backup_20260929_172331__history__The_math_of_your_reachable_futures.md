**Summary of Key Concepts and Conclusions**

1. **Layered View of Consciousness:**
   - *Manifold Structure (Layer One):* This represents the physical wiring—86 billion neurons, synapses—that defines the absolute limits of what a brain can do. It’s like the piano keys that cannot be played; they are fixed by biology.
   - *Admissible Continuations (Layer Two):* These are the mathematical maps (e.g., gamma C of X) plotting all possible reachable futures from any given state. This is the true underlying conserved object, allowing survival and consistent behavior despite constant biological drift.
   - *Biological Mechanism (Layer Three):* Brain waves and oscillations act as tools to traverse these continuations—highlighting and selecting among mathematically possible futures—but they do not define the space of what’s reachable.

2. **Identity and Stability:**
   - Despite the brain’s constantly shifting physical substrate, human identity remains stable because it is anchored in the continuity of admissible continuations rather than any single snapshot of neural activity.
   - This suggests that we are defined by our potential to navigate future states (our “continuations”) rather than by the exact biological state at any given moment.

3. **Implications for Consciousness Research:**
   - Modern theories like integrated information theory and global workspace theory often conflate correlates with causation, mistaking correlation for explanation.
   - Flyxion’s approach demands rigorous separation of what is correlated (e.g., wave organization under anesthesia) from what is causally required (the actual mechanism generating subjective experience).

4. **Philosophical Consequence:**
   - If consciousness arises from the mathematical structure of reachable futures rather than any specific biological or electrical state, it implies a profound shift in understanding human identity.
   - We are not defined by our current physical processes but by the potential to continue existing and acting within those future possibilities.

**Final Provocative Thought:**
We are fundamentally patterns of reachable futures—mathematical continuations—that preserve our sense of self across biological instability. This challenges traditional notions of personal continuity tied to specific neural states, suggesting instead that identity is rooted in the abstract structure of what we can do next.
