1. **Agency**: Traditional notions of agency—seeing choice as a singular event in time—are challenged. This shifts the focus from predicting outcomes to actively suppressing non‑desired future paths.

5. **Mistakes and Flexibility**: Errors are not just computational errors but legitimate, internally coherent plans that happen to incur penalties. The key leverage for control lies in early suppression of these trajectories rather than fighting them at the last moment when they’re already irreversible.

6. **Control as a Long‑Term Process**: The highest form of self‑control isn’t about white‑knuckled struggle against temptation but involves cultivating neural architecture—adjusting biases, learning rates, and dynamics—to physically prune undesirable future possibilities before they even manifest.
