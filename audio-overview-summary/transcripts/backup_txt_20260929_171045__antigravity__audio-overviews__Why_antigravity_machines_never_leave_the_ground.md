**What a Granted Patent Actually Certifies**

A granted patent does not certify that the invention works as claimed in terms of functionality or physics. Instead, it certifies three legal criteria:

1. **Novelty:** The configuration of parts is legally new and has not been previously patented.
2. **Non-obviousness:** The combination of elements is unique enough that a standard engineer would not immediately think of it.
3. **Enablement:** The inventor has described the physical structure thoroughly enough that someone else could theoretically build the device based on the drawings.

In essence, a patent serves as proof that an inventor can format and file legal documentation correctly and pay the required filing fee—not that the invention works or is physically feasible.

**Boeing’s Anti-Gravity Investigation**

The Boeing GRIASP program investigated speculative literature on gravity modification. The engineers at Boeing Phantom Works understood basic physics but recognized:

- **Theroretical Infeasibility:** There was no viable mechanism to interact with gravitational curvature.
- **Experimental Evidence:** No experimental setups demonstrated a credible effect beyond known phenomena like thermal expansion or ionic wind.

Consequently, the program was quietly shut down without filing patents or building functional devices. This illustrates that corporate investigations into fringe science are often conducted as risk management and due diligence rather than pursuit of actual breakthroughs.

**Implications for Critical Thinking**

Understanding these points helps critically evaluate claims about anti-gravity or reactionless drives:

- **Vacuum Test:** Check if the effect persists with more sensitive instruments; a diminishing effect toward zero suggests it’s not real.
- **Mechanistic Validity:** Ensure any proposed mechanism has a logical channel to interact with established gravitational bookkeeping. Many such devices fail this test (e.g., spinning magnets treated like local magnetic flux lines).

**Psychological Aspect**

The persistent cultural fascination with anti-gravity might be more about psychological rebellion against gravity’s binding nature—symbolizing our desire for freedom and escape from mortality rather than a genuine scientific breakthrough.

In summary, patents certify legal novelty, non-obviousness, and enablement—not functionality. Investigative programs like Boeing's serve as cautionary examples of how rigorous examination can reveal the futility of certain speculative claims without actual physical proof.
