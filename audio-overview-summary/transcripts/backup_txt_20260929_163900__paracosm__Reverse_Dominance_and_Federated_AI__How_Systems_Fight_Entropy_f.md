**Summary**

The audio overview discusses how both ancient human societies and modern artificial intelligence systems manage disorder by actively suppressing the tendency toward domination or selfishness. In prehistoric egalitarian groups, “reverse dominance hierarchy” was maintained through collective psychological pressure—mainly ridicule and social isolation—that kept ambitious individuals in check without stifling genuine talent. When these measures failed, extreme sanctions such as execution were employed to prevent a single individual from consolidating power.

In the digital age, federated AI algorithms like Federated Averaging (FedAvg) mirror this principle by allowing devices to train locally on their own data and share only model updates with a central server, ensuring raw information never leaves the device. Differential privacy further protects individual privacy by adding noise to these updates so that no single update can be traced back to its original source.

The overview also introduces the Relativistic Staler Vector Plenum (RSVP) framework, which models order as emerging from three interacting fields—capacity (storage), vector flow (attention), and entropy (disorder)—and describes how systems progress through stages of adaptive attention, creative bifurcation, cooperative synchronization, and finally reflexive metacognition. This model suggests that cooperation in any form requires active suppression of individual selfish signals to maintain collective coherence.
