# guardrails/stack/Beyond__Thinking_Out_Loud__Unpacking_AI_s_True_Reasoning_and_the_Future_of_Trustworthy_AI

The audio overview discusses an essay that critiques the notion of “thinking out loud” (chain‑of‑thought reasoning) used by advanced AI systems. The central thesis is that this output often appears as a plausible narrative rather than genuine causal reasoning, leading to potential misinterpretations and trust issues. Key arguments include:

- **Confabulation**: Experiments show that altering intermediate steps in an AI’s explanation sometimes leaves the final answer unchanged, indicating the explanations may be fabricated rather than reflecting true internal processes.
  
- **Limitations of Co‑T**: The approach relies on linguistic performance, which can mislead users into believing they understand how decisions were made when only a superficial story is provided.

The essay proposes alternatives such as **Chain of Memory (CoM)** and **Calmed Reasoning**, emphasizing causal faithfulness by tracing internal latent memory states through techniques like gradient‑based tracing. This yields more reliable, interpretable reasoning.

Further developments are explored:

- **RSVP‑Based Calm**: Treats internal memory states as dynamic fields governed by thermodynamic principles, ensuring coherence and stability.
  
- **Tartan**: Introduces oscillatory semantics where even static states encode nuanced meaning through subtle micro‑movements or “cognitive glyphs,” allowing interpretation of complex embodied abstractions.

The overarching goal is to create truly understandable AI—systems whose reasoning can be verified, trusted, and personalized for safety‑critical applications (e.g., medical diagnosis, autonomous vehicles). This shift also raises philosophical questions about the nature of cognition and memory in both artificial and biological systems.
