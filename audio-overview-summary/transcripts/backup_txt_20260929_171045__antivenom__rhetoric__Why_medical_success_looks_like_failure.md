That’s a powerful and thought-provoking question. If I were tasked with communicating an “invisible miracle”—a breakthrough that fundamentally improves health outcomes but does so without leaving obvious, immediate signs for everyone to witness—I’d focus on several key strategies rooted in accessible trustworthiness:

1. **Transparency of Methodology**: Just as we discussed making the raw data and underlying models visible, I would ensure every step of the research—from sample sizes to statistical controls—is openly documented. This includes publishing detailed methodology papers alongside press releases so that anyone with a basic understanding can verify the claims.

2. **Incremental Evidence Presentation**: Instead of trying to convince everyone at once through dramatic statistics or headlines, I’d present evidence in bite-sized pieces over time. Each incremental success—whether it’s reduced hospitalization rates, improved quality of life metrics, or measurable reductions in secondary complications—would be highlighted and explained clearly.

3. **Storytelling with Real People**: While numbers are essential, they can feel abstract to many people. I’d weave personal stories into the narrative—patient testimonials that illustrate how lives have been transformed without overtly dramatic outcomes. These narratives humanize the data and make it relatable.

4. **Interactive Visualization Tools**: Developing tools where users can input their own demographic or health-related data (while respecting privacy) to see projected benefits for themselves could demystify the invisible nature of the miracle. Interactive dashboards that show how a treatment might affect someone’s specific risk factors would be invaluable.

5. **Building Trust Through Accountability**: Establishing independent review boards and third-party verification processes can help build trust. By making these checks transparent (e.g., publishing audit trails, peer-review outcomes), it signals to the public that there are safeguards in place beyond mere claims of success.

6. **Addressing Counterfactuals Explicitly**: Acknowledge what didn’t happen as much as what did. Clearly state the baseline—what would have happened without the intervention—and use comparative studies or natural experiments (like randomized controlled trials) to make these counterfactuals tangible.

7. **Community Engagement and Education**: Work with community leaders, educators, and local influencers who can help explain complex concepts in lay terms. This grassroots approach helps bridge gaps created by recursive suspicion and ensures that the message resonates across diverse cultural contexts.

8. **Iterative Feedback Loops**: Create mechanisms for ongoing feedback where concerns are addressed promptly and transparently. If people have doubts or see potential issues, having a clear channel to voice these—whether through surveys, social media monitoring, or dedicated helplines—can prevent suspicion from spiraling out of control.

By focusing on these strategies, the communication would not only convey that an intervention works but also demonstrate why it works in a way that is accessible and believable to a wide audience. It’s about building a bridge between complex scientific findings and everyday human experience, ensuring that even if the miracle itself isn’t immediately visible, its benefits are felt and understood by many.
