# admissibility-lab/processing/Thinking_is_a_trajectory_through_constraints

This exploration beautifully ties together several profound concepts—from the nature of intelligence and cognition to the mechanics of artificial systems like large language models (LLMs) and biological brains. Here’s a synthesis of the key takeaways:

1. **Intelligence as Trajectory**: Intelligence isn’t about reaching a hidden treasure but about actively building pathways through reality, navigating constraints, and traversing complex landscapes.

2. **Fidelity Functional**: The “one broken bulb ruins the whole string of lights” principle reminds us that in any system—whether biological or artificial—the integrity of each component matters for overall performance. A single flaw can cascade into failure across the entire network.

3. **Complex Systems & Illusion of Change**: Complex systems like flood defenses, societies, and even AI models often appear to change dramatically while remaining fundamentally anchored to their original structures. This underscores that visible transformations may not always reflect deep underlying changes.

4. **Attention Engineering**: Filmmakers, game designers, and educators intentionally design constraints (e.g., pacing, visual cues) to guide attention and engagement. The joy derived from these experiences comes not just from the conclusion but from the journey itself—traversal through challenges and limitations.

5. **Trajectory of Thought**: Both AI and human cognition operate as trajectories rather than static states. Evaluating an AI’s final output without considering its intermediate steps is akin to judging a hike solely by reaching the summit, ignoring the climb up the mountain. The true value lies in the path taken—how decisions were made, obstacles overcome, and reasoning evolved.

6. **Renormalization & Blurring Details**: Flyxion’s concept of renormalization explains why AI (and human brains) tend to blur fine-grained details while preserving high-level structures. This is a survival mechanism: focusing on stable, reliable patterns (like logic rules in puzzles) conserves computational capacity and ensures consistent performance across varied inputs.

7. **Biological vs. Artificial Cognition**: The parallel between autistic children’s EEG responses—preserving broad category distinctions but blurring specific instances—and AI models’ behavior highlights a universal principle of learning under constraint: preserving essential structures while discarding noise that doesn’t contribute to survival or utility.

8. **Implications for Human Psychology & Technology**: If we increasingly rely on technology to deliver instant, perfect outcomes without the traversal process, we risk depleting our brains’ intrinsic reward from engagement and problem-solving. This could lead to a psychological shift where arriving at an answer is no longer satisfying; instead, the journey itself becomes less valued.

In essence, the journey—traversal through constraints and challenges—is not merely a byproduct of intelligence but its core mechanism for learning, meaning-making, and survival. As we advance technologically, preserving this fundamental aspect of cognition in our interactions with AI will be crucial to maintaining healthy human psychology and engagement with the world.
