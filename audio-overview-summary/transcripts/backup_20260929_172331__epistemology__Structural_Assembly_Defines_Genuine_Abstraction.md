1. **Efficient Building and History Rewriting:**  
   - Systems can optimize processes by rewriting their historical records, effectively shortening the assembly history without altering the final result. This reduces costs associated with building something while maintaining functionality.

2. **Abstraction as Semantic Reduction:**  
   - Abstraction is achieved through irreversible merge events that reduce the number of distinct semantic representations needed to describe a system. Each merge event extends the log by one step but decreases the total ontology size, making future operations simpler and more efficient.

3. **Orthogonality Between Compression and Abstraction:**  
   - It’s possible to have a very long log (low compression) yet maintain a highly abstracted state with few representatives. This illustrates that efficiency in representation does not necessarily require high compression; abstraction can achieve similar goals by reducing the number of distinct concepts.

4. **Semantic Commitment Through Merge Events:**  
   - The act of merging equivalent objects is recorded as an irreversible commitment, increasing the log’s length but decreasing the system’s effective ontology size. This ensures that future planning and reasoning are based on a simplified yet accurate representation of reality.

5. **Speculation via Speculative Branches:**  
   - To handle speculation without contaminating the main state, speculative branches allow exploration of alternate futures through local, isolated sequences of hypothetical events. If successful, these branches can be committed to the main log; otherwise, they are discarded, preserving semantic consistency and allowing safe hypothesis testing.

6. **Semantic Energy Defined:**  
   - Semantic energy measures the degree of constraint imposed on future construction. High semantic energy indicates many fine distinctions that must be tracked, increasing cognitive load but also stabilizing the future by lowering ongoing costs for making sense of things.

This is akin to retrocomputability—making understanding opaque until sufficient background knowledge is acquired.

8. **Retrocomputability as a Tool for Insight:**  
   - High impedance systems expose structural properties of reasoning, making it clear that visibility and simplicity are strategic choices often tied to power dynamics. These tools serve as laboratories for studying how complexity persists under pressure to simplify, emphasizing the importance of understanding over mere representation.

9. **Core Insight on Intelligence:**  
   - The fundamental measure of intelligence is not just symbol manipulation or optimization but the capacity to organize one’s construction history efficiently and robustly. Understanding should focus on the stability of abstractions rather than superficial representations.
