# negentropy/Xenophon

Summary:

The transcript discusses Xenophon, a Greek historian, philosopher, soldier, and mercenary who lived from circa 430-354 BCE. Born in Erchia near Athens, he received an education under various teachers, including Socrates, whose influence is evident but not fully explored. As a young man, he served as a soldier in the Peloponnesian War, witnessing significant events like the defeat of Athens and Spartan hegemony. Post-war, he became a mercenary fighting for Sparta, Cyrus the Younger, and other forces, developing his skills as a tactician, writer, and philosopher.

His later life saw him settle in Corinth where he wrote extensively on history, philosophy, politics, and economics, with works like "Anabasis," "Hellenica," "Memorabilia," and "Cyropaedia" becoming influential. Xenophon maintained strong ties to Athens, known for his patriotism and loyalty to the Athenian cause. His accounts of events are not abundant but are documented in his own writings and other historical sources.

"Hellenica" suggests that Xenophon personally witnessed the events he wrote about, continuing Thucydides' work on Greek history from the Peloponnesian War's end to Philip II of Macedon's victory over Greece. It provides a detailed account of political and military events, offering an objective viewpoint on decisions made by Greeks and the rise of Macedon under Philip II.

The transcript also touches upon Xenophon’s involvement in significant historical moments such as Alcibiades' return from exile and the Trial of the Generals (406 BC), illustrating his role in documenting pivotal political and social climates of Athens. This underscores his reputation as a reliable historian chronicling Greek history, offering valuable insights into ancient Greece's culture, society, and military systems.
