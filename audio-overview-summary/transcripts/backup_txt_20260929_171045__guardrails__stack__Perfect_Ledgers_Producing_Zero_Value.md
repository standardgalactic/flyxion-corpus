The key takeaway from this discussion is that while technological systems—whether they be blockchain networks, corporate infrastructures, advertising platforms, or children’s gaming environments—can appear highly functional and efficient due to their flawless records (logs) and measurable activity, they may still be fundamentally degenerate if they do not meet three strict conditions for genuine productivity:

1. **Global Boundary Costs**: The benefits of the system must outweigh its total environmental, energetic, and material costs imposed on external populations who do not share in the upside.

2. **Structural Stability**: The system’s dynamics should naturally gravitate toward stable, low-action configurations that promote utility and generation rather than volatility or rapid reallocation for profit.

3. **Decrease in Global Work Functional**: The overall work functional of the planet—meaning the ease with which humans can navigate their environment tomorrow compared to today—must objectively decrease due to actual physical or organizational transformations.

Examples provided include supply chain verification using decentralized ledgers, which reduces friction and fraud in moving pharmaceuticals globally without displacing more energy than it saves. Similarly, credential management via secure ledgers cuts bureaucratic overhead for professional licensing across borders. However, these examples underscore that the choice of consensus mechanism (e.g., proof-of-work vs. federated networks) is crucial; an inefficient blockchain can negate its benefits if it incurs disproportionate environmental costs.

Ultimately, the paper argues against viewing success solely in terms of internal elegance or user engagement metrics but emphasizes a holistic evaluation based on whether systems contribute positively to human progress and reduce overall effort required for living sustainably. This perspective challenges us to critically assess not just the surface-level achievements (like flawless logs) but also the real-world impact of these technologies on our environment and well-being.
