# kitbash/philosophy/Why_Rust_rules_are_physical_laws

The discussion revolves around how strict boundaries—whether in software design (like Rust’s rules), mechanical systems (the hardware store analogy), biological taxonomy (inheritance vs. composition), or everyday life (communication and workflow)—serve to create trustworthy, reliable systems by preventing the propagation of errors or incomplete information. By enforcing unique authority, explicit state spaces, exhaustive pattern matching, precise trait bounds, and honest handling of partiality, we ensure that each component operates within its defined capabilities without being burdened with unnecessary obligations it cannot fulfill. This approach minimizes action at a distance—where one part’s failure unexpectedly affects another—and thus builds systems that align closely with physical reality rather than abstract claims.

In practical terms, this means:

1. **Software Design**: Using Rust (or similar languages) to enforce compile-time checks and explicit error handling prevents bugs from propagating silently through the codebase.
2. **Mechanical Systems**: Just as a vending machine’s internal mechanisms only accept genuine currency, real-world systems should validate inputs against their honest domain rather than merely their claimed one.
3. **Biological Taxonomy**: Inheritance in biology can impose unnecessary constraints (like obliging flightless birds to inherit flying capabilities), whereas composition allows organisms to evolve without carrying baggage they cannot use.
4. **Everyday Life**: Applying these principles to communication or workflow ensures that responsibilities are clearly assigned and maintained, reducing misunderstandings and inefficiencies.

Ultimately, the goal is not just to avoid friction but to eliminate it by making every boundary explicit and enforced, leading to systems that are robust, predictable, and trustworthy across all domains—from code to daily interactions.
