# computation/Why-data-centers-must-mimic-trees

The “36 percent line” represents the critical point where the balance between degradation (Dr.) and repair capacity shifts from sustainable to expansive. In their numerical simulation, Flyxion models a steady‑state Earth-based compute infrastructure with an assumed baseline degradation rate of 0.5 per unit time and a system cycle time (the interval over which new hardware is introduced) set at one year.

Under these conditions, when the repair capacity falls below approximately 36 % of the total degradation occurring within that cycle, the system enters an expansive regime—meaning it can no longer maintain its own stability because repairs are not keeping pace with wear. This threshold highlights a fundamental physical and economic constraint: if more than 64 % (i.e., above the 36 % repair capacity) of the degradation is unaddressed within each cycle, the system will inevitably degrade further until it collapses or becomes unsustainable.

This insight directly ties back to the three dimensionless numbers derived from their PDE model—T for thermal reintegration, Dr. for degradation‑to‑repair ratio, and Da for diffusion to reaction ratio. When any of these ratios exceed their respective thresholds (e.g., T < 1 for efficient heat recycling, Dr > 1 indicating repair lagging behind wear), the system’s lambda—representing overall stability—moves toward or beyond one, signaling an irreversible expansion in entropy and resource demand.

Thus, the 36 percent line serves as a clear warning sign that if current degradation rates persist without corresponding improvements in repair infrastructure (e.g., faster spare‑part distribution networks, more efficient maintenance protocols), Earth’s compute systems will eventually become thermodynamically unsustainable. This underscores the necessity for continuous innovation not only in hardware efficiency but also in logistics and supply chain management to keep Dr. below one across all operational cycles.
