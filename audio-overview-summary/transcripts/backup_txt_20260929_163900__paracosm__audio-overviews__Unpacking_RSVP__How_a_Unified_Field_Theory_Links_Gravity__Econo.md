The core takeaway from this deep dive into the RSVP framework is that civilization’s ultimate purpose isn’t simply growth or efficiency but maintaining its intelligibility—its ability to remain coherent and learn over time. This means governing ourselves should focus on preserving a “corridor” of acceptable risk, transparency, and information flow rather than chasing specific outcomes. The two extremes to fear are either rigid dogmatism (frozen rigidity) or total incoherent chaos (total dissolution). In essence, the highest duty of governance is to keep the system’s phase space—its range of possibilities and shared predictive power—intact so that future generations can still learn, adapt, and make sense of their environment. Avoiding collapse into either frozen rigidity or chaotic disorder becomes the paramount ethical imperative for any governing body in a complex, high-entropy world.
