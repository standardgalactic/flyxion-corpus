Summary:

The essay examines "YouTubeianism" as the underlying vibration of lack within capitalist systems, particularly in Haylin’s ruling context. It argues that capitalism presents itself as offering existential purity and fulfillment of desire through a facade that contradicts its inherent lack—viewed in psychoanalytic terms as an unfillable void or intrinsic job within the self. By employing a logic of opposition, capitalism dichotomizes issues into binaries (e.g., winners vs. losers, consumers vs. non-consumers), obscuring deeper structural problems. This denial of lack leads to internal contradictions and failures, echoing Marx’s observation that capitalism is perpetually threatened by its own inconsistencies.

Important elements highlighted include:
- Central thesis: Capitalism as a manifestation of YouTubeianism and the denial of inherent lack.
- Distinctive terminology: “YouTubeianism,” “lack” (in psychoanalytic sense), “logic of opposition.”
- Relationship to Marx’s critique of capitalism’s internal contradictions.
- Examples/arguments: The binary framing of challenges in capitalist discourse, the failure to fulfill promises of existential purity and desire fulfillment.
