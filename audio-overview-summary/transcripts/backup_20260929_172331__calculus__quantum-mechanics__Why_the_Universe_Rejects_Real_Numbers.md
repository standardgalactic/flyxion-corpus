**Summary of Key Points**

1. **Matrix Multiplication vs. Kronecker Product**  
   - The standard matrix multiplication method is straightforward, while the Kronecker product offers a different way to represent how matrices combine. It’s essentially a mathematical convention rather than a fundamental necessity.

2. **Complex Numbers as “Amplitwists”**  
   - When multiplying a quantum state by a complex number, you simultaneously scale (amplitude) and rotate (twist) the state in a plane. This concept is introduced via Tristan Needham’s *amplitwist*, where scaling and twisting are distinct operations.
   - The matrix \(J\) (the missing real dimension or anti-symmetric matrix) represents a 90-degree rotation, analogous to the imaginary unit \(i\). Thus, complex numbers can be viewed as 2D real matrices “wearing a trench coat.”

3. **Tensor Product and Global Phase Ambiguity**  
   - The traditional tensor product forces one subsystem to carry the global phase (rotation), creating redundancy or an artificial rigidity in quantum mechanics.
   - By quotienting out this gauge redundancy—redistributing the phase across the entire tensor product—the ambiguity is resolved, suggesting that complex numbers might not be fundamentally necessary but are a convenient shorthand.

4. **Analogy with Mercator Projection**  
   - Just as a standard map of Earth (Mercator projection) distorts Greenland’s size due to its 2D representation, real quantum mechanics can appear distorted when trying to capture the full complexity of reality without complex numbers.
   - Complex numbers provide an efficient way to handle simultaneous rotation and scaling without introducing artificial “topological holes.”

5. **Real Quantum Mechanics (RQM) vs. Complex Quantum Mechanics**  
   - The deep dive leaves open whether RQM can fully describe joint states using only local measurements without additional rules, highlighting the unresolved nature of this question.
   - This exploration suggests that complex numbers might be a convenient bookkeeping device rather than an essential feature of the universe.

8. **Philosophical Implications**
