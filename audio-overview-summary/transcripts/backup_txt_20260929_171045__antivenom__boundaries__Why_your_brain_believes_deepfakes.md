**Summary**

The essay argues that humans do not inherently detect deepfakes because their brains are wired as coherence engines rather than truth detectors. Memory is reconstructive, lacking any biological metadata to indicate origin; instead, it relies on heuristic cues like perceptual richness and retrieval fluency. This leads the brain to accept synthetic media as real when it appears seamless and consistent (≈65% similarity) due to low entropy and minimal obstruction.

The paper introduces the CLIO architecture—a computational model for memory reconsolidation that assigns confidence values based solely on internal structural properties, not provenance. Synthetic media exploits this by providing high perceptual density (low entropy) and near consistency, bypassing the brain’s rejection mechanisms. The danger is amplified in individuals without established belief systems (e.g., children), as synthetic inputs can become foundational epistemic norms.

The only structural defense against deepfake deception is social correction via interaction with people who hold differing beliefs, which introduces friction and raises both obstruction and entropy, forcing a re-evaluation of reality. However, if multiple individuals are exposed to identical synthetic content, they converge into correlated drift, reinforcing the delusion rather than correcting it.
