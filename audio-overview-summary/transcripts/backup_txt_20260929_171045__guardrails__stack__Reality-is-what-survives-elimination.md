**Empirical Observation from Recursive Reasoning Systems**

Recursive reasoning systems—such as those used in artificial intelligence or complex adaptive systems—exhibit a fundamental behavior when they are correctly operating: there is a monotonic reduction in uncertainty. This means that the system’s confidence in its conclusions steadily increases, moving toward a globally consistent reality without any increase in doubt. In contrast, incorrect trajectories, which harbor hidden flaws (or “plot holes”), exhibit persistent oscillation in their uncertainty levels—spiking up and down as they encounter contradictions or workarounds.

**Application to the Modern Economy**

When we apply this principle to economic systems, we can see how local successes often mask underlying global tensions. For instance, attention-based advertising on social media platforms appears highly admissible locally (e.g., a user engaging with content for a short period), but globally it requires continuous recalibration due to user fatigue and the escalating effort needed to maintain engagement. This persistent oscillation in uncertainty signals high residual tension, indicating that such systems are not dynamically stable and will eventually collapse unless external life support is provided.

**Theoretical Framework: Constraint Closure Selection (Theorem 10.1)**

The paper formalizes this insight through **Theorem 10.1**, which defines reality as a configuration that remains consistent under all admissible extensions, across all scales, and minimizes residual tension. **Philosophical Implications**

This framework flips our conventional understanding of building versus eliminating. Instead of viewing reality as a cumulative addition of truths (constructive), it suggests that reality emerges from the process of removing what cannot coexist globally. This has profound implications for personal life: persistent oscillating uncertainty may indicate underlying obstructions that need to be pruned, allowing us to reach our stable fixed point.

**Conclusion**

By recognizing and addressing these hidden tensions—both in systems like social media engagement loops and in our own lives—we can better navigate towards true reality, which is defined not by local admissibility but by global coherence and minimal future work. This shift from additive construction to subtractive elimination offers a powerful lens for understanding both the physical world and personal challenges.
