**Mathematical Proof on Extinction Probability**

The core issue with asserting a 10% chance of extinction—whether it’s framed as “extinction” or any other catastrophic event—is rooted in the impossibility of empirically verifying such probabilities through frequency evidence. Here's why:

1. **Frequency Evidence Requirement**: To scientifically prove that an event, like AI causing human extinction, occurs with a specific probability (e.g., 10%), you must conduct repeated trials where each trial is independent and observable.

2. **Application to Extinction**: Imagine trying to prove that a weighted coin flips heads exactly 50% of the time by flipping it many times. You’d need thousands or millions of flips, counting every head precisely, to statistically validate the claim.

3. **AI Extinction Scenario**: Applying this logic to AI systems is fundamentally different because you cannot run “trials” on humanity itself. There’s only one Earth and one human civilization; thus, you can’t replicate an extinction event in a controlled environment like flipping coins.

4. **Poisson Distribution Application**:
   - The Poisson distribution models the probability of a given number of rare events occurring within a fixed interval (e.g., failures per hour).
   - For AI systems, if we claim an error rate is “one in a million hours,” this translates to a very low failure frequency.
   - To mathematically support such a claim with 95% confidence using the Poisson distribution, you’d need an astronomically long period of flawless operation—specifically, three million hours (≈340 years) without any critical error.

5. **Implications for AI Corporations**:
   - When corporations assert safety based on failure-free red team trials in simulated labs, they’re essentially saying: “We’ve run the model perfectly for six months and haven’t seen an extinction event.”
   - This is statistically insufficient because it doesn’t account for the vast timescale required to validate such a minuscule probability empirically.

6. **Conclusion**:
   - The inability to conduct repeated trials over human lifetimes makes any percentage-based claim about AI risk fundamentally unverifiable.
   - Instead of relying on probabilistic percentages, Flyxion proposes using tiered evidence standards that focus on structural and physical safeguards against catastrophic outcomes.

This mathematical framework underscores why reliance solely on statistical probabilities for existential risks like AI is inherently flawed. It pushes the conversation toward concrete, auditable safety measures—such as ensuring robust oversight gates are free from corporate pressure—that directly address the root cause of potential catastrophes.
