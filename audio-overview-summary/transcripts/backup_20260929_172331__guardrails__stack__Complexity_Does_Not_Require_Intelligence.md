**Synthesis of Key Ideas**

1. **Complexity as Affordance Landscape:**  
   Complexity isn’t an internal, abstract property residing in the mind; it’s a relational construct between an agent and its environment—its affordances. This shifts our focus from “what can be learned” to “what can be done now,” emphasizing real-time interaction.

2. **Constraints as Structural Elements:**  
   Constraints (the “no”) are not limitations but essential ingredients that shape behavior, create hierarchy, and enable complexity to emerge without being overwhelmed by detail. They define the boundaries within which an agent operates, allowing for stable, goal-directed actions.

3. **Relegation & Automation:**  
   By delegating lower-level tasks—like driving a car or braking during a skid—to automatic processes (relegation), we free cognitive resources and build scalable habit pyramids that prevent overload from details. This automation is crucial for managing high-dimensional complexity in everyday life.

4. **Goal/Intention as Intentional Stance:**  
   The intentional stance, inspired by Daniel Dennett, allows us to describe complex systems (like a heat-seeking missile or a Roomba) as if they have goals without requiring an underlying human-like consciousness. This is useful for prediction and understanding behavior without getting bogged down in metaphysical debates about “intent.”

5. **Indeterminacy & Plurality:**  
   Attempting to rank all forms of complexity (humans, spiders, AI systems, etc.) on a single scale is mathematically meaningless due to incomparability—different systems excel at different affordances and operate within distinct activity spaces. Complexity manifests in many ways; there’s no universal “peak” or hierarchy.

6. **Living with Fluency:**  
   Recognizing that much of daily life operates through fluency (autopilot) rather than conscious planning highlights the potential for greater capability when we let go of constant self‑directed thought. This encourages a shift from valuing abstract intelligence to appreciating embodied, skillful engagement.

**Homework Prompt:**  
Reflect on your own day and identify moments where you’re operating in a state of fluency (autopilot) versus conscious planning. Consider whether those fluent moments might be more complex or capable than the times when you’re actively “thinking.” This exercise can reveal how much agency is actually embodied rather than solely cognitive, prompting a deeper appreciation for mindless yet sophisticated forms of behavior.
