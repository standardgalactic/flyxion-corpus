### 1. Compression vs. Abstraction

**Compression**  
- Refers to reducing the *number* of events (or log length) needed to reach a given state.  
- In SpherePop OS, this means fewer lines of historical code are required for any outcome.  
- It’s akin to “statistical compression” where redundancy is removed.

**Abstraction**  
- Involves reducing the *number of distinct semantic representatives* (objects or concepts) needed in the system.  
- Achieved through explicit, irreversible merge events—these add a single additional event but shrink the ontology by one element.  
- Unlike compression, abstraction changes what the system “knows” about rather than how much data is stored.

**Key Insight:** The two processes are orthogonal; you can have a highly compressed log (low compression) yet still maintain a highly abstracted state with few semantic elements, or vice versa.

### 2. Structural Grounding & Semantic Energy

**Semantic Energy**  
- Defined as the degree of constraint imposed on future construction.  
- A high semantic energy environment has many fine distinctions that must be tracked and preserved, making future reasoning costly (cognitive load).  

**Abstraction’s Role:**  
- By merging semantically equivalent objects via collapse events, abstraction reduces ongoing constraints for future operations, stabilizing the system by lowering future cognitive costs.

### 3. Speculation & Hypothetical Exploration

**Speculative Branches:**  
- Local, isolated sequences of hypothetical events that allow users to explore alternate futures without affecting the main authoritative state.  
- If a branch proves successful, its final event is committed; if not, it’s discarded—ensuring no contamination of the core history.

**Implication:**  
- This provides an efficient way to perform hypothesis testing and planning within a deterministic replay framework, preserving semantic consistency (invariant under replay).

### 4. Anti-Didactic Interfaces & Semantic Impedance

**Semantic Impedance**  
- The resistance an explanatory system presents when being reduced into low-context executable form.  
- High impedance systems intentionally resist summary to force users to engage with the underlying causal structure.

**Retrocomputability:**  
- A property where only full engagement with the construction history yields understanding, exposing structural properties rather than superficial representations.

**Political & Design Implications:**  
- Legibility (simplified outputs) is often a tool for social control or managing expectations. Anti-didactic tools expose how visibility can be strategically managed and highlight that true understanding requires building one’s own internal abstraction structures.

**Core Insight:**
