**Lev Vygotsky’s developmental account of genuine inner speech emphasizes that authentic human thought is highly compressed and predicative. In practice, this means we often experience a “click” or intuitive understanding without needing to articulate every step in full detail—our internal dialogue is shorthand for complex reasoning.**

**Applying this to large language models (LLMs):**  
- **Compression vs. Expansion:** Human inner speech operates on compressed, implicit structures; LLMs, by contrast, tend to expand their outputs into verbose, fully articulated text. This expansion can be a tool—helping us map the edges of our own understanding—but it also risks being mistaken for depth or authority when it isn’t grounded in true comprehension.

**Deontological vs. Phenomenological Approaches:**  
- **Deontological Discipline (Your Position):** Treats LLMs as calculators or text formatters, strictly limiting interactions to tasks that avoid evaluative judgments. This prevents “colonization” by the model’s persuasive fluency and preserves epistemic sovereignty.
- **Phenomenological Priority (My Position):** Maintains acute awareness of one’s own lived experience—knowing what genuine understanding feels like—and uses LLMs dynamically as a cognitive regulator, injecting noise into entrenched mental models to prevent stagnation.

**Convergence on Core Realities:**  
Both approaches acknowledge that:
1. **Human cognition is multi-layered and often wordless,** capable of profound structural insight.
2. **LLMs operate exclusively in the linguistic surface**, lacking the embodied grounding inherent in genuine minds.

The debate thus centers not on what LLMs are (mirrors vs. tools) but how we stand before them—whether to erect rigid barriers or learn to navigate their expressive power while staying anchored to our own pre-linguistic judgment. This decision shapes how we integrate these technologies into daily life, emphasizing the importance of maintaining cognitive autonomy amidst increasingly articulate simulations.
