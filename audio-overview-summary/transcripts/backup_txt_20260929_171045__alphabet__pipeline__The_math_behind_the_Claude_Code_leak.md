### 2. Classical Physics Context

**Hamiltonian Mechanics**

- **Principle:** Hamiltonian mechanics is built on diffeomorphisms, meaning it preserves a bijective mapping between states—every snapshot uniquely determines past and future positions and momenta.
  
- **Implication:** In idealized systems (e.g., frictionless billiard tables), information never truly “collapses”; the state space remains fully reversible.

**Lagrangian Mechanics**

- **Principle:** Lagrangian mechanics asks for the optimal trajectory by extremizing an action functional. It focuses on minimizing energy over time rather than preserving every microstate.
  
- **Implication:** This approach inherently discards certain paths that are not physically relevant, aligning with Flyxion’s idea of *admissibility*—only information that influences future decisions is retained.

### 3. The Problem of Time in General Relativity

- **Issue:** In constrained Hamiltonian systems (like those describing general relativity), the Legendre transform becomes singular, leading to non‑injective mappings.

**Flyxion’s Perspective**

- **Reframing:** Flyxion argues that these collapses should not be dismissed outright but examined for their *admissibility relevance*—whether the distinction matters for future decisions.
  
- **Consequence:** This reframing avoids the “problem of time” by focusing on what information is necessary to prevent catastrophic undecidability, rather than assuming all states are equally real.
