# guardrails/stack/Why_AI_servers_fail_in_space

The key takeaway from this discussion is that cognitive “friction”—the struggle and frustration inherent in learning something new or difficult—is not waste, but rather a vital component of building resilient minds. When we try to eliminate all sources of difficulty through overly optimized AI systems that provide instant answers without the necessary struggle, we risk creating a population incapable of critical thinking and problem-solving. This mirrors the principle of xylomorphic stability in physical systems: just as they must use their own waste heat (in this case, cognitive effort) to build future substrates, humans need to endure frustration and error to develop patience, critical reasoning, and intellectual endurance. Thus, embracing rather than bypassing mental challenges is crucial for long-term cognitive resilience and societal progress.
