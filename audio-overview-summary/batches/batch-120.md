**Reflection on Daily Activities: Deliberate vs. Automatic Thinking**

1. **Conscious vs. Automatic Thought Distribution**
   - **Morning Routine:** In the morning, I often find myself engaging in deliberate, conscious thinking—planning my day’s tasks, setting intentions, and reviewing goals for the week. This period is marked by decision-making about what to prioritize and how to allocate time efficiently.
   - **Midday Work Sessions:** During work hours, especially when tackling complex problems or creative projects (e.g., writing code, designing layouts), I frequently operate in an automatic, fluent execution mode. Here, routine actions—like coding syntax, formatting documents, or navigating software tools—are performed without full awareness of the underlying processes.
   - **Afternoon Breaks:** During breaks, I tend to revert to more conscious thinking as I reflect on my work, evaluate progress, and plan future steps. This is a time for meta-cognition where I assess what worked well and what needs adjustment.

2. **Feeling Most Complex or Capable**
   - **Active Planning:** When actively planning—whether setting project milestones, strategizing solutions to intricate problems, or engaging in strategic thinking—I feel most complex and capable. These moments require integrating diverse information, anticipating future states, and making high-stakes decisions.
   - **Routine Tasks:** Conversely, performing routine tasks without full awareness (e.g., driving a car, using familiar software) feels like an elegant display of unconscious skill. I often notice that these actions are performed with minimal cognitive load yet yield reliable results, highlighting the sophistication of embodied agency.

**Key Takeaway**
This reflection underscores that intelligence and capability aren’t solely tied to conscious effort but also reside in the seamless execution of routine tasks through well-honed skills. Recognizing this balance can shift appreciation from abstract planning to valuing natural, embodied competence as equally sophisticated—a reminder that complexity isn’t always about thinking deeply; sometimes it’s about doing effectively without constant awareness.
