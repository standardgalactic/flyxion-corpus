**Why Intelligence Thrives on Extreme Abundance**

### 1. **The Principle of Excess Capacity**
- **Concept**: The brain operates in a regime where it can store vast amounts of detailed information (memorizing specific training images) while still generalizing broadly.
- **Implication**: This redundancy allows the brain to quickly adapt and generalize, essential for survival skills like fast decision-making under pressure.

### 2. **Metabolic Cost Justification**
- **Argument**: Critics claim that building a "gas-guzzling V8 engine" is inefficient biologically. However, the high metabolic cost of the brain is justified by its ability to rapidly adapt and generalize.
- **Survival Benefit**: The energy spent on this redundancy ensures quick decision-making in critical situations.

### 3. **Artificial Intelligence Insights**
#### a. **CNNs vs. LLMs**
- **Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs)**: Operate within an excess capacity regime, memorizing specific training images while maintaining generalization—mirroring the human visual system.
  
#### b. **Large Language Models (LLMs)**
- **Constrained Capacity**: Despite their enormous parameter count, LLMs only perfectly memorize about 0.2%–1.5% of their training data, discarding most specifics.
- **Hallucination Problem**: Inability to recall exact facts stems from this constraint; they compress information into generalized rules rather than detailed memories.

#### c. **Implications for Human Learning**
- **Decluttering vs. Embracing Complexity**: Modern society often promotes minimalism (curated feeds, standardized education). However, if Dubova and Sloman’s framework is correct, we may be starving ourselves of complexity necessary for deep learning.
- **Value of Noise**: Our brains evolved to thrive in an excess capacity regime, processing noisy data efficiently. Removing this noise might hinder robust generalizations and insights.

### 4. **Conclusion**
Embracing a little mess—allowing complex details into our daily lives—could be crucial for optimal learning and intelligence. Recognizing the value of excess capacity encourages us to reconsider how we design environments, curate information, and approach education, potentially leading to deeper understanding and creativity in both humans and artificial systems alike.
