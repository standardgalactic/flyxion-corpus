### **1. Why Objects Are Just Stable Boundaries**

**Core Idea:**  
- Distinctions (boundaries) create information by separating one thing from another, but this compression hides underlying complexity.
- Maintaining these distinctions requires energy and effort (repair), which generates entropy—loss of information about past states.

**Key Points:**
1. **Distinction & Information:** Drawing boundaries creates separable entities, thus generating information.
2. **Entropy as a Consequence:** Repairing damage consumes energy, leading to irreversible loss of historical detail (entropy).
3. **Repair vs. Regeneration:**  
   - *Repair* fixes immediate issues (e.g., fixing a leak).  
   - *Regeneration* builds capacity for future repairs (e.g., strengthening hulls), preserving optionality.
4. **Reachability & Future Possibilities:** The volume of reachable futures shrinks as entropy grows; the admissibility manifold visualizes this loss of options.
5. **Generative Admissibility Principle (GAP):** A system’s value lies in expanding its future distinction production, not merely surviving.

**Takeaway:** Reality is dynamic and fractal; solidity is an illusion sustained by energetic effort to suppress complexity.

### **2. The Universal Law of Admissibility and Repair**

**Core Idea:**  
- Systems survive disruptions through active repair mechanisms rather than static perfection.
- Disruptions (unconformities) are natural breaks that allow new processes to emerge, reshaping the system’s trajectory.

**Key Points:**
1. **Geological Perspective – Unconformities:**  
   - James Hutton observed angular unconformities where older rock layers were overlain by younger sediments after uplift and erosion.
   - The Grand Canyon’s Cambrian–Proterozoic gap represents a billion‑year interruption, symbolizing geological repair events.
2. **Philosophical Implications – Dynamic Resilience:**  
   - Embracing resilience over static perfection challenges traditional views of stability as ideal.
3. **Personal Identity – Trajectory Rather Than Snapshot:**  
   - Viewing identity as an admissibility regime allows us to see life changes (memory loss, narrative shifts) as integral growth processes.

**Takeaway:** Disruptions are opportunities for adaptation and forward movement, fostering both personal and systemic resilience.

### **3. Continuation Across Gaps – A Universal Principle**

**Core Idea:**  
- Continuation involves persisting or evolving despite disruptions, focusing on adapting to new conditions rather than returning to an exact previous state.

**Key Points:**
1. **Human Systems – Sleep & Repair:**  
   - *Non‑REM sleep* cleanses the brain of irrelevant information; *REM sleep* integrates selected memories emotionally.
2. **Geology – Unconformities as Legible Gaps:**  
   - Massive tectonic events create gaps in geological records (e.g., Sicker Point, Scotland), symbolizing intentional breaks where new layers form later.
3. **Psychological Resilience – Trauma & Grief:**  
   - Traditional view seeks restoration to a perfect past; the alternative perspective embraces adaptation and growth through change.

**Takeaway:** Continuation is achieved by navigating disruptions with resilience, celebrating new pathways forward rather than yearning for an unbroken past.
