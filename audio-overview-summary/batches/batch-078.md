# Batch 78

**Summary**

The audio overview discusses an essay that advocates for a radical rethinking of artificial intelligence (AI) reasoning processes. The core argument is against the current “chain of thought” methods used in AI models, which are often viewed as superficial narratives rather than genuine causal reasoning. Instead, the essay proposes a “chain of memory,” where AI would perform its reasoning within an internal latent memory space, allowing for traceable causal steps.

The essay introduces RSVP and Tartan—a fictional framework that posits reality consists not only of matter and energy but also of a vast information field akin to a universal weather map. This model includes four key rules:

1. **Space is composed of recursive tiles.**
2. **Objects emit Gaussian auras broadcasting their properties.**
3. **Causality can be visualized through pixel stretching, leaving visible traces like comet tails.**
4. **There exists annotated noise containing hidden information.**

The primary thesis is to develop an AI capable of true causal reasoning by simulating a universe where such reasoning is as fundamental as gravity or thermodynamics. However, the essay acknowledges several significant criticisms:

1. **Lack of empirical connection:** The theory cannot be measured or tested against observable reality.
2. **Risk of paradoxes:** Without clear causality rules, the system could become unstable due to self-referential loops.
3. **Ethical integration issues:** Attempting to embed morality as a mathematical field is deemed meaningless without grounding moral value.

In response to these critiques, the author proposes Hydra—a unified architecture that integrates four distinct paradigms (industrial efficiency, cognitive realism, causal traceability via chain of memory, and refined RSVP/Tartan physics)—to create a more robust and practical AI system.

**Keywords**

AI reasoning, chain of thought, chain of memory, information field, RSVP Tartan framework, causal reasoning, cosmic cathedral critique, ethical integration, Hydra architecture.
