**Summary of Key Points from “Why Flat Roofs Are Mathematically Broken”**

1. **Thermal Runaway**:  
   - A mid-sized city with 10 million square meters of flat roofs generates approximately 2 billion watts of heat, comparable to multiple industrial power plants running continuously.  
   - This excess heat intensifies the demand for air conditioning, which in turn consumes more energy and produces additional heat, creating a positive feedback loop that exacerbates urban temperatures.

2. **Hydrological Aggregation**:  
   - During storms, flat roofs cause massive amounts of water runoff, overwhelming existing drainage systems designed for lower volumes.  
   - The problem is modeled as an exponential decay issue: increased paved surfaces (like flat roofs) lead to greater stormwater demand while the capacity to handle it remains unchanged, leading to inevitable urban flooding.

3. **Ecological Collapse**:  
   - Continuous impermeable surfaces reduce ecological connectivity, causing dead zones where pollinators and other species cannot survive.  
   - This results in genetic bottlenecks and ecosystem collapse, described using an exponential decay model that shows how small increases in paved areas can lead to abrupt, irreversible changes.

4. **Ecological Floor**:  
   - The concept of an “ecological floor” is introduced, analogous to gravity in structural engineering—just as skyscrapers cannot be built with non-load-bearing materials like wet cardboard, cities must adhere to ecological limits; otherwise, they face inevitable failure.  
   - Mathematically, the feasible set for maintaining inert roofs shrinks to zero under persistent urban and environmental conditions.

5. **Subsidies and Future Implications**:  
   - Currently, the costs of these failures are subsidized by cheap fossil fuels and massive infrastructure projects (like sewer systems).  
   - However, as energy prices rise and urban density increases, this subsidy may disappear. This could make owning an inert roof uninsurable due to its quantifiable negative impact on city infrastructure and climate.

**Conclusion**: The analysis demonstrates that conventional flat roofs are not merely inefficient but mathematically unstable structures that export their failures (heat, water, ecological degradation) into the broader urban environment, potentially leading to financial or insurability issues in the future.
