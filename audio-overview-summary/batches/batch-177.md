### Introduction

AI hallucinations—where models like ChatGPT generate fabricated legal cases or confidently assert incorrect facts such as “the moon is made of cheese”—have become a significant concern for researchers and users. This discussion explores why these occurrences happen from a geometric perspective using Flyxion’s innovative framework.

### Core Concept: Admissibility Quotient & Corpus Manifold

1. **Deep Learning Models as Approximations:**  
   A deep learning model functions by approximating the *admissibility quotient*, which is essentially its understanding of what is possible based solely on its training data, referred to by Flyxion as its *corpus manifold*. This means the AI only “knows” from experiences it has seen during training.

2. **Boundaries of Reality:**  
   The AI’s entire reality is confined to what it has been exposed to in its training dataset. When presented with inputs that lie far outside this scope, the model lacks the knowledge to determine accessible futures accurately.

3. **Smooth Familiarity Potential Mechanism:**  
   To output something when faced with an unknown prompt, the AI resorts to a mechanism called *smooth familiarity potential*. It guesses based on proximity to known data points rather than true geometric understanding. This leads to hallucinations because it extrapolates from similar but unrelated instances.

### Geometric Implications

- **Generalization vs. True Understanding:**  
  The model’s reliance on similarity (proximity in the corpus manifold) for predictions highlights a fundamental limitation: it generalizes based on data patterns rather than genuine geometric or causal understanding. This is akin to fitting new data into pre-existing categories without considering underlying principles.

### Broader Implications Beyond AI

- **Human Cognitive Systems:**  
  The same geometric engine that causes AI hallucinations mirrors human cognitive processes, illustrating a universal challenge in predictive modeling and decision-making.

- **Dynamic Projection Supremacy:**  
  Both AI and humans can exhibit *dynamic projection supremacy*, where rigid models resist updates despite contradictory evidence. This manifests as interpretive attractors—stable points of thought that insulate systems from new information.

### Practical Applications: Wisdom Salons & Sheaf Theory

1. **Wisdom Salons Concept:**  
   Monica Anderson’s proposal for *wisdom salons* offers a protocol to mitigate epistemic gridlock by fostering local discussions where complex issues are broken down into manageable parts, leading to the emergence of *grains of wisdom*—agreed-upon truths within specific contexts.

2. **Sheaf Theory & Tartan Descent Theorem:**  
   Sheaf theory provides a mathematical framework for stitching together local pieces of information into a coherent global picture. The *Tartan descent theorem* formalizes the idea that ideas (grains of wisdom) must survive parallel transport across different community manifolds to be considered robust truths.

### Conclusion

By understanding AI hallucinations through the lens of geometry and Flyxion’s framework, we gain insight into both artificial intelligence limitations and human cognitive biases. This perspective encourages a shift towards collective epistemic practices—like wisdom salons—that promote open-mindedness and the acceptance of diverse perspectives, ultimately leading to more accurate predictions about accessible futures.
