3. **Repression as Survival Mechanism**: For human babies, basic animal instincts are insufficient for navigating complex social environments due to premature birth; repression becomes necessary—a non-declarative action plan stored in the basal ganglia—allowing children to cope with overwhelming prediction errors without fully understanding them.

6. **Therapeutic Implications**: Suggests therapy should provoke new prediction errors safely, allowing the brain to rewire itself in a therapeutic environment, acknowledging that old strategies are often ineffective in adulthood.

7. **Philosophical Shift**: Challenges traditional psychoanalytic views by proposing consciousness resides in affective processes (brainstem) rather than solely in the neocortex where unconscious predictions reside, suggesting a healthy mind tolerates and creatively engages with its contradictions.

**Conclusion**

This exploration underscores that our struggles are adaptive responses to an inherently unpredictable world, driven by limited tools developed early in life. Embracing uncertainty might be essential for a more vibrant, neurologically alive existence.
