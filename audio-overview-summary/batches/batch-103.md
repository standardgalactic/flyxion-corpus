**Audio Overview: ARDO (Accountable Recoverable Degradation) Framework**

The ARDO framework provides a rigorous mathematical approach to ensure systems can gracefully degrade and recover from overload without losing critical information. It consists of four interlocking factors:

1. **DO – Delegation Operator:** Ensures there is an effective mechanism for transitioning the system into an obligation-preserving state during degradation.
2. **FO – Fixed Obligation Model:** Declares a core invariant before any operation begins, preventing “self-sealing” where priorities are redefined after failure.
3. **HO – Historical Sufficiency:** Guarantees that every task retains sufficient representation to guarantee its future continuation, preserving critical state information.
4. **WO – Witness Object:** Ensures each excluded or aborted task receives an externally visible disposition witness, preventing silent forgetting.

The interlocking nature of these factors means any failure in one area leads to a complete breakdown of the degradation process. For example:

- If only three factors are present but WO is missing, even if the operator works perfectly and history is saved, there’s no way to prove what was discarded or why, leading to potential loss of critical information.
  
- Conversely, having all four factors without a flawed operator might still result in failure if the system cannot adapt when external conditions change.

The framework also addresses dynamic environments where obligations can shift mid-operation. It suggests maintaining a **look-back margin**, a buffer of raw data retained even when it seems useless at the time, sacrificing present efficiency for future safety against unforeseen changes in requirements or rules—mirroring the analogy of packing an extra winter coat for a beach trip despite knowing you’ll likely not need it.
